Some of what your body does has nothing to do with what your body did. Mood
sinks through a dark winter; blood pressure and pulse drift up in a heatwave.
When a pattern in your own data has no obvious cause, the cause is sometimes
outside the window.

The environmental module gives those patterns somewhere to point. It is **on by
default since v1.29.1, but inert**: nothing about the weather is recorded, and
no outbound fetch happens, until you set a home location on the Environment
settings surface.

## What it adds

Once a home location is set, the module folds in a small set of environmental
measures as
**influence signals** — context correlated with your mood, sleep, and vitals
rather than health readings in their own right:

- weather conditions and **temperature**,
- **daylight hours** and sunshine,
- **precipitation**,
- **barometric pressure**,
- since v1.42.0, the outdoor air: **fine particles** (PM2.5 and PM10),
  **ozone**, nitrogen dioxide, the European and US air-quality indices,
  **UV**, Saharan dust and six kinds of **pollen**.

Three of the air values join the correlations: PM2.5, the day's highest
8-hour ozone and the day's highest pollen count. UV, nitrogen dioxide and dust
are shown and readable by the Coach, without being correlated.

These join the [correlation engine](/explanation/health-scores/) through the
signal registry, so the influence relationships
surface the same way any other driver does — winter darkness against mood, heat
against blood pressure and pulse — with the usual descriptive, never-causal
framing. For the physiology behind those links, see
[environment and your health](/knowledge/environment/).

## Correlations look past the season

Temperature and blood pressure both follow the seasons, and so do daylight and
sleep. Two such series line up strongly without anything connecting them from
one day to the next. Since v1.42.0 every daily correlation first removes a
linear trend and the yearly cycle from both series (only the trend when less
than 120 days are paired) and then compares what is left. The confidence takes
into account that neighbouring days resemble each other, so a pattern needs
more evidence than a simple day count would suggest. A weather or air channel
is compared as the mean of the day before and the day itself against the same
day's outcome, which pairs a warm night with the sleep it shaped.

Some patterns you saw before the update may no longer appear. Those were
mostly the season talking.

## What the values are, and what they are not

- The weather settles over a few days, so the newest stored day is usually
  yesterday or the day before. Nothing here is a forecast.
- Air quality is modelled hourly on about 11 km in Europe and about 45 km
  elsewhere. Pollen is only available in Europe. The feed reaches back to 2013;
  pollen, UV, dust and aerosol values only to about mid 2022.
- A value the feed did not cover is stored as empty, never as zero. Means and
  maxima need at least 18 of a day's 24 hours, ozone is the highest 8-hour
  running mean, and UV needs all of the midday hours.
- These are modelled outdoor conditions for an area, not anyone's personal
  exposure. No surface says the weather caused anything.

The values carry their attribution wherever they appear: Open-Meteo data
under CC BY 4.0, and "Contains modified Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring
Service information" for the air values.

On the dashboard a quiet note appears on a day with high pollen, a warm night
or very poor air. It shows the newest stored day, never a forecast.

## Switches

- **Per account:** Settings, Location & weather, **Air quality and pollen**.
  On by default. Off stops the air-quality requests for that account and keeps
  the air values out of every surface; days already stored keep theirs.
- **For the whole instance:** an operator sets
  `ENVIRONMENT_AIR_QUALITY_DISABLED=1`. The weather keeps working, and the
  settings page says the operator turned air quality off.
- The module as a whole is a normal switch under Settings, Modules.

An operator who wants no outside requests at all can run Open-Meteo themselves
and point `OPENMETEO_BASE_URL`, `OPENMETEO_AIR_QUALITY_URL` and
`OPENMETEO_GEOCODING_URL` at it. A request carries the rounded coordinates, a
date range and a time zone, and nothing about the person.

The instance keeps its own count of requests to Open-Meteo, weather and air
quality together, and stays below 500 a minute, 4,000 an hour and 8,000 a day.
The budget is built in and applies to a self-hosted Open-Meteo as well. A request over that budget
is not sent; the nightly run stops early and continues the next night. One
account cannot use up the day for everyone: it gets at most a twentieth of the
daily budget, at most three backfills a rolling hour (`429`
`environment.backfill_rate_limited`) and one queued backfill per twenty
minutes (`409` `environment.backfill_pending`). After the update the nightly
run fills in air quality for up to 90 stored days per account and night,
newest first, so two years of history catch up within about nine nights.

## A conservative location posture

Location is the sensitive part of weather, so the module treats it conservatively
by design. **You** set a home location; the app does not derive one. There is no
silent tracking, no coarse-location guess from your network, no continuous
position feed. The override is something you reach for deliberately, not a sensor
running in the background.

Whatever place you set is stored coarse. Since v1.39.4 the home location, the
places of travel periods and the location saved with each day's weather are
rounded to one decimal of a degree, about 11 km north to south, the size of a
town or a city district. Before, they were rounded to about a kilometre, which
is closer to a street; the upgrade rounds what is already stored. The weather
data comes in grid cells of 9 to 25 km, so the coarser location changes the
weather attached to your days very little.

Since v1.42.0 the home location, the place of each travel period and the
location saved with each day are also stored encrypted with the instance's
key. After the update a background pass seals the rows older releases wrote
and clears their readable copies. Key rotation covers all three. What you see
in settings does not change: the server opens the location to show it to you.

Later stages — coarse location from the iOS client, or a bedroom-temperature feed
from a home-automation setup — are separate, opt-in follow-ups, not part of this
first version.

## What the Coach and MCP clients can see

Since v1.42.0 the Coach and a connected [MCP client](/reference/mcp/) can read
your stored days through one tool, `get_environment`: per day the temperature,
rain, sunshine, pressure and humidity, and while air quality is on, the air
values, plus a short summary such as hot nights or high pollen days. It
carries no coordinates and no place name, only how many days were spent away
from home, and every result lists the attribution lines. With the module off
it answers that the module is off.

The general metric reads still do not hand out the environmental series, and
the correlation engine both use includes the environmental channels, so a link
between pressure and sleep can appear as a named driver in a correlation the
Coach describes. See the [privacy architecture](/explanation/privacy-architecture/).
