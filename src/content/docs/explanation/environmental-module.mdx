Some of what your body does has nothing to do with what your body did. Mood
sinks through a dark winter; blood pressure and pulse drift up in a heatwave.
When a pattern in your own data has no obvious cause, the cause is sometimes
outside the window.

The environmental module gives those patterns somewhere to point. It is **on by
default since v1.29.1, but inert**: nothing about the weather is recorded, and
no outbound fetch happens, until you set a home location on the Environment
settings surface.

## What it adds

Once a home location is set, the module folds in a small set of environmental
measures as
**influence signals** — context correlated with your mood, sleep, and vitals
rather than health readings in their own right:

- weather conditions and **temperature**,
- **daylight hours** and sunshine,
- **precipitation**,
- **barometric pressure**.

These join the [correlation engine](/explanation/health-scores/) through the
signal registry, so the influence relationships
surface the same way any other driver does — winter darkness against mood, heat
against blood pressure and pulse — with the usual descriptive, never-causal
framing. For the physiology behind those links, see
[environment and your health](/knowledge/environment/).

## How v1 sources the data

The first version keeps the moving parts deliberately small:

## A conservative location posture

Location is the sensitive part of weather, so the module treats it conservatively
by design. **You** set a home location; the app does not derive one. There is no
silent tracking, no coarse-location guess from your network, no continuous
position feed. The override is something you reach for deliberately, not a sensor
running in the background.

Later stages — coarse location from the iOS client, or a bedroom-temperature feed
from a home-automation setup — are separate, opt-in follow-ups, not part of this
first version.

## What the AI surfaces can and cannot see

The raw environmental series (`ENV_*` — temperature, sunshine, daylight,
precipitation, barometric pressure) stay off the AI metric-read allowlist: no
Coach or MCP tool can fetch them as a series. The correlation engine both
surfaces query does include the environmental channels, though, so an
environment-vs-outcome relationship — barometric pressure against sleep, say —
can appear as a named driver in an AI-narrated correlation. The raw exclusion
matches the deliberate exclusions described in the
[privacy architecture](/explanation/privacy-architecture/): being defined is not
the same as being streamed to a model.
