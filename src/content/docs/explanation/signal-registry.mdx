A health app accretes metrics. Each new one needs a typed measurement, a detail
page, a place in the correlation engine, a line in the Coach's snapshot, an API
entry, and a FHIR mapping. Wire those by hand, one surface at a time, and a
signal eventually ends up half-connected — visible on a chart but missing from
the export, or named in the API but absent from the correlation pairs.

The signal registry exists to make that drift impossible. A signal is **declared
once**, and that single definition wires it across every surface together.

## One definition, six surfaces

A registry entry carries the signal's identity — its key, unit, and population
reference band — and from it the signal is wired into:

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="Typed measurement / biomarker" icon="document">
    The signal becomes a first-class measurement or biomarker type with its unit
    and validation, so a reading can be stored and retrieved like any other.
  </Card>
  <Card title="The detail page" icon="bars">
    It renders through the shared detail template (the same one resting pulse and
    the biomarker pages use): heading, stats, chart, target range, value table.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Correlation, baseline, changepoint" icon="random">
    It joins the engine that finds drivers, computes your usual range, and flags
    level shifts — no separate hook-up per signal.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Narrative + Coach snapshot" icon="comment">
    It appears in the briefing and the Coach's grounded snapshot, described in
    human terms rather than as a raw enum name.
  </Card>
  <Card title="API / OpenAPI" icon="seti:json">
    Its Zod schema and OpenAPI entry are generated from the same definition, so
    the contract native clients read never lags the app.
  </Card>
  <Card title="FHIR mapping" icon="seti:default">
    It maps into the read-only FHIR export with its coding, so a clinician's
    system sees it too. See the <a href="/reference/fhir-api/">FHIR API</a>.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

## Why this matters

The guarantee the registry buys is a simple one: **a signal can never be
advertised but missing.** Because the API entry, the chart, the correlation
pairs, and the FHIR map all descend from one declaration, there is no state where
the contract promises a metric the record cannot return, or the chart shows a
trend the export silently drops. One source of truth, end to end.

Each signal also carries its **unit and population reference band** in the
registry itself, so the band a chart draws, the range a status card compares
against, and the bounds the export states are the same numbers in every place.

## How the new signals were added

The v1.25 clinical signals were each added the same way — declared in the
registry, wired everywhere at once:

- **Grip strength** — declared with its unit and age/sex norms; the
  [grip-strength detail page](/knowledge/grip-strength/), its trend, and its
  correlation pairs all came from that one entry.
- **Pain (0–10 NRS)** — declared as a numeric rating scale; the
  [pain](/knowledge/pain/) trend and its optional per-region tracking followed.
- **Waist circumference** — declared with its clinical bands; the
  [waist-circumference](/knowledge/waist-circumference/) page and the derived
  waist-to-height ratio came together.

Each one proves the backbone end to end: log a reading, and it appears on the
detail page, in the correlations, in the snapshot, in the API, and in the FHIR
map without a separate change per surface. See
[Log clinical signals](/guides/clinical-signals/) for the user-facing side.

## Declared, but deliberately off the AI surface

Being in the registry is not the same as being exposed to the Coach or MCP. Some
registry-defined signals are intentionally kept off the AI/MCP surface:

- the **PHQ-9 / GAD-7** mental-health screeners (item content and totals),
- the **environmental-context** signals (see
  [the environmental module](/explanation/environmental-module/)),
- and the **structured records** (allergies, family history).

They are registry signals — typed, charted, exportable — but they do not resolve
through any read tool, because exposing them is a decision the registry leaves to
the [non-diagnostic standard](/explanation/non-diagnostic-standard/) and the
[privacy architecture](/explanation/privacy-architecture/), not an automatic
consequence of being defined.
