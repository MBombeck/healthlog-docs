HealthLog charts blood pressure against reference ranges so a reading reads in
context rather than as a bare number. The defaults follow established clinical
guidelines, and you can override them with the targets your own clinician set.

:::caution[Not medical advice]
The classifications below are reproduced from published guidelines for context
only. They are not a diagnosis and not advice. Blood pressure should be measured
and interpreted by a clinician across multiple visits; a single home reading does
not classify you.
:::

## The default: ESH 2023

HealthLog's default office-BP bands follow the
[2023 ESH Guidelines for the management of arterial hypertension](https://journals.lww.com/jhypertension/fulltext/2023/12000/2023_esh_guidelines_for_the_management_of_arterial.2.aspx)
(European Society of Hypertension). In that framework, hypertension is diagnosed
at an office systolic BP ≥ 140 mmHg or diastolic ≥ 90 mmHg, graded:

| Category | Systolic (mmHg) | | Diastolic (mmHg) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Optimal / normal / high-normal | < 140 | and/or | < 90 |
| Grade 1 hypertension | 140–159 | and/or | 90–99 |
| Grade 2 hypertension | 160–179 | and/or | 100–109 |
| Grade 3 hypertension | ≥ 180 | and/or | ≥ 110 |

(ESH also stages patients by risk factors and organ damage in addition to these
BP-based grades.)

## Why guidelines differ

The US [2017 ACC/AHA guideline](https://www.acc.org/Latest-in-Cardiology/Articles/2024/02/05/11/43/2023-ESH-Hypertension-Guideline-Update)
draws the line lower, labelling 130–139/80–89 mmHg as "Stage 1 hypertension"
where ESH calls a similar range "high-normal." Neither is "wrong" — they reflect
different thresholds for when a label changes management. HealthLog defaults to
the ESH 2023 thresholds and lets a clinician override them, rather than picking a
side for you. A
[comparison of the two](https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jacadv.2024.101230)
is worth reading if you want the detail.

## Clinician-set targets override the default

The default bands are a starting point. Under custom thresholds you can replace
the computed range with the target your clinician actually set for you — for
instance a tighter target after a cardiac event, or a looser one where strict
control is not the goal. Overrides are audit-logged, and the doctor-report PDF
prints the range actually in force, so an override you set is the one the
report reflects.

## How the bands feed the scores

The blood-pressure share of the [Health Score](/explanation/composite-health-score/) grades
by *how close* a reading sits to its target band, with recent readings weighted
more — so being consistently a little high reads as borderline, not a crisis,
while genuinely high pressure still scores low. A reading slightly over target
does not collapse the score the way an all-or-nothing rule would.

## Other metrics, same principle

The same "published default, clinician override" model applies to other metrics:
blood-glucose bands follow established diabetes-association guidance (fasting /
postprandial / random / bedtime, with mg/dL ↔ mmol/L conversion), and SpO₂ uses
consensus pulse-oximetry guidance. Each links its source in the app, and each is
overridable with your clinician's target.
