The Health Score is the single number on the dashboard's Today ring and on the
Insights card. It is a 0 to 100 average of up to seven pillars, each graded
against a published reference band, and below full breadth it carries a line
saying how many areas of health it rests on. This page explains what goes in,
how the number is formed, what the tiers mean, and what the card tells you
when the set behind the number changes.

The [derived wellness metrics](/explanation/health-scores/) (readiness,
recovery, the sleep score, and the rest) are a separate tier with their own
page. They compare you against your own baseline. The Health Score compares
each pillar against a band somebody published.

The Health Score is a descriptive summary of your own record, not a clinical
verdict. Which pillars count is yours to choose, so the combined number is not
itself a clinical standard. See the
[non-diagnostic standard](/explanation/non-diagnostic-standard/).

## Seven pillars, five areas of health

| Pillar | Area of health | What is graded | Reference band | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Blood pressure | Cardiometabolic | A representative reading from the last 90 days, from at least 12 paired readings | 120–129 / 70–79 mmHg under 65; 130–139 / 70–79 mmHg from 65 | ESH 2023 |
| Glycaemia | Cardiometabolic | The latest HbA1c from the last 180 days; without one, the median fasting glucose over the last 90 days, from at least 8 readings | HbA1c 5–5.6 %; fasting glucose 70–99 mg/dL | ADA 2025; Selvin 2010 |
| Lipids | Cardiometabolic | The latest complete lipid panel from the last 365 days, marker by marker | The reporting laboratory's own printed reference ranges | The laboratory that issued the panel |
| Activity | Activity | Mean steps per day over the last 28 days, from at least 21 days | Benefit plateaus at 10,000 steps a day under 60 and at 8,000 from 60 | Paluch 2022 |
| Sleep | Sleep | Minutes asleep per night over the last 28 nights, from at least 14; regularity joins in from 21 nights | At least the age-band need (7 hours for an adult); long sleep is not penalised | NSF 2015; Ai 2021; Daghlas 2019; Windred 2024 |
| Adiposity | Adiposity | Waist-to-height ratio from the latest waist measurement in the last 180 days | Below 0.5 | NICE 2022 |
| Wellbeing | Wellbeing | The latest completed screener from the last 90 days | PHQ-9 0–4; GAD-7 0–4; WHO-5 52–100 | Kroenke 2001; Spitzer 2006; WHO 1998 |

Three of the seven pillars (blood pressure, glycaemia, lipids) share the
cardiometabolic area. That matters for the breadth rule below: blood pressure,
glucose and cholesterol together are three pillars but one area of health.

Two pillars, activity and wellbeing, do not rest on a physiological
measurement. That fact travels with the score as a label. It does not change
how the score is computed and it no longer decides whether a score exists.

Daily mood does not feed the wellbeing pillar; only the validated screeners
do. A PHQ-9 whose ninth item is answered above zero is left unscored for that
window, and the app's crisis signposting takes over instead.

## Published bands, not invented targets

Every scored band is one of three kinds: a clinical threshold, a population
band, or a guideline band. There is deliberately no fourth kind. No band is
derived from a profile field you did not set. Where a pillar reads your age or
height, it does so only to pick a row in a published table (the ESH 2023 age
bands for blood pressure, the two step plateaus Paluch 2022 publishes, the NSF
2015 sleep-need table) or to form the observation itself (height divides the
waist measurement into a ratio; the 0.5 threshold is the same for everybody).
Blood pressure waits for a date of birth rather than guessing an age band.

Your own yardstick is not excluded from the app, only from the scored band. A
blood-pressure target your clinician set is shown beside the ESH band and
labelled as a personal target; it never replaces the band the pillar is graded
against. The weight goal stays unscored context next to the score.

Earlier versions graded weight against a target computed from height (22 times
height squared, widened by 2 kg either side). That band was removed in v1.34,
and since v1.38 a structural test holds every pillar to the rule above.

## How the number is formed

- Each pillar with enough recent data produces a 0 to 100 score. A pillar
  without enough data is left out, not counted as zero.
- The Health Score is the rounded, equal-weighted mean of the pillars that
  count.
- Green starts at 70, yellow at 50, red below that. The weakest pillar can pull
  the overall band down: if one pillar sits in a lower band than the mean, the
  score keeps its number but takes that pillar's band, and the card names which
  pillar set it.
- A weekly comparison appears only when both windows were computed over the
  same pillar set, under the same method and the same pillar selection, and
  only when the move clears the combined measurement noise of the pillars
  involved. Slow markers such as a lipid panel contribute to the level but
  never to the weekly comparison. When there is no comparison, the card says
  why.

## Breadth: full, partial, minimal

Three areas of health is the breadth the method recommends, not a requirement.
The score is computed the same way at every breadth; what changes is the
label.

| Tier | Distinct areas counted |
| --- | --- |
| `full` | Three or more |
| `partial` | Two |
| `minimal` | One |

An account scoring on activity alone gets a `minimal` score that says on its
face what it rests on. The only refusal left is an account with no readable
pillar at all: a mean over nothing is an absent score, not a low one. The card
then reads "Nothing has enough recent data to score yet. One area of health is
enough to start, and the score will say which areas it rests on."

Because the tier counts areas rather than pillars, the server resolves it and
sends it with every score as a `scoreBasis` block (`domains`, `recommended`,
`tier`, `physiological`). A client counting pillars would get it wrong
whenever two cardiometabolic pillars are both in play.

The pillar selection under Settings follows the same rule: a selection with at
least one pillar saves, and the page still recommends three.

## What the card shows

- **The basis line.** Below full breadth the Insights card and the Today ring
  carry one line, "Based on 2 of 3 areas of health." The number stays at full
  size in its band colour at every tier. At full breadth the line is absent.
- **The composition notice.** When a pillar joins or leaves the set behind the
  number since the last stored day (a window rolled past its floor, a source
  went quiet, a new area got its first readings), the card says which area left
  or joined and, for a departure, the same reason the pillar shows on its own
  row. It can be dismissed and comes back only if the set moves again. Two
  changes do not raise it, because they carry a notice of their own: the method
  changed, or you changed which pillars count.
- **The composition list.** The pillars behind the number are one tap away,
  each with its observed value, its reference band, and the source of that
  band.

## What changed in v1.38

Until v1.38 the score existed only when the pillars behind it spanned at least
three distinct areas of health and at least one of them was a physiological
measurement; anything narrower showed "not enough data". Since v1.38 that
floor is a recommendation: one or two areas produce a score by the same
arithmetic, labelled by the basis line, and only an account with nothing
readable is refused. The method version moved from 3 to 4; days stored under
the old version keep it, and the weekly comparison is suppressed across the
change. Older notes that describe the three-area floor describe the earlier
rule.

:::caution[Not medical advice]
The Health Score summarises your own record against published bands. It is
not a diagnosis, not a risk score, and not a substitute for a clinician's
reading of the same numbers. Discuss your values and targets with a qualified
clinician.
:::
