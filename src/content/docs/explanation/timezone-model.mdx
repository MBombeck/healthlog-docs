Health data is full of "what day did this happen on" questions, and those answers
depend on a timezone. HealthLog separates **storage** (always UTC) from
**interpretation** (your timezone), so the same instant is unambiguous in the
database and correct on your screen.

## Store UTC, render local

Every timestamp — `measuredAt`, an intake's `takenAt`, a session expiry — is
stored as a UTC instant. UTC has no daylight-saving discontinuities and no
ambiguity, so it is the only safe storage representation. The display layer
converts to your configured timezone at render time via the formatter layer
(`useFormatters()`), which also handles locale-aware numbers, dates, and units.

A **time-format preference** sits on top of this: automatic (follows the
language), 24-hour, or 12-hour, set once in the profile and applied to every clock
the app renders. It changes how a time is *shown*, never how it is *stored*.

## Day boundaries are where it gets subtle

A UTC instant is unambiguous, but "which day" it belongs to is not — it depends on
the observer's timezone. Two places care deeply:

### Cumulative daily aggregates

When an Apple Health import collapses steps into one row per day
(`stats:<HKType>:<YYYY-MM-DD>`), the day boundary respects your timezone
preference. A step taken at 00:30 local time belongs to the new local day, not to
the previous UTC day. The import worker defaults to `Europe/Berlin` when no
preference is set. Get this wrong and a midnight workout lands on the wrong day's
total.

### Compliance windows

A [dose window](/explanation/medication-compliance/) like `07:00–09:00` is a
*local* wall-clock band. Whether a dose was taken "on time" is evaluated in your
timezone, so the answer does not shift when you cross daylight-saving boundaries
or travel. A weekly dose's multi-day catch-up reach is likewise computed in local
days.

## Why a stored preference, not the browser's guess

HealthLog uses your **stored** timezone preference rather than inferring from the
browser on every request. Background jobs — the nightly briefing, the reminder
cron, the daily-mean consolidation — have no browser and no request context, so
they need a durable per-user timezone to compute "today" correctly. A stored
preference gives every surface, foreground and background, the same answer.

## The practical guarantee

Because storage is UTC and interpretation is an explicit per-user timezone:

- The same reading shows the same wall-clock time on your phone and your laptop.
- A daily total counts the readings that fall on that local day, not a UTC day.
- "On time" for a dose means on time where you are, consistently, across DST and
  travel.
- Exports and the FHIR API carry the UTC instant, so a downstream system applies
  its own interpretation without inheriting HealthLog's display choice.
