HealthLog computes a transparent tier of derived wellness metrics — a daily
Readiness and Recovery score, a Sleep score, an early-Strain flag, a fitness-age
band, a vascular-age delta, and an HRV-balance band — each from your own
measurements. The design rule is honesty: every score cites its inputs and the
method behind it, and returns "not enough data" rather than fabricating a number
when its minimum inputs are missing.

These are **descriptive wellness framings, not clinical or training-grade
assessments.**

## The signals underneath

Most of the recovery-style scores lean on two physiological signals your wearable
already records:

- **Heart-rate variability (HRV)**, usually as **RMSSD** (the root mean square of
  successive differences between heartbeats). RMSSD is a widely used,
  non-invasive marker of **parasympathetic** (vagal) activity and short-term
  recovery dynamics. Higher vagally-mediated HRV generally reflects a more
  adaptable, resilient autonomic state, and higher RMSSD has been associated with
  better self-reported sleep and lower fatigue and stress. See, for orientation,
  a [14-day observational study on daily HRV and self-reported wellness](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12300306/)
  and a [narrative review of HRV for monitoring recovery in athletes](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12787763/).
- **Resting heart rate (RHR)**, which tends to rise with incomplete recovery,
  illness, or strain.

A recovery score blends today's HRV and RHR against *your own* recent baseline,
not against a population norm, so it reads the direction of travel for your body.

## Why baseline-relative, not absolute

Absolute HRV varies enormously between people — by age, fitness, genetics, and
measurement method — so a fixed threshold would mislabel most users. The honest
approach is to compare each day against a rolling personal baseline: a recovery
score answers "how does today compare to your normal?", which is the question the
underlying research supports. The same logic applies to RHR.

## Degrade honestly

Every score has a minimum-input gate. If you have not worn a device long enough
to establish a baseline, or a night's HRV is missing, the score does not
guess — it returns "not enough data." A score is only **stored** once its minimum
inputs are present. The recovery score persists as a daily 0–100 series that the
dashboard, charts, and native client read without recomputing.

## The derived metrics

| Metric | Built from | Framing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Readiness | Recent HRV, RHR, sleep, recent strain | Daily "how ready are you" band |
| Recovery | HRV + RHR vs personal baseline | Stored 0–100 daily series |
| Sleep score | Sleep duration + stage distribution | Band |
| Strain (early flag) | Coincident deviation across signals | A flag, not a training load |
| Fitness-age band | VO₂max vs age/sex norms | Re-frame of an existing measurement |
| Vascular-age delta | Blood-pressure-derived estimate | Delta vs chronological age |
| HRV balance | HRV trend vs baseline | Band |

Each one re-frames or blends signals you already recorded, cites its inputs, and
links the method behind it in the app. None of them invent data.

## What they are not

These scores are not a diagnosis, not a training prescription, and not validated
against clinical endpoints. They are a way to read your own trends. The
proactive coach nudge that watches "a falling recovery trend" is using the same
baseline-relative signal — a gentle prompt to look, not an alarm.

:::caution[Not medical or training advice]
Readiness, Recovery, Strain, and the rest are descriptive framings of your own
data. Do not treat them as medical guidance or a training plan. Closed
commercial ecosystems compute similar scores with undisclosed methods; HealthLog's
differ in being transparent and honestly degrading, not in being clinically
validated.
:::
