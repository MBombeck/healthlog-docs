This is the page the rest of the project points at. One line governs how
HealthLog, its Coach, its briefing and insights, the README, these docs, and the
website all describe themselves:

> HealthLog is a personal health record-keeping and wellness tool. It is not a
> medical device and does not diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease.

Everything below is the working-out of that sentence. It exists so the same
framing holds everywhere, with no surface quietly drifting into the language of a
diagnosis.

## What this is, and what this is not

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="What HealthLog is" icon="approve-check">
    A place to log, visualise, and export your own health data. It charts your
    numbers against guideline reference ranges, finds patterns across them, and
    hands you — and your clinician — a clear record. It helps you keep track.
  </Card>
  <Card title="What HealthLog is not" icon="error">
    A medical device. It does not diagnose a condition, prescribe or change a
    treatment, calculate a clinical risk score, or tell you that a reading means
    you are ill. It never replaces a consultation, and it never claims to.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

The distinction is not a disclaimer bolted on at the end. It is a design
constraint that shapes what each surface is allowed to say.

## A screen is not a diagnosis

HealthLog supports validated self-checks — the PHQ-9, GAD-7, WHO-5, and SCI
questionnaires sit beside [mood tracking](/knowledge/mood/) as an opt-in
addition. These are
**screening instruments**, not verdicts. A PHQ-9 total places you in a severity
band that researchers and clinicians use to decide whether a conversation is
worth having; it is not a diagnosis of depression, and HealthLog never presents
it as one.

The right way to read a score is the way a clinician reads it: a structured,
repeatable number you record over time and hand to someone who can examine you.
The value of the instrument is the trend and the conversation it starts — not a
label the app applies on your behalf.

:::caution[A flagged self-harm item is handled with care]
PHQ-9 item 9 asks about thoughts of self-harm. When it is flagged, HealthLog
surfaces a supportive crisis-resource note — calmly, never casually, and never
auto-interpreted into a conclusion. The app points you toward help; it does not
assess risk, and it does not decide what the answer means.
:::

## The Coach describes; it never concludes

The conversational Coach, the daily briefing, and the insight cards all narrate
your data. They report what a number is, how it compares to your usual range,
and what changed. They do not cross the line into "you have," "this is caused
by," or "you should take." That boundary is enforced, not merely encouraged —
see [why the Coach renders plain text](/explanation/no-markdown-in-coach/) and
[the proactive Coach nudge](/explanation/proactive-coach/) for how the same
restraint shapes its outreach.

The MCP surface follows the identical rule: it ships data and context, never a
diagnosis or treatment change, and several signals (the mental-health screeners,
environmental context, structured records) are kept off it
entirely. The assistant narrates the facts and leaves clinical judgement to your
clinician.

## Where the standard applies

The same framing governs every surface where the project speaks:

- **Coach copy** — chat replies, the briefing, insight narratives, and proactive
  nudges describe data and never diagnose.
- **The app UI** — status cards, charts, and reference bands present context, and
  point you to a clinician where a reading crosses a recognised safety line.
- **README and docs** — including the [health knowledge base](/knowledge/),
  which cites guidelines for context but is general education, not advice.
- **The website** — the public framing matches the in-app one, word for word
  where it counts.
