A capability tour of what HealthLog does. For exactly what shipped in a given
release, read the
[changelog](https://github.com/MBombeck/HealthLog/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md); for
the reasoning behind specific designs, follow the links into the
[explanation](/explanation/) pages.

## Health metrics

Track weight, blood pressure, pulse, body fat, sleep, steps, blood glucose
(fasting / postprandial / random / bedtime, mg/dL ↔ mmol/L), total body water,
bone mass, and pulse oximetry (SpO₂) with interactive trend charts, moving
averages, and traffic-light ranges based on established guidelines
([ESH 2023 for blood pressure](/explanation/blood-pressure-targets/), diabetes-
association guidance for glucose, consensus pulse-oximetry guidance). Glucose is
stored in mg/dL and shown in whichever unit you pick under Settings → Account;
entry and editing follow the same choice, so a value typed in mmol/L is filed as
one. Every stored
HealthKit metric has a chart surface — flights climbed, walking speed / step
length / asymmetry, respiratory rate, the body-composition family, mobility,
daylight, and more — and a metric/imperial display toggle renders values in your
preferred units while storage and exports stay canonical SI.

Anything a device does not send can be typed in by hand. The manual form covers
blood pressure, weight, pulse, resting heart rate, sleep, steps, glucose, oxygen
saturation, body temperature, waist, waist-to-height ratio, grip strength and pain, and the full
body-composition set a scale reports: body fat, body water, bone mass, fat mass,
fat-free mass, lean body mass, muscle mass and visceral fat. The masses follow
your kilogram or pound setting; visceral fat is entered as the scale's own
rating, not a percentage. Resting heart rate, part of the Recovery module, lands
in its own chart rather than among your pulse readings.

The sleep field takes a duration the way you say it: `7.5`,
`7,5`, `7:30`, `7h30m`, `7 h 30 min` or `45 min`, and shows how it read your
entry ("Read as 7 h 30 min") before you save. One entry is at most 24 hours.

## Custom thresholds

Override the computed default ranges per metric with the targets your clinician
set. Audit-logged. The doctor-report PDF prints the effective range in force,
overrides included.

A weight target also sets which way counts as progress: below it a gain shows as
progress, above it a loss does, and inside it holding steady does. The weight
tile, the weight status note, the daily briefing and the Coach all judge your
weight against it.

## Customizable dashboard

Show, hide, and drag-to-reorder every widget; per-user layout with reset-to-
defaults. The above-the-fold tiles arrive together in a single first-paint
snapshot, and charts reveal together once their data is in.

## Daily companion

The dashboard opens with a [Today view](/insights/today/): the
[Health Score](/explanation/composite-health-score/) ring with how long it has
been steady, a lead line about the day (built from your own readings when no AI
provider is set up), a short [**Today** list](/insights/today/#the-today-overview)
of what is on (medications taken, an appointment, last night, vitals, cycle day),
a "worth a look" rail of tap-through priority items you can dismiss, and a
coach check-in when a plan is due for a look-back. The score starts provisional
overnight, finalises the moment last night's sleep syncs (morning freshness),
and says which areas of health it rests on. [Quiet milestones](/insights/today/#quiet-milestones) mark durable
states reached — a personal best, a return to your usual range — never streaks to
keep. An optional once-a-day morning note (off by default, opt-in per channel)
carries the day's read. Everything reads the insight prepared overnight; nothing
generates on open.

## Intraday pulse

Heart rate drawn [across the day](/insights/intraday-pulse/) as a 10-minute-mean
curve with a day navigator — dense detail for 90 days, an honest hourly fallback
for older days, and no fake line across gaps. When heart rate stays lifted while
you're at rest and not moving, it's noted plainly as possible tension —
conservatively, never as a verdict.

## Nutrients

A [nutrients module](/guides/nutrients/) for fluid intake, caffeine, macros, and
the vitamins and minerals your device records — a dashboard water tile with
quick-add and a Nutrients page charting hydration and micronutrients against
their reference daily intake as context. Opt-in: the module ships off, and
ingest refuses while it is off. Once you enable it, device-synced dietary data
flows as your client sends it. A supplement with its composition
counts every taken dose toward the day's micronutrients, the page shows each
amount by source, and a day above an EFSA tolerable upper intake level says so
with its source; see [Upper limits](/guides/nutrients/#upper-limits).

## Metric catalog

A [quiet "All metrics" view](/insights/metric-catalog/) lists everything
HealthLog can track and which device supplies each — so a metric you don't have
data for stays out of your daily views but is discoverable in one place.

## Mood logging

A five-face quick capture backed by a structured tag catalogue. An entry can
also record five separate 0 to 10 values (pleasantness, strain, energy,
connectedness and agency), binary tags across feelings, sleep, health, social,
work and hobbies, and a note. Trends and a mood-relations view associate tags
and factors with your other metrics, and once there are enough days the record
shows how far a day sits from your own past pattern, as a connection and never
a cause. See [Mood](/knowledge/mood/).

## Medication compliance

Flexible scheduling driven by a single recurrence engine: time windows,
day-of-week recurrence, interval weeks, rolling intervals, RFC-5545 RRULE,
one-time doses, as-needed (PRN), and cyclic on/off plans — so non-daily plans
[score honestly against their real cadence](/explanation/medication-compliance/).
Route of administration (oral / injection / other) carries an injection-site
picker. Take / skip / snooze logging, a traceable dose history, supply runway,
and structured side-effect tracking. An
[efficacy chart](/guides/track-medications/#see-a-medications-effect-on-a-metric)
plots how a metric moved since a medication started — a data-scaled axis, a
"% since start" view, and the full intake history. A medication can have
several [courses](/guides/track-medications/#courses-medications-that-come-and-go),
each with its own start and end, and **End** finishes the running one at once;
you can file medications under
[categories of your own](/guides/track-medications/#your-own-categories). A
medication you take reliably can
[record its doses by itself](/guides/track-medications/#record-doses-automatically),
without reminders and never over a dose you logged by hand. See
[Track medications](/guides/track-medications/).

## Clinical signals and records

Additive to the everyday metrics, on by default and inert until data arrives:
[validated mental-wellbeing self-checks](/guides/clinical-signals/) (PHQ-9,
GAD-7, WHO-5 and the Sleep Condition Indicator, in every app language: the
validated translation where one exists, otherwise a faithful one marked as
such, with published severity bands and calm crisis-resource handling), grip strength, a 0–10 pain
score, waist and body measurements, and a longevity
[lab panel](/knowledge/longevity-labs/). Each is added through one
signal registry, so a new signal wires into
charts, correlations, the Coach, the FHIR map and the API at once. Structured
[allergies and family history](/guides/structured-records/) are encrypted at
rest and print in the doctor report.
[Life circumstances](/guides/life-circumstances/) (smoking, alcohol with the
AUDIT-C screening in validated wording, activity, work, sleep and diet habits,
goals) are dated, each with its own AI switch. A vaccination record keeps a lifetime immunization log: doses grouped under the disease they
protect against, optional booster reminders, and a doctor-report section you
can leave out. An
[environmental module](/explanation/environmental-module/), on by default,
folds weather, daylight, temperature, air quality, pollen and UV in as influence signals; nothing is fetched until you configure a home
location. **Custom metrics** let you
define your own measurement — name, unit, optional target range — and log and
chart it alongside the built-ins; custom definitions stay private to your
account and deliberately stay out of device sync and FHIR export, though they
do feed the correlation discovery that the Coach and the insights surfaces
read. You can also define up to eight
[symptoms of your own](/guides/illness/#your-own-symptoms), such as aura or
headache, and log one in a single step with an intensity from 0 to 10. Single-lead [ECG recordings](/insights/ecg-recordings/) synced from a
compatible watch are shown on a calibration grid with date, duration and average
heart rate: the device's own result attributed to the device, and no
interpretation of the trace by HealthLog. Each carries a plain-language, strictly
non-diagnostic descriptor that restates the recording's device-reported facts in
words.

## Doctor visits

Appointments and the practices behind them have their own section.
A visit records what kind it was, when it happened, its status, an optional
practice drawn from a contact list you keep, and an encrypted note. It links to
the lab results, documents and illness episodes it concerned, and those links
point at the originals rather than copying them, so a later correction in the
source shows through on the visit.

Visits and their checkups sit under one segmented view. A preventive-care
checkup is closed by recording the visit that fulfilled it, and a due checkup
can carry a planned appointment. A planned visit sets a reminder that the server
re-anchors when you move the date and clears when the visit happens or is
cancelled. It fires once, never nags, and carries no "done" button, because an
appointment is not a task you tick off.

Medications, visits, lab results, documents, conditions and
vaccinations show what they belong to in a **Related** section, from links you
set and from relations the record already implies; see
[Link related records](/guides/related-records/).

A procedure is a visit of its own kind, and a visit or an illness episode can
name a body site and a side. The **Body sites** view gathers the procedures,
visits and conditions filed at one site, with what is linked to them; see
[Checkups, visits and practitioners](/guides/checkups-and-practitioners/#body-sites).

In the [record-sharing model](/explanation/record-sharing/) visits belong to
the health-background section rather than a scope of their own, so a delegate
granted your health background also reads your visits and the practice list
behind them, under the same rules as the rest of the record. The section also
appears in the doctor-report PDF as a visit list you can leave out.

## Device integrations

[Withings](/guides/connect-withings/), [WHOOP](/guides/connect-whoop/),
[Oura](/guides/connect-oura/), [Polar](/guides/connect-polar/) and
[Strava](/guides/connect-strava/) sync over OAuth2; [Fitbit and Pixel](/guides/connect-google-health/) connect through
Google Health (the [classic Fitbit API](/guides/connect-fitbit/) ends on
30 October 2026); a
[Nightscout](/guides/connect-nightscout/) instance streams
continuous glucose; a [Dawarich](/guides/connect-dawarich/) instance adds daily
movement (distance, time away from home, travel days) without any coordinate; an [Apple Health `export.zip`](/guides/import-apple-health/)
folds your full history in, and so does an Android
[Health Connect export](/guides/import-health-connect/); the native iOS app streams HealthKit live.
Lab results and medications come in from a
[spreadsheet](/guides/import-csv/), and a scale, a Home Assistant sensor or
an indoor-climate sensor writes readings through a
[measurement token](/guides/ingest-token/), which can also pass them on to
Apple Health through a current build of the iPhone app. A per-metric
[source priority](/guides/source-priority/) decides which reading is canonical
when devices overlap, and each metric page shows where a value comes from.

The workouts page has an activity calendar and fitness, fatigue and form from
the training load. Lab markers with enough readings show their trend, and a
lasting change of level in thirteen vitals over the last 180 days is marked.

## AI coach + insights

Multi-provider, evidence-grounded insights: per-metric assessments, correlations,
a daily briefing, a weekly report, a
[Health Score](/explanation/composite-health-score/) tile that grades each
pillar against a published band and labels how many areas of health it rests
on, and a conversational coach grounded in your own data, reading your history longitudinally and able to
ask up to three follow-up questions from an encrypted self-context. An answer
shows which parts of your record the Coach read and, with a provider that can
call tools, the values it read as tables and charts you can copy, plus
suggestions for what to ask next. Conversations sit in a panel beside the
chat, and you choose how far back the Coach may look. With a model that
supports it, the Coach thinks before it answers, at a depth you choose, and a
single line above the answer says what it is doing while it works. It can draw
two periods or two metrics in one chart, asks once when a question could mean
two things, and remembers what you tell it, with an undo, keeping anything
about your health only after you agree; see
[Coach conversations](/guides/coach-conversations/).
[Proactive check-ins](/explanation/proactive-coach/) watch a handful of signals
and send at most one gentle prompt, suppressed when there is nothing genuine to
say. Pick
[OpenAI, Anthropic, ChatGPT via Codex, a local endpoint, or an OpenAI-compatible gateway](/guides/ai-providers/);
BYOK or admin-shared. Local endpoints keep all data on your network. An
off-by-default [MCP connector](/guides/mcp-server/) can open your own record to
an external assistant over OAuth, read-only unless you opt into narrow,
confirmed writes.

AI is optional all the way through. With no provider, AI switched off by the
operator, or **AI analysis** switched off by you, every reading, chart, score
and correlation still loads, and nothing is sent to a model. The operator's
switches stop the work they cover, including document reading and lab scans,
not just the text. See
[When AI is not available](/guides/ai-providers/#when-ai-is-not-available).
Each account picks how much background AI work it wants, frugal,
standard or full, within the bounds the operator sets; see
[AI usage levels](/guides/ai-providers/#ai-usage-levels).

## Derived wellness metrics

A [transparent metrics tier](/explanation/health-scores/) computed from your own
measurements: a fitness-age band, a vascular-age delta, an HRV-balance band, a
sleep score, daily readiness and recovery scores, and a coincident-deviation
early-strain flag. Each cites its inputs and degrades to "not enough data" rather
than fabricating a value.

## Clinician-ready output

A doctor-report PDF rendered on your own server (in your UI language), a read-only
[HL7 FHIR R4 API](/reference/fhir-api/), and scoped, time-limited share links you
revoke after the visit. The health-record export ships PDF, FHIR R4, or both.

## Shared access between accounts

One account can give another account on the same instance access to its health
record at a read, write or manage level, for all of it or for named parts, so a
household looks after each other without anybody sharing a password. A grant
never carries the account around the record: credentials, integrations,
notification channels, exports and grant management stay with the owner.

A health record can also exist for somebody who does not sign in at all, a
child or a person you care for. Its guardians look after it, can connect its
integrations, receive its reminders, and can hand it over as an ordinary
account later.

See [Give another account access to your record](/guides/shared-access/),
[Records with no login of their own](/guides/managed-profiles/) and
[What a shared record is](/explanation/record-sharing/).

## Built to be lived in

Installable PWA with offline support and an opaque-cursor sync delta feed;
medication reminders over [APNs, Telegram, ntfy, webhook, email and Web Push](/guides/reminder-channels/)
with late/missed escalation, each notification opening the page it is about
and saying no health figure on the lock screen unless you
[allow it](/guides/reminder-channels/#health-figures-in-notifications);
a [search](/guides/search/) in the command palette (⌘K or Ctrl K) that reaches
into your own visits, documents, lab results, medications and notes; a sub-second dashboard on years of imported history;
gamification achievements; nine display languages (English, German, Spanish,
French, Italian, Polish, Korean, Dutch and Brazilian Portuguese) end to end; an
explicit language choice sticks everywhere, and the automatic setting follows
your system language. Invite links with QR codes for closed
registration. Light and dark themes share one token system, so both stay
readable without a separate design.

## Private by construction

[AES-256-GCM encryption at rest](/explanation/encryption-at-rest/) with
zero-downtime key rotation, passkey login (WebAuthn + Argon2id fallback),
server-side sessions, strict CSP, HMAC-hashed Bearer tokens, and
[no third-party tracking anywhere](/explanation/privacy-architecture/).

## How it compares

HealthLog does not ship a sensor and does not try to be a ring or a strap. It
takes the readings your devices already collect, plus the ones they do not hold
(cuff blood pressure, finger-stick or CGM glucose, medication intake), and
keeps them on a server you run.

| | HealthLog | Withings web | Apple Health | Oura web | WHOOP web | Fitbit web |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Self-hosted | Yes | No | No | No | No | No |
| Source-available | Yes (PolyForm NC) | No | No | No | No | No |
| Withings sync | OAuth2 | Native | Via shortcut | No | No | No |
| WHOOP sync | OAuth2 (BYO-keys) | No | No | No | Native | No |
| Google Health / Fitbit | BYO Google client | No | No | No | No | Native |
| Apple Health import | `export.zip` | No | Native | No | No | No |
| Aggregates many providers | Yes | No | Partial | No | No | No |
| Multi-source dedup + priority | Per-metric | n/a | Limited | n/a | n/a | n/a |
| Blood pressure + glucose | First-class | BP only | Manual | No | No | Limited |
| Medication compliance | Cadence-aware | No | No | No | No | No |
| Derived wellness metrics | Transparent | Limited | Limited | Closed | Closed | Limited |
| Custom clinician targets | Yes (audit-logged) | Limited | No | No | No | No |
| Doctor-report PDF + FHIR | On your own server | No | No | No | No | No |
| AI insights | Multi-provider BYOK | No | Limited | Subscription | Subscription | Limited |
| Encrypted at rest | Free text, documents and credentials, on your host | Vendor cloud | Vendor cloud | Vendor cloud | Vendor cloud | Vendor cloud |
| Subscription required | No | For some metrics | No | Yes | Yes | For some |
| Health data leaves your server | Only to services you connect | Withings cloud | Apple cloud | Oura cloud | WHOOP cloud | Fitbit cloud |

HealthLog is the right fit when you want your wearable and clinical numbers in one
place that you control, with explainable analytics on top, not when you want a
closed device that scores you and keeps the data.
