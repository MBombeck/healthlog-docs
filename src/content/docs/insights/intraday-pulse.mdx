Heart rate is drawn across the day, not just as a single daily number — so you
can see it settle overnight and climb during the day. The intraday view sits on
the **pulse** insight page and reads the raw samples your devices already synced;
it computes nothing new about your health.

## The curve

The day's raw pulse samples are folded into **10-minute means** in your local
time. Ten minutes is fine enough to show the shape of the day without turning
noise into a jagged line. A **day navigator** steps back through previous days.

The view is honest about coverage: where a stretch of the day has no readings,
it does not draw a fake continuous line across the gap, and it shows how many
readings the curve is based on. A sparse day reads as sparse. A failed load
shows a retry rather than an empty state.

## Dense, then hourly

The fine-grained 10-minute detail is kept for the most recent **90 days**. Older
days fall back to a coarser **hourly** shape — the per-sample rows are folded to
one mean per local hour rather than pretending an old day still carries
10-minute resolution. The chart labels which resolution a day was drawn at, so a
folded older day is never mistaken for a dense recent one.

## The elevated-at-rest note

When heart rate stays lifted while you're at rest and not moving, Today notes it
plainly as possible tension — carefully, and never as a verdict. This is the
one interpretive line on the surface, and it is deliberately conservative.

What makes it careful:

- **It is not raw heart rate.** A hard run is high heart rate with high movement;
  it can never read as tension. The signal is heart rate sustained above your own
  **resting baseline** while movement is low.
- **A high bar.** A bucket must sit a clear margin above your resting baseline,
  hold for a sustained stretch, pass a mandatory low-movement gate, and clear a
  per-bucket sample-density floor before anything is reported. Under-flagging is
  the intended failure mode — silence over speculation.
- **Suppressed on the hourly tier.** The note requires the dense 10-minute
  resolution, so it never fires on an older, folded day.

Where a device also records intraday heart-rate variability, that is surfaced as
a confirming flag but is never required.

This is descriptive context, never a clinical stress score — the
[non-diagnostic standard](/explanation/non-diagnostic-standard/) applies to
every line on the surface.
