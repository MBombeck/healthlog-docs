This page walks through what actually happens between the moment you open the **Insights** page and the moment you see a recommendation card. It complements the [AI Insights feature page](/features/ai-insights/), which lists what's there; this page explains why each piece is on the card and how the pieces fit together.

The diagram above is the bird's-eye view of the same flow: your question (or the dashboard's implicit "what changed recently?") goes through a snapshot builder, then a prompt assembler that bolts the system prompt + locale + Zod citation schema onto the snapshot, then a per-user provider chain that walks ChatGPT OAuth → BYOK OpenAI → BYOK Anthropic → local → admin-shared. The response parser validates against the schema (corrective retry on a 422) before the cited reply renders. The sections below zoom in on each card on the resulting `/insights` page.

## The recommendation card

Every recommendation is a single card with five parts.

### 1. Headline + severity

The top line is the recommendation itself, in plain language. The left edge of the card carries a severity-coloured stripe — **urgent** (red), **important** (orange), **suggestion** (purple), **info** (cyan) — that also drives the card's order in the grid (urgent first, info last). Severity is decided by the model, but constrained by the schema and by post-validation: a recommendation that claims "urgent" but doesn't cite a meaningful deviation is downgraded.

### 2. Confidence meter

On the right of the headline, a colour-banded ring + 5-bar meter shows the **confidence score** for the recommendation, from 0 to 100. The score is **server-computed**, not whatever the model claimed:

- **Sample size** (up to 40 points). Log-saturating curve so going from 5 to 50 readings buys you a lot more confidence than going from 50 to 500. Below three readings, the score is hard-capped at `5 × n` regardless of the rest.
- **Recency** (up to 30 points). Linear decay from 30 points at zero days to zero at 30 days old.
- **Signal strength** (up to 30 points). 30 at \|z\| ≥ 1.5 standard deviations from baseline; null signal contributes a neutral 15.

Below 25 the meter renders a small "Low confidence — based on limited data" pill. The recommendation is **still shown** — we don't hide low-confidence findings. You see them, you see why they're low, and you decide whether to act on them.

### 3. Rationale (expandable)

Click the chevron and the card expands inline to show three rows plus a mini-chart:

- **Window** — the time range the recommendation was derived from. One of `last_7_days`, `last_30_days`, `last_90_days`, `last_year`.
- **Compared to** — the baseline. Usually your 90-day personal baseline, sometimes the prior period (when the comparison toggle is active), sometimes the guideline target.
- **Deviation** — the difference in plain numbers and units. Example: "+6 mmHg systolic vs. last month".

The mini-chart pinned beneath those three rows is the same Recharts wrapper that powers the dashboard, scoped to the rationale window. You see exactly the data the model saw, in 140 px of vertical space. If the chart series doesn't actually match the metric the recommendation talks about, that's a schema violation and the recommendation is rejected before it reaches you — the corrective retry runs first; if the model still won't comply, the recommendation is dropped.

### 4. Citation footnote

If the recommendation makes a normative claim ("your readings are above target", "consider increasing intake"), it has to cite a curated guideline by id. The footer renders **Source: ESH 2023 — Title** with an external-link icon; clicking opens the source guideline in a new tab. The cite-able sources are a curated bundle:

- **ESH 2023** — European Society of Hypertension guidelines.
- **ESC 2024** — European Society of Cardiology guidelines.
- **ACC/AHA 2017** — American College of Cardiology / American Heart Association.
- **WHO 2021** — Hypertension management guidance.
- **WHO BMI** — Body-mass-index categories.
- **DGE 2024** — German Nutrition Society dietary reference.
- **AHA pulse** — American Heart Association resting heart rate guidance.

The system prompt only injects references whose `metricApplicability` actually overlaps the metrics in your snapshot — a recommendation about glucose won't have a list of BP guidelines floating around in its prompt. The schema validates `referenceId` against the bundle: a fabricated id fails parse before it can render.

### 5. Was-this-helpful feedback

Two thumbs at the bottom of the card. Tap the up-thumb for "this was useful", the down-thumb for "this missed the point". The vote is stored against `(userId, recommendationId, recommendationText)`, so toggling within the same recommendation overwrites the previous vote — you can change your mind, you can't double-count yourself.

The mutation is **optimistic** — the UI updates immediately and writes through to localStorage so the verdict survives a refresh while the backend mutation is in flight. If the mutation fails, the local state rolls back.

## What "low confidence" actually means

When the meter shows below 25, the heuristic that produced it is one of:

- **n < 3** — there literally are not enough data points to compute a stable mean. The hard cap of `5 × n` is in play.
- **All readings older than 30 days** — recency contributes zero.
- **Within ±0.5 SD of baseline** — there's no real signal, the model spotted noise and called it a finding.

The card is shown anyway because hiding it lets the model's borderline finding stack up silently into a confidence-laundering exercise. You see the suggestion, you see the meter, you decide.

## How the multi-provider fallback chain works

Most users configure one provider — that's fine, the chain just has one entry. When you configure two or more under **Settings → AI**, the wrapper tries them in order. A provider is considered **hard-failed** if it returns:

- `401` / `403` — auth or scope problem.
- `429` — rate limit.
- `5xx` — vendor server error.
- Transport-level error — network refused, DNS failed, TLS handshake bad.

On hard fail the wrapper cascades to the next provider in your chain.

**Schema 422 still bubbles.** A 422 means the response body didn't conform to the strict schema — the provider returned text, but it wasn't valid as a HealthLog recommendation. Retrying that against another provider just wastes another paid request, so the wrapper surfaces the error directly and the corrective retry on the same provider tries to fix it.

The **last provider that successfully answered** is cached for an hour per user. So after a vendor outage, the cascade only runs once — the next generation goes straight to the working provider. The "active provider" badge in **Settings → AI** shows which entry actually served the most recent generation, so you can see at a glance whether the fallback kicked in.

## How feedback shapes future generations

Today, feedback affects two things:

1. **Admin observability.** A daily aggregator (`feedback-aggregator`, runs at 04:00 Europe/Berlin) writes a per-(severity × provider × prompt-version) helpful-rate summary into `AppSettings.adminAiInsightsFeedbackSummary`. The admin can see at `/admin/ai-quality` whether one provider is producing recommendations users find useful and another isn't, or whether a new prompt version is regressing.
2. **Per-user learning** *(not yet enabled)*. The cross-user prompt ratchet — feeding aggregate helpful-rate data back into the system prompt to nudge the model toward what users actually find helpful — is intentionally deferred until enough feedback has been collected to be statistically meaningful.

Feedback is **not** used as a soft-block filter. A recommendation isn't hidden because previous similar recommendations got down-voted; that would create a feedback loop where the model only ever surfaces what's already popular and never tells you the thing you need to hear.

## Privacy: feedback is per-user only

Your individual thumbs-up / thumbs-down votes are stored against your user id and **never leave your instance** in any form an admin can read back. The `/admin/ai-quality` aggregate is only counts, grouped by severity and provider. The per-user `RecommendationFeedback` rows are admin-invisible: there is no admin route that returns them, and the daily aggregator is the only consumer of the table.

The aggregator runs **on the same Postgres** that holds the rest of your data. Cross-user feedback data is never exfiltrated to the cloud, and the daily summary only writes counts (number of up-votes / down-votes per group), not text.

If you delete your account, your feedback rows cascade-delete with the rest of your data via the standard GDPR cascade — there is no orphan row left behind to feed an aggregate after you're gone.

## What the schema enforces

For reference, every recommendation is parsed against a strict Zod schema that requires:

- A non-empty `text`.
- A severity in `{ urgent, important, suggestion, info }`.
- A non-empty `rationale.dataWindow` from the bucket enum, plus non-empty `comparedTo` and `deviation` strings.
- A `referenceId` (optional, but if present must match the curated bundle).
- A confidence number in `0..100` (which the wrapper then **overwrites** with the deterministic computation).
- A non-empty `metricSource`.

A recommendation that fails any of those is sent back to the model once with a corrective prompt. If the second attempt still fails, the recommendation is dropped — you see the rest of the response, not a half-broken card. If the entire response fails parse, you get a clean error message instead of a half-formed dashboard.

## A worked example

A user has 14 daily blood-pressure readings, the most recent 2 days ago, the 30-day mean is 138/85 and the 90-day mean is 132/80.

The model emits a recommendation with severity `important`, dataWindow `last_30_days`, comparedTo "your 90-day personal baseline", deviation "+6 mmHg systolic over last 30 days", and `referenceId: "esh-2023"`.

Server-side:

- **Sample size**: n = 14 → log-saturation gives ~30 / 40 points.
- **Recency**: 2 days old → 28 / 30 points.
- **Signal**: \|z\| ~ 1.0 → ~20 / 30 points.

Final confidence: **78** — green band, confident recommendation. The card shows the headline, the rationale opens to a 30-day BP mini-chart, the citation footer links to the ESH 2023 guideline page, and the thumbs sit at the bottom waiting for your verdict.

If the same user only had 2 readings, the hard cap kicks in at `5 × 2 = 10` confidence and the "Low confidence — based on limited data" pill shows. Same recommendation text, same rationale, same citation — the difference is the meter, and the meter is honest.
