Body weight is the simplest health number to record and one of the easiest to over-read. A single figure on a scale conflates fat, muscle, bone and water, and it swings a kilogram or two across a day on hydration alone. The skill is in reading the trend and the distribution, not the morning number.

## What the numbers measure

- **Body weight** — total body mass. Useful as a trend; uninformative about composition.
- **BMI** — weight (kg) divided by height squared (m²). A population-level proxy for adiposity, designed to screen groups rather than verdict on individuals.
- **Waist circumference (WC), waist-to-hip ratio (WHR), waist-to-height ratio (WHtR)** — proxies for central and visceral fat, which predicts cardiometabolic risk independently of BMI.

## Why it matters

Weight and its distribution are practical signals of energy balance and sit at the centre of managing obesity, type-2 diabetes, hypertension and dyslipidaemia. A sustained 5–10% weight loss meaningfully improves glycaemia, blood pressure and lipids, and ≥10–15% yields greater cardiometabolic benefit.[^nih1998] Central fat is more metabolically harmful than peripheral fat — which is why a waist tape often tells you more than the scale.[^who2008]

## How to read your numbers

### BMI

| Category | Range (kg/m²) | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Underweight | &lt;18.5 | WHO, 2000[^who2000] |
| Normal | 18.5–24.9 | WHO, 2000[^who2000] |
| Overweight | 25.0–29.9 | WHO, 2000[^who2000] |
| Obesity class I / II / III | 30.0–34.9 / 35.0–39.9 / ≥40.0 | WHO, 2000[^who2000] |
| Asian-population increased risk / obesity | ≥23 / ≥27.5 | WHO, 2004[^who2004] |

<Aside type="note" title="BMI is a screening tool, not a diagnosis">
BMI does not measure body fat. It misclassifies muscular people as falsely high and sarcopenic older adults as falsely low, and it ignores where fat sits. The cut-offs are also ancestry-sensitive: Asian populations carry higher cardiometabolic risk at lower BMI, so the WHO 2004 consultation flags increased risk from ≥23 kg/m², and the ADA screens for diabetes from BMI ≥23 in Asian-American adults.[^who2004][^ada2025] Read BMI as one input among several, never as a standalone verdict.
</Aside>

The 2025 *Lancet Diabetes & Endocrinology* Commission on Clinical Obesity argues this point formally: BMI alone over- and under-diagnoses obesity and should be confirmed with a body-fat or anthropometric measure such as waist circumference.[^lancet2025] HealthLog treats BMI accordingly — as a starting band, paired with the waist and [body-composition](/knowledge/body-composition/) views.

### Body weight

There is no universal healthy absolute weight. A healthy range is derived from height via BMI 18.5–24.9.[^nih1998] For intervention in adults with overweight or obesity, an initial 5–10% loss over about six months is the recommended first goal.[^nih1998]

### Waist circumference and ratios

The thresholds below are for European-origin populations; ancestry-specific cut-offs are lower (commonly ≥90 cm for men and ≥80 cm for women in Asian populations).

| Measure | Increased risk | Substantially increased | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| WC, men | >94 cm | >102 cm | WHO 2008; NIH/NHLBI 1998[^who2008][^nih1998] |
| WC, women | >80 cm | >88 cm | WHO 2008; NIH/NHLBI 1998[^who2008][^nih1998] |
| WHR | men >0.90 / women >0.85 | — | WHO, 2008[^who2008] |
| WHtR | ≥0.5 (keep your waist under half your height) | — | NICE[^nice] |

The waist-to-height ratio is the most portable of these: keep your waist under half your height, and it holds across sexes and ancestries.[^nice]

<Aside type="note" title="Measure the waist consistently">
Waist circumference is technique-sensitive. Use the midpoint between the lowest rib and the top of the hip bone (iliac crest), and standardise breathing phase and tape tension between readings. A casual measurement varies by centimetres.
</Aside>

## What changes your numbers

Day-to-day weight moves 1–2 kg on food, fluid and glycogen alone, which is noise, not signal — use a 7-day moving average to see the trend. Body-fat percentage at a given BMI rises with age, so an unchanged BMI across decades can mask a shift from muscle to fat. Ancestry shifts the risk a given BMI or waist represents, as the tables above note.

Cardiovascular risk is multiplicative across blood pressure, lipids, central adiposity, glucose, activity, smoking and alcohol — it does not stack additively, and no single number is destiny.[^who_cvd] HealthLog teaches how the factors interact but deliberately does not produce a percent-risk score, which is regulated clinical-decision territory.

## When to act

<Aside type="caution" title="Rapid gain — possible fluid retention">
An unexpected gain of more than 2 kg in 3 days (or more than 0.5 kg/day) is the one weight signal warranting prompt clinical attention in people with cardiac risk, because it can reflect fluid retention rather than fat.[^esc] Confirm with a re-weigh on consistent conditions before acting.
</Aside>

<Aside type="caution" title="Unintentional loss">
Unintentional weight loss of ≥5% over 6–12 months is clinically significant and worth discussing with a clinician. Loss of more than 5% in a month, or more than 10% over six months, is more urgent.[^aafp] "Unintentional" is the operative word — deliberate loss on a managed plan is a different story.
</Aside>

Routine fluctuation, a slow planned loss, or a BMI sitting just inside the overweight band are matters for coaching and habit, not alerts.

## How this connects to your other metrics

Weight is a lever on almost everything else HealthLog tracks. Roughly 1 mmHg of systolic blood pressure falls per kilogram lost in overweight or hypertensive adults, and a 5–10% loss improves glycaemia and lipids together.[^cross] Because BMI cannot see composition, it pairs naturally with the [body-composition](/knowledge/body-composition/) page — the two answer different halves of the same question. Glucose and central adiposity travel together; see [blood glucose and HbA1c](/knowledge/glucose-hba1c/).

Of these figures, only the waist-derived waist-to-height ratio enters the composite score, through its adiposity pillar. Weight and BMI are tracked and banded but are not score inputs; see the [health-score methodology](/explanation/health-scores/).
