Mood is easy to log and surprisingly informative once you have enough of it: patterns, triggers and correlations with sleep, activity, cycle and stress emerge that no single day reveals. This page explains how to read a daily mood scale and the four validated instruments HealthLog supports — and where the firm line sits between a screening signal and a diagnosis.

<Aside type="caution">
The validated questionnaires here are **screening tools, not diagnoses**. A positive screen is a prompt to talk to a professional, never a conclusion. If you are thinking about harming yourself, contact your local emergency number or a crisis line now — in many countries you can dial or text a national suicide-and-crisis line. This page cannot replace urgent help.
</Aside>

## What it is

Mood logging is subjective self-report of affective state, often a simple ordinal scale such as 1–5. Since v1.37.1 a HealthLog entry can carry five separate dimensions rather than one number: how pleasant the day felt, how much strain it carried, how much energy there was, how connected you were, and how able to act you felt. Each is an optional 0 to 10 scale, so a day can be pleasant and draining at once instead of averaging away, and a dimension you never answer is shown as not recorded rather than drawn through the middle. The quick one-tap check-in maps onto the pleasantness scale. Alongside all of that sit four structured, validated instruments: the **WHO-5** Well-Being Index, the **PHQ-9** (depression), the **GAD-7** (anxiety) and the **SCI** (Sleep Condition Indicator, insomnia). The quick scales are fast and good for spotting trends; the validated screens are repeatable severity measures with published interpretation.

## Why it matters

A daily mood entry reveals patterns and triggers and correlates with sleep, activity, cycle, medication and stress — often giving an early signal of declining wellbeing before it is obvious day to day. The validated screens turn that into something structured you can track over time and share with a clinician.

## How to read your scores

### WHO-5 Well-Being Index (0–100%)

A score of **50% or below (raw ≤12) indicates poor wellbeing** and warrants further depression screening. A fall of 10 percentage points or more is a clinically meaningful change.[^who5]

| Band | Reading |
| --- | --- |
| 72–100% | High wellbeing |
| 52–68% | Moderate |
| 32–48% | Low |
| ≤50% (raw ≤12) | Screen-positive — consider further screening |

### PHQ-9 (depression; 0–27)

The standard action threshold is a total of **10 or above**, which carries about 88% sensitivity and specificity for major depression.[^phq9]

| Score | Severity |
| --- | --- |
| 0–4 | Minimal |
| 5–9 | Mild |
| 10–14 | Moderate |
| 15–19 | Moderately severe |
| 20–27 | Severe |

<Aside type="caution">
PHQ-9 item 9 asks about self-harm and suicidal thoughts. **Any non-zero answer is a flag at any total score** and should prompt immediate crisis-resource signposting and professional contact — never an algorithmic dismissal.[^phq9]
</Aside>

### GAD-7 (anxiety; 0–21)

The standard threshold for probable generalised anxiety disorder is **10 or above** (about 89% sensitivity, 82% specificity).[^gad7]

| Score | Severity |
| --- | --- |
| 0–4 | Minimal |
| 5–9 | Mild |
| 10–14 | Moderate |
| 15–21 | Severe |

### SCI (Sleep Condition Indicator; 0–32)

Eight items scored 0–4 each, and unlike the PHQ-9 and GAD-7 the direction is inverted: **higher is better sleep**. A total of **16 or below** is the published screening threshold for probable insomnia disorder.[^sci] The instrument is validated in English; HealthLog presents the original English items in every language rather than an unvalidated translation.

| Score | Reading |
| --- | --- |
| 17–32 | Above threshold |
| 0–16 | Screen-positive — probable insomnia disorder, consider professional evaluation |

## What changes it

Self-report is sensitive to time of day, recent events, recall and how a question is framed, so the single most useful habit is consistency: same scale, same time of day. Mood moves with sleep, physical activity, the menstrual cycle, medication, alcohol, acute stress and life load — which is exactly why the correlations are informative once you have a baseline. A poor night's sleep or a high-stress day is a plausible contributor to a low mood entry, not proof of anything clinical.

Once you have logged enough days, HealthLog can also place a day against your own history: what the day would have looked like on your past patterns, and how far the day you recorded sits from it. Read it as a correlation, not a cause. It always carries the number of days it rests on and a range rather than a single figure, it names which of the things you recorded moved with it, and it stays silent below a floor of days rather than producing a number to fill the space. Your own rating is the leading reading; the comparison sits beside it and never replaces it.

## When to act

<Aside type="caution">
Treat every threshold here as a **screen, not a diagnosis**. A WHO-5 at or below 50%, a PHQ-9 or GAD-7 at or above 10, or a clear downward trend in your own scores is a reason to speak with a clinician — not a verdict. Any positive answer to PHQ-9 item 9, or a very low wellbeing score, should prompt crisis-resource signposting and prompt professional contact. If you are in immediate danger, contact emergency services or a crisis line now.
</Aside>

A single-item daily scale is not a validated diagnostic instrument — read it for trend only. The validated screens are widely concordant (WHO-5 screen-positive at ≤50%, PHQ-9 and GAD-7 action thresholds at ≥10), but concordant screens are still screens.
