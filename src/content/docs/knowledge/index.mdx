HealthLog records a lot of numbers. This section explains what each one means: what the metric is, why it matters, how to read your own values against guideline reference ranges, what moves it, and when a reading is worth a clinician's attention. Every range and claim is cited to a primary source — a recognised guideline body or peer-reviewed study, named with its year — and linked in each article's References section.

<Aside type="note" title="General guidance, not medical advice">
These articles describe population-level reference ranges and the physiology behind them. They are general education, not a diagnosis or a treatment plan. Reference ranges vary with age, sex, ancestry, altitude, medication, and underlying conditions, and your personal normal can sit outside a textbook band for good reasons. Where a reading crosses a recognised safety threshold, each article says so and points you to a clinician — but only a clinician who can examine you can interpret your numbers in context.
</Aside>
