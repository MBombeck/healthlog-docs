Medication adherence sounds simple — doses taken over doses due — but the "due" half is where a naïve count goes wrong. If a medication is taken every Monday, a per-day tally that expects a dose all seven days reports ~14% adherence for a perfectly-followed schedule. HealthLog computes compliance **against the cadence that was actually in force**, so the rate reflects the doses that were genuinely due.

This matters most on irregular, rolling and as-needed schedules — and it is computed once, by one authority, so the card, the health score, the coach, the blood-pressure gate, the dashboard pillar and the doctor-report all quote the same number.

## What "due" really means

The compliance engine builds the timeline of doses that *should* have happened from the live schedule, honouring every cadence dimension:

- **Days of week** — a Monday/Thursday medication is only "due" on Mondays and Thursdays. Other days are not misses.
- **Week intervals** — an every-other-week dose is due on its weeks only; the off weeks expect nothing.
- **Rolling / interval schedules** — "every N hours/days" lays its slots from the anchor, not from a fixed wall clock.
- **As-needed (PRN)** — declares itself never due, never reminded, and is excluded from every compliance rate. Intakes still log and still consume inventory; the history stays. The adherence denominator simply does not invent doses for it.

Each expected slot is then matched against your recorded intakes: taken on time, taken late, skipped, or missed. The rate is taken over the slots that were due in the window — never over a flat seven-days-a-week assumption.

## Schedule history is respected

When a schedule changes, the past is not rewritten. Every material cadence change archives the outgoing schedule as a dated revision (a "dose era"), and history, compliance and write-attribution evaluate each period against the schedule that was valid **at that time**. A dose taken at its then-correct time stays on time forever, even after you later move the schedule. Switching an as-needed medication onto a fixed schedule never repaints the schedule-less stretch as missed doses.

## Slot attribution and cross-source dedup

A dose recorded off-schedule — through the API, a Telegram button, the phone app — is attributed to the slot it belongs to rather than floating loose, so the same intake is not counted twice and a genuinely-due dose is not hidden by an off-schedule one. Intakes that arrive from more than one path for the same slot are de-duplicated. The result is that the figure a clinician reads in the PDF/FHIR export agrees with the figure on the detail page, because both run through the one cadence-aware authority.

## Streak

The streak counts consecutive days on which every due dose was taken. A day with no due dose does not break a streak (there was nothing to miss), and an as-needed medication never contributes a "missed" day.

## Why this is the honest way

A compliance number is only useful if "100%" means "you followed your actual plan". Measuring against a flat daily expectation would punish every non-daily schedule and make the headline meaningless for exactly the people whose schedules are most complex. Computing against the real cadence — and freezing history at the schedule that was live then — is what keeps the number trustworthy enough to act on.
