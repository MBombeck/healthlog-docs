The health score is a single 0–100 number that blends four pillars. It is meant to be reproducible — you could recompute it by hand from the four components — and it is assembled from one shared input builder over identical windows, so the dashboard ring and the insights card can never show two different scores.

## The four pillars

When every component is present:

```
score =
    0.30 × blood-pressure (graded)
  + 0.20 × weight-trend alignment
  + 0.20 × mood stability
  + 0.30 × medication compliance
```

Each component is a 0–100 sub-score:

- **Blood pressure (0.30)** — a *graded* clinical-proximity score of a recency-weighted representative reading (see below).
- **Weight-trend alignment (0.20)** — how well your weight trend moves toward your target range.
- **Mood stability (0.20)** — the steadiness of your recent mood, not its level.
- **Medication compliance (0.30)** — your [cadence-aware adherence](/methodology/medication-compliance/), the same authority every other surface reads.

The bands are green ≥ 75, yellow 50–74, red below 50.

## Why blood pressure is graded, not binary

An earlier version scored blood pressure as a binary "in range / out of range" rate. That is too blunt: a reading one point over a threshold and a hypertensive-crisis reading both scored as simply "out", and a borderline reading flipped the whole pillar on a single point. HealthLog now scores blood pressure on **graded clinical proximity** — how close a recency-weighted representative reading sits to the healthy range — so the pillar moves smoothly with your real numbers instead of snapping at a cutoff. The older "X% in range" figure is kept as a secondary stat, but it no longer drives the pillar.

The representative reading is recency-weighted so a fresh measurement counts for more than a months-old one, and the score reads a single labelled window everywhere it appears, so the headline figure is consistent across the tile, the pillar, and the coach.

## Why a missing pillar redistributes, not zeroes

Not everyone has all four signals — no medications, no weight target, too few readings. If a missing pillar scored zero, the score would punish you for *not tracking* something rather than for a health signal. Instead, **a null pillar drops out and the remaining weights scale up proportionally**, so the score is always computed over the pillars you actually have. Three present pillars are renormalised to sum to one; the number stays meaningful and comparable.

This is the same null-redistribution pattern the [readiness blend](/methodology/recovery/) uses — a missing input lowers confidence, never silently drags the result toward zero.

## "Still computing" beats a wrong number

While the score warms up — too little history, a brand-new data type just connected — the surface says it is being computed rather than asserting a confident ring. A first reading of a new metric cannot knock the score out, because the gate checks only the types the score actually reads. As with every derived figure in HealthLog, an honest "not yet" is preferred to a fabricated number.
