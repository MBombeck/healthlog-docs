HealthLog's derived metrics re-frame or blend signals you already recorded against published age/sex norms or your own personal baseline. None of them is a clinical or training-grade assessment — they are descriptive wellness framings. Three rules hold across all of them:

1. **Computed from your own data.** Every score reads your stored measurements; nothing is fabricated to fill a gap.
2. **Cited inputs and method.** Each metric names the signals it consumes and the published standard it follows (listed below).
3. **Honest fallback.** When the minimum inputs are missing, the metric returns "not enough data" rather than a placeholder number. A score is stored only once its inputs are present.

Two complementary ideas underpin the tier. Metrics like fitness age and vascular-age compare your value against a **population norm band** for your age and sex. Metrics like readiness, HRV balance, and the early-strain flag compare your recent value against **your own personal baseline** — the [standard-score](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_score) idea of asking "how far is today from my normal, measured in my own spread?" rather than against a population. That keeps a personal signal meaningful even when your absolute numbers differ from the population average.

## The metrics

### Fitness age (cardio-fitness band)

Places your latest device-computed VO₂max against a published age × sex norm band. It never recomputes VO₂max — the number is your device's (HealthKit or Withings); this layer only bands and trends it, and expresses the result as a coarse "≈N years younger/older" frame rather than a false-precise single number. Without demographics, it surfaces the value and trend but no band.

*Standard:* Kaminsky et al. 2015 (FRIEND registry reference standards for cardiorespiratory fitness), with Apple's low-cardio-fitness cutoffs as the framing.

### Vascular-age delta

Frames a vascular-age estimate as a signed delta against your chronological age, banded optimal / normal / elevated with a ±2-year dead-band around your actual age reading as "normal".

*Standard:* Vlachopoulos et al. 2010 (arterial-stiffness prediction of cardiovascular events), framed against Withings' own relative presentation.

### HRV balance

Builds a personal-baseline band from your own HRV history and places the recent 7-day average inside, above, or below it (Balanced / elevated / suppressed). It is gated behind the baseline engine's minimum of seven distinct days, and it is a personal-baseline band — never presented as a population-normed HRV grade.

*Standard:* Task Force of the European Society of Cardiology / North American Society of Pacing and Electrophysiology HRV measurement standards, applied as a personal baseline.

### Sleep score

Blends sleep efficiency, duration adequacy, and stage composition into a band. Duration is scored against age-based need; stage percentages against age norms.

*Standard:* AASM scoring conventions (efficiency), Hirshkowitz et al. 2015 (age-based duration need), Ohayon et al. 2017 (stage-percentage norms by age).

### Daily readiness

A 0–100 blend of deviation-from-baseline components — resting-HR deviation, HRV deviation, respiratory-rate stability, and sleep adequacy. Each component scores 0–100 and the weights renormalise over only the components actually present, so a missing signal lowers confidence rather than silently defaulting to zero.

*Standard:* RHR elevation and HRV suppression as recovery/strain markers — Plews et al. 2013, Buchheit 2014.

### Recovery score

Persists as a daily 0–100 series that the dashboard, charts, and native client read without recomputing, so the same number is shown everywhere it appears.

### Early-strain flag (coincident deviation)

Fans the personal-baseline engine across your flagship vitals and flags when several signals deviate from their established personal bands *at the same time*. Multi-signal coincidence is descriptive, not a diagnosis.

*Standard:* the same robust personal-baseline basis as the vitals baseline (Hampel 1974; Leys et al. 2013 on the median absolute deviation), framed against the consumer "health monitor" surfaces of Apple Vitals, WHOOP, and Fitbit.

## Why "not enough data" matters

A wellness score that invents a number when its inputs are thin is worse than no score: it reads as confident while being unsupported. HealthLog's tier degrades to an explicit "not enough data" state instead, so a band only ever appears once the signals behind it actually exist.
