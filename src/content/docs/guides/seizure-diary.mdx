You look after someone with epilepsy, or you have it yourself, and you want a
record a neurologist can read: which seizures, how often, how long, what was
given and whether it worked. The seizure diary keeps that record. It is off by
default; switch it on under Settings, Modules ("Seizure diary"). In a managed
profile, the guardian switches it on for the child's record and logs there.

:::caution[What the diary is not]
The diary records what you observe so you can share it with the care team. It
does not diagnose or assess seizures, it never estimates a risk, and it never
tells you it is safe to wait. HealthLog is not a seizure alarm or a medical
device. Follow the emergency plan agreed with your doctor.
:::

## Set up the seizure types once

Open `/seizures` and add the person's seizure types under **Seizure types**,
ideally together with the care team. Each type has:

- **Your name for it**, the word your family uses ("the big one"). Encrypted at
  rest.
- **The classification**, in two steps: the onset (focal, generalized, unknown
  whether focal or generalized, unclassified), then the type. "Not sure" at the
  second step records the onset class itself, which is what the ILAE 2025
  paper advises when the next level is not known. HealthLog never suggests a
  classification.
- **Usual duration**, used for the "more than 2 minutes longer than usual"
  threshold below.
- **Emergency plan**: the steps agreed with the care team, in your own words.
  Encrypted at rest. This text is what the quick log shows when the timer
  passes the threshold.
- **Rescue medication**: one of the person's **as-needed** medications (add it
  under Medications first, marked as needed), with its dose and route.

A type you no longer use can be archived; it leaves the quick log and every
seizure recorded under it keeps it. A type no seizure names can be deleted.

### Classification: ILAE 2025, with the 2017 names beside it

The diary stores the ILAE 2025 seizure type (Beniczky, Trinka et al.,
Epilepsia 2025) and shows the ILAE 2017 name next to it, because most letters
and many clinicians still use it. The mapping is one to one for every common
type, for example:

| ILAE 2025 | ILAE 2017 |
| --- | --- |
| Focal preserved consciousness seizure | Focal aware seizure |
| Focal impaired consciousness seizure | Focal impaired awareness seizure |
| Focal to bilateral tonic-clonic seizure | Focal to bilateral tonic-clonic seizure |
| Typical absence seizure | Generalized non-motor (absence) seizure, typical |

Generalized negative myoclonus is new in 2025 and has no 2017 name.

## Log a seizure with the quick log

The quick log is one screen made for a phone held in one hand:

1. Tap the type (the last one used is already picked).
2. Tap **Seizure started**. The large timer runs.
3. If you give the rescue medication, tap **Rescue given**; the time, drug and
   dose from the plan are stamped.
4. Tap **Seizure stopped**. The seizure is saved with its timed duration.
5. Tap **Add details** to fill in the rest now or later.

The timer stores the moment it started, not a running count, so reloading the
page or locking the phone does not lose it. If nothing could be timed, use
**Record a seizure that was not timed** and enter the start and an estimate.

### When the plan appears

The person's own plan and a call button for the local emergency number appear
when the timer passes the type's threshold:

| Type | Threshold | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bilateral tonic-clonic (focal to bilateral, generalized, unknown onset) | 5 minutes, or the usual duration plus 2 minutes when that comes first | ILAE t1 (Trinka et al., Epilepsia 2015); NICE NG217 §7.3 |
| Focal with impaired consciousness, absences | 10 minutes | ILAE t1 (Trinka et al., Epilepsia 2015) |
| Every other type, and no type | 5 minutes | CDC seizure first aid |

Before the threshold the timer says nothing about waiting. The emergency
guidance below the timer stays visible the whole time: call emergency services
if a seizure with jerking or stiffening lasts 5 minutes or longer, if another
seizure follows before the person recovers, if breathing is difficult, if they
are injured, if the seizure happened in water, or if it is their first
seizure. Your own emergency plan comes first.

The call button uses the emergency number HealthLog has for the language you
use it in: 112 for most languages (it works across the EU and is routed on most
mobile networks elsewhere), 119 in Korean. If your local number differs, write
it into the plan text.

## The details

Everything except the start is optional:

- **Certainty**: a seizure, or suspected. Suspected events are listed and never
  counted in the figures.
- **How many**: more than one for a tally, such as the absences of a day.
- **Awake or asleep**, responsiveness and recall (asked separately, because a
  carer can often answer one but not the other), what happened in order, a
  description, a warning beforehand.
- **Afterwards**: minutes until back to usual, sleep, confusion, headache,
  weakness on one side (Todd's paresis), injuries, emergency care.
- **Suspected triggers**: your own suspicion, shown as suspected in the report.
- **Witness**, and who logged it. A seizure logged by a guardian or a carer is
  marked as recorded by someone with access.

Each rescue dose can be given an effect (stopped, eased, no effect) once you
know it.

### Rescue doses and the medication record

A rescue dose that names an as-needed medication is also recorded as a dose on
that medication, so its history and stock include it. As-needed medication is
never counted in adherence, so a rescue dose changes no compliance figure.
Deleting the dose or the seizure takes the dose off the medication again.

## Clusters

A cluster is a number of seizures within a number of hours. The rule belongs in
the person's emergency plan and is set under **Cluster rule**; until you set
one, the diary uses 2 within 24 hours. NICE (NG217 §7.2.1) uses 3 within 24
hours, and that count is shown beside yours. There is no single agreed
definition (Haut et al., Epilepsia 2022), which is why the rule is yours.

Clusters are derived each time from the recorded seizures: a window starting at
each seizure qualifies when it holds enough seizures (a tally counts with its
number), and overlapping qualifying windows are one episode. Changing the rule
re-derives every past cluster.

## The figures

The diary shows, for the last 90 days: recorded seizures, days with a seizure,
days since the last recorded seizure, the longest run without one, clusters
under your rule and under NICE's, and seizures per type and month. These are
recorded seizures: diaries miss many, especially seizures from sleep (Hoppe et
al., Arch Neurol 2007), so the diary never calls a period "seizure-free".

## The doctor report

Choose **Seizure diary** in the report selection (it is on the standard
template). The section holds:

1. A summary: recorded seizures, seizure days, seizure-free days, seizures per
   28 days, the longest run and days since the last seizure, bilateral
   tonic-clonic and from-sleep counts, clusters under your rule and NICE's,
   rescue doses, and paramedic, emergency department and admission counts.
2. Seizures by type and month, with seizure days.
3. A monthly chart with the starts, stops and dose changes of regularly
   scheduled medications as numbered markers.
4. Durations per type: median, longest, how many reached t1, how many were 2
   minutes over the usual duration.
5. Rescue medication: date, drug, dose, route, minutes after onset, effect.
6. Suspected triggers.
7. Things recorded alongside, each only when the data exists and its own
   section is in the report: seizures that followed a missed or skipped
   scheduled dose within 48 hours; seizures by cycle day, with the rate around
   the period and around ovulation compared with other days after three
   complete tracked cycles (Herzog's twofold criterion is named as a
   reference, never as a finding; Herzog et al., Epilepsia 1997); sleep the
   night before seizure days; seizures during an illness.
8. Every recorded seizure.

The footer says what the record is: a patient or carer recorded diary,
recorded seizures, likely an undercount.

## FHIR export

In the FHIR document export each seizure is an `Observation` coded SNOMED CT
313307000 "Epileptic seizure" (91175000 "Seizure" when suspected or
unclassified, status `preliminary` when suspected), with the type as a SNOMED
value; each rescue dose is a `MedicationAdministration` that points at its
seizure; an emergency department visit or admission is an `Encounter`; each
cluster is an `Observation` with its seizures as members; and three summaries
use LOINC 99312-1 (seizure frequency), 99313-9 (time since last seizure) and
99311-3 (seizure type, 2017 categories). No `Condition` is ever created from
the diary, and a duration over t1 is a note, never a status epilepticus code.
See [FHIR export](/features/fhir-export/).

## Sharing, backups and the assistant

- **Sharing**: the diary belongs to the **Illness** section of a shared record.
  A read grant sees it; a write grant can log seizures and rescue doses; a
  manage grant (and the guardian of a managed profile) can also edit, delete,
  set up types and the cluster rule.
- **Backups** carry the types, every seizure with its doses, and the rule.
- **MCP**: the `get_seizures` tool reads the diary for an assistant you
  connected, with your own words fenced as user text.
