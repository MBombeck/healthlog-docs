Some things shape your health without being a measurement: whether you smoke,
how much you drink, whether you work nights. Since v1.43.0 the **Life
circumstances** card under **Settings, Medical history** keeps them in six
sections. Every answer is optional, and every section can be left empty.

## The six sections

| Section | What it asks |
| --- | --- |
| **Smoking and nicotine** | Smoking status, products used, cigarettes per day, the year you started, when you stopped, regular secondhand smoke |
| **Alcohol** | The three AUDIT-C questions, see [below](#audit-c) |
| **Activity and sitting** | Days a week with moderate to strenuous activity, minutes on those days, hours sitting on a workday |
| **Work and rhythm** | Shift work, work situation, kind of work, night work, exposures at work, free days of the week |
| **Sleep, diet and caffeine** | Regular nap, whether others say you snore, diet and its specifics, late meals, fruit and vegetable portions, caffeinated drinks and the latest one of the day |
| **Goals** | Up to three things you want to work on |

A few values are worked out from the answers and shown beside them:
pack-years from cigarettes per day and the start year, minutes of activity a
week against the WHO recommendation of 150, and your free days. Without an
answer, Saturday and Sunday count as free days. Measured steps come first; the
activity answer adds context.

## Valid since, and still true

Each answer can carry a **Valid since** month. Without one it holds from the
day you save it. A new answer to the same question starts where the previous
one ended, so the history of an answer stays in order.

Once the newest answer or confirmation of a section is more than a year old,
the section asks **Is this still true?**. **Still true** records the date and
changes nothing else.

## What the AI may read

Each section has its own switch, **Share this section with the AI**. Off
means the Coach and the briefings never see those answers; they are not even
decrypted for a prompt. Smoking, alcohol and shift work were sections of the
health profile before and keep their earlier setting, which is on unless you
turned it off. Activity, work, sleep and diet habits, and goals are new and
start off.

The free days of the week belong to the work section. They reach a prompt only
while that section is shared; otherwise the Coach reads nights by the weekend
default, as for somebody who never answered. Your own sleep page and dashboard
always use your answer.

## Where the answers go

- **The Coach**, for the sections you shared.
- **The [doctor report](/guides/checkups-and-practitioners/#report-for-a-visit)
  and the [FHIR export](/reference/fhir-api/)**: pack-years, the newest
  AUDIT-C as a screening result, activity minutes a week, hours sitting, and
  the work situation, kind of work, night work and exposures. Diet, habits
  and goals stay out: they are context for a conversation, not a clinical
  finding.
- **Screening hints**, see [below](#screening-hints).

A [delegate](/guides/shared-access/) with access to the profile section reads
the card like the rest of the medical history.

## AUDIT-C

AUDIT-C is the three alcohol questions of the WHO AUDIT. Each answer scores 0
to 4, so the sum runs from 0 to 12. It is a screening, not a diagnosis. The
card shows the sum and, when your profile states a sex, whether it reaches the
screening cut-off, with the source of that cut-off. A profile without a sex,
or with another sex, gets the sum only: the published tables have no third
row, and picking one of the two would be a guess. The card suggests answering
again once a year; earlier results stay in a history you can remove entries
from.

### Wording by language

The questions are shown in a validated translation where one exists, and in
the WHO English original otherwise. HealthLog never shows a translation of
its own.

| App language | Questions | Source of the wording |
| --- | --- | --- |
| English | WHO original | Babor et al., AUDIT manual, WHO/MSD/MSB/01.6a (2001) |
| German | AUDIT Lübeck | Rumpf et al., the version attached to the German S3 guideline on alcohol-related disorders (AWMF 076-001) |
| Korean | AUDIT-KR | Kim CG, Kim JS et al., Korean J Fam Med 2014;35:2-10 |
| Polish | Polish AUDIT | Klimkiewicz A et al., Drug Alcohol Depend 2021 |
| Spanish, French, Italian | English original, marked as not validated | none read yet |

The standard drink in the help text follows the language. The Korean version
asks about soju bottles and standard glasses side by side, as its source does.

### Cut-offs

The cut-off follows the language you answered in, because a national cut-off
was set against that wording:

| Language | Men | Women | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| German | 5 | 4 | German S3 guideline on alcohol-related disorders (AWMF 076-001) |
| Korean | 5 | 4 | AUDIT-C cut-offs from the Korea National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (KNHANES) |
| Every other language | 4 | 3 | Bradley KA et al., Alcohol Clin Exp Res 2007;31(7):1208-1217 |

An answer to the short alcohol question asked before this release stays
visible as an earlier answer. Once a questionnaire result is on file, the
Coach no longer reads the old answer, and you can remove it.

## Screening hints

From the smoking answers, your age and your sex the card shows two hints:
low-dose CT for lung cancer and an ultrasound of the abdominal aorta. Each
says whether it may apply, does not apply, or needs more information, with the
reason and the rule it comes from. The rule follows the account language:
German accounts read the G-BA rules, every other language the USPSTF
recommendations. A hint is never a reminder; nothing is scheduled.

## Taking over older free text

Chronic conditions and allergies written as free text in an older version can
be taken over into the structured condition journal and allergy list, one
entry at a time and only when you choose to. The free text stays until you
clear it yourself.
