You want to log your menstrual cycle in HealthLog. This page is the how-to;
the background on cycle physiology and what the observations mean is in
[Menstrual cycle](/knowledge/menstrual-cycle/). Predictions here are
descriptive. They are shown as a band, never a single date, and nothing in
the app is a contraception-grade or "safe day" claim.

## Turn it on

Cycle tracking follows its own switch, not the general module preference.
Under Settings, Modules the cycle switch writes that setting directly. When
it has never been set, the account's gender decides: accounts registered as
female start with it on, everyone else with it off. The admin can disable the
module for the whole instance. With it off, every cycle route refuses.

The `/cycle` page holds the goal (general health by default), your typical
cycle, period and luteal lengths (bounded to 15 to 60, 1 to 15 and 10 to 16
days), which secondary sign you track (cervical mucus or cervix), whether
predictions are computed at all, a raw chart mode without interpreted
overlays, discreet notifications, and the sensitive-category encryption
switch described below.

## Log a period

One tap starts or ends a period. Starting one opens a new cycle, closes the
previous one, and records medium flow for the day. You can also set the
period end, mark or confirm ovulation, and delete a cycle. Each day can carry
one log, and a day log can be edited or removed later (removal leaves a
tombstone so an offline phone learns it went away).

## Log a day

A day log holds:

- **Flow** and **spotting** outside the period window.
- **Basal body temperature** in degrees Celsius, with an **excluded** flag for
  a disturbed reading (fever, alcohol, late measurement). An excluded reading
  is dropped from both the baseline and the shift evaluation.
- **Ovulation test** result and **cervical mucus** observation.
- **Cervix** position, firmness and opening.
- **Symptoms** from the catalog or your own custom symptoms (custom labels are
  encrypted), each with a severity from 1 to 4.
- **Sexual activity**, **protection**, **pregnancy test**, **progesterone
  test** and **contraceptive**.
- **Note**, always encrypted.

Bulk entry accepts up to 500 day logs in one request, which is how the iOS
app syncs.

## Predictions

When predictions are on, the app computes the next period start as a range
with a confidence value, and, depending on your goal, a fertile window and
predicted ovulation. The method uses robust statistics (median and median
absolute deviation) over your completed cycles with outlier trimming, plus a
symptothermal layer that re-implements published double-check rules: a
three-over-six temperature shift confirmed by the mucus or cervix peak. With
few cycles the band is wider and the confidence lower. The iOS app runs the
same method with the same constants.

Turn predictions off and the app computes and stores none; it becomes a
plain logbook.

## What stays encrypted

Free-text notes are always encrypted. With **sensitive-category encryption**
on (the default), sexual activity, protection, pregnancy test, progesterone
test and contraceptive are stored only inside an encrypted envelope. The
trade is that those five then drop out of correlation and rollup queries.
Flow, temperature, test results, mucus observations and cycle dates stay
plaintext because the prediction and correlation engines read them.

## Notifications and export

Three notification types exist (period soon, confirm period, fertile window
soon) and all are off by default. Discreet notifications keep the event name
off the lock screen. The doctor report has a cycle leaf that is not part of
the standard template; when selected it carries statistics only (last period,
recent cycles, median length and variability, current phase), never
predictions or notes.

## Deleting cycle data

Three things mean "delete" and they differ:

1. **Deleting an entry** hides it from every read, export and report, but
   leaves a tombstone row so offline devices can sync the removal. No job
   clears these tombstones on a schedule.
2. **Delete all cycle data** (`DELETE /api/cycle/all`) hard-deletes day logs,
   custom symptoms, cycles, predictions and the cycle audit trail, and
   resets goal and length priors to neutral defaults. The tracking switch and
   settings row survive.
3. **Deleting the account** cascades everything.

Self-hosting means there is no cloud vendor to hand the data over, but it does
not make the data unreachable: a host image, a VPS snapshot, a database
backup and a device holding a live session are yours to protect. The app
repository's
[reproductive-health data note](https://github.com/MBombeck/HealthLog/blob/main/docs/self-hosting/reproductive-health-data.md)
covers the operator side, including keeping `ENCRYPTION_KEYS` off the
database host.

## API

`POST` and `GET /api/cycle/day-logs`, `PATCH` and `DELETE
/api/cycle/day-logs/{id}`, `POST /api/cycle/day-logs/bulk`, `POST
/api/cycle/period`, `GET /api/cycle/cycles`, `DELETE /api/cycle/cycles/{id}`,
`GET /api/cycle/calendar`, `GET /api/cycle/insights`, `GET /api/cycle/profile`,
`DELETE /api/cycle/all`, the custom-symptom routes under
`/api/cycle/symptoms/custom`, and `GET` and `PATCH /api/auth/me/cycle-prefs`,
which is the one route that flips the switch and therefore does not enforce
it.
