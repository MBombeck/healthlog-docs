HealthLog's clinical signals are **additive**. They sit beside your
existing tracking rather than replacing it — use the ones that fit how you
manage your health, ignore the rest. Each is added through one
shared signal registry. The somatic signals (grip strength, pain, waist) show
up on their own detail pages and in the correlations; the screener scores trend
on the mental-wellbeing page and are deliberately kept out of the correlations.
Both kinds reach the doctor-report PDF and the
[FHIR export](/reference/fhir-api/).

:::caution[These are self-checks and measurements, not diagnoses]
The instruments below are validated screening and tracking tools. A score is a
structured number you repeat over time and hand to a clinician — not a verdict
the app applies. See the [non-diagnostic standard](/explanation/non-diagnostic-standard/).
:::

## Mental-wellbeing self-checks

The mental-wellbeing module is available by default and can be turned off under
**Settings → Modules**; with it off, the `/mental-wellbeing`
page is hidden and the assessment routes refuse server-side. With it on, the
page leads with four instrument cards — answer the items, and HealthLog
computes the total and its published band. Results chart as a trend so you can
see movement between checks rather than a single snapshot.

- **PHQ-9** (depression) and **GAD-7** (anxiety) — each item scores **0–3**;
  the total falls in a published severity band. The PHQ-9's optional tenth
  question (how much the answers affect daily life) can be included.
- **WHO-5** (wellbeing) — five positively worded items over the last two
  weeks, scaled to **0–100** per the official WHO scheme. A low score gently
  points toward the PHQ-9 as a next step — a hint, never a verdict. The
  official WHO translations ship in all six app languages.
- **SCI** (Sleep Condition Indicator) — an eight-item validated sleep
  questionnaire. It is validated in English only; locales without a validated
  translation show the English items with an honest note rather than a
  home-made translation.

Instrument wording and thresholds are taken from the **official primary
sources only** — no paraphrased questionnaires, no invented bands.

- **They sit beside [mood tracking](/knowledge/mood/), never replace it.** Mood is
  your day-to-day log; the questionnaires are a periodic structured check. Both
  stay; they are not silently merged.
- **Item-level answers are encrypted** at rest, like the rest of your sensitive
  data — see [encryption at rest](/explanation/encryption-at-rest/).
- **You stay in control.** The module ships enabled, but nothing is recorded
  unless you fill in a check-in, and turning the module off hides the page and
  refuses the routes.

### Check-ins on a rhythm

Each check-in can be planned under **preventive care** with a default 28-day
interval — the intro suggests a two-to-four-week rhythm. The reminder routes
straight to the check-in page, and completing a check-in satisfies the
reminder, in line with the activity-aware reminder rule: remind only while
something is genuinely due, never nag after it is done. Completed scores are
recorded as server-computed readings, so they trend like any other signal.

:::caution[A flagged item 9 is handled supportively]
PHQ-9 item 9 asks about thoughts of self-harm. When it is flagged, HealthLog
shows a supportive crisis-resource note — calmly, and never auto-interpreted into
a conclusion. The screeners are deliberately kept off the Coach and MCP surface;
item content and totals are never exposed to a model.
:::

### How results are interpreted

A score is shown **with its published band** — the severity ranges from the
instrument's own primary source — and plain words for what the band means.
Every band cites its source, and the framing stays descriptive: where your
total sits, what the instrument's authors say about that range, and nothing
beyond it. A screening band is not a diagnosis, and the app never turns one
into a conclusion — see the
[non-diagnostic standard](/explanation/non-diagnostic-standard/).

## Grip strength

Grip strength, taken with a hand dynamometer, is a simple, repeatable marker.
Record the reading, and HealthLog trends it against age- and sex-based reference
norms so a single number reads in context. See
[grip strength](/knowledge/grip-strength/) for what the bands mean.

## Pain (0–10 NRS)

For anyone managing pain, the 0–10 Numeric Rating Scale is the standard. Log a
score, optionally **per region**, and HealthLog keeps it as a **trend rather than
a snapshot** — the shape over days and weeks is what tells you whether something
is improving. See [pain](/knowledge/pain/) for how the scale is used.

## Waist and body measurements

Waist circumference adds what BMI misses. Record it with **consistent technique**
(same point, same time of day, relaxed), and where your height is known HealthLog
derives the **waist-to-height ratio** and charts both against clinical bands. See
[waist circumference](/knowledge/waist-circumference/).

## Longevity labs

The longevity lab panel — markers like ApoB, Lp(a), and hs-CRP — is **entered as
biomarkers**, not as a separate signal type. Key in (or scan) the values and they
ride the existing labs and biomarker-detail rails, with reference bounds from the
curated catalogue. Since v1.37.1 a value read from a lab report also keeps the
reference range printed beside it, and that source range decides in or out of
range everywhere the value shows, because the physician evaluates against the
printed range, not a general one. The chart draws the source range as a second
band and marks where it diverges from the catalogue; a value without a printed
range keeps the catalogue as its net. See
[the longevity lab panel](/knowledge/longevity-labs/).
