Two accounts on the same instance can be connected so that one of them can open
the other's health record. A parent and an adult child, two partners, somebody
keeping an eye on a relative's blood pressure: the case this exists for is a
household.

Both people keep their own account, their own password and their own second
factor. Nobody signs in as anybody else. What you are handing over is a grant
from your account to theirs, and it does one thing: it lets them read your
record.

:::note[Read access, nothing more]
Access granted this way is read access. A delegate cannot add a reading, edit an
entry, or change anything at all in your record, and the server refuses a write
under a grant no matter what a screen offers. There is no setting that turns a
grant into a writing one.
:::

## Before you start

The person you want to invite needs an account on this instance already. There
is no invite-by-e-mail flow here; if they have never registered, they have to do
that first, either through open registration or through an
[invite link](/guides/invites/) from your operator.

You will invite them by the username or e-mail address they type into the login
form. If you are the operator too, that is the name you see in the admin user
list.

## Send the invitation

1. Open **Settings → Shared access**.
2. Under **Invite someone**, enter the username or e-mail address they sign in
   with.
3. Choose **Send invitation**.

Nothing is shared yet. The invitation sits in their own settings and gives them
nothing until they accept it, and until then your record is exactly as private
as it was a minute ago.

If the name you typed does not belong to an account on this instance, the form
says so rather than creating a pending invitation nobody will ever see. That is
deliberate: a silent pending row after a typo is a worse failure than a clear
refusal.

The endpoint allows ten invitations an hour per account. If you are setting up
several people at once and hit that, wait and carry on.

## Accepting an invitation

For the person who was invited:

1. Open **Settings → Shared access**.
2. The invitation appears under **Records I can open**.
3. Choose **Accept**.

Acceptance is the moment access begins, and the grant row keeps the timestamp.

## Opening somebody's record

Once an invitation is accepted, the record appears in the user menu under **Open
a record**. Choosing it reloads the app into that person's record.

A strip across the top of every page names whose record is open and says it is
read-only, with a button back to your own. It is loud on purpose. Somebody who
forgets they are switched will try to log their own weight into another person's
record, and an entry that was never theirs is a mess to unpick.

To leave, use the button in the strip or pick **My own record** from the same
menu.

## What a delegate sees, and what they do not

**They can read the health record.** Measurements and charts, medications and
the dose history, mood entries, lab results, the illness journal, preventive-care
reminders, stored documents.

**They cannot reach anything about the account around the record.** Not the
password, passkeys, second factor or recovery codes. Not the sessions or trusted
devices. Not API tokens or the MCP connector. Not the connected services, so
they cannot re-point a sync or feed their own wearable into your record. Not the
notification channels, so your reminders cannot be redirected to another phone.
Not the exports, the health-record archive or the clinician share links. Not
Insights or the Coach, because those send your data to a provider under a
consent you gave for your own use. Not sharing itself, so a delegate can neither
invite a third person nor pass their access on.

Display preferences stay with the person at the keyboard. Reading somebody
else's record does not change your own theme, language or dashboard layout.

These are refusals the server makes, not screens that happen to be hidden. The
reasoning is written up in
[What a shared record is](/explanation/record-sharing/).

## See when your record was opened

**Settings → Shared access** answers three questions for the owner at any time.

- **Who can open my record** lists everybody with access, when they accepted,
  and when they last opened it. Access that has ended stays on the list, so you
  can always see who had it and between which dates.
- **When my record was opened** is a day-by-day view: one line per person per
  day, with how many times.

That second list is bounded by your instance's audit retention, which defaults
to 365 days and is set with `AUDIT_LOG_RETENTION_DAYS`. The page prints the
number your instance actually uses. An empty list means nobody opened the record
inside that window, not that nobody ever did.

## End the access

Either side can end it, and neither needs a second factor or a typed
confirmation. Reducing access should never be harder than granting it was.

- **As the owner:** **Settings → Shared access → Who can open my record**,
  then **End access**. If the invitation was never accepted, the same row offers
  **Withdraw**.
- **As the delegate:** **Settings → Shared access → Records I can open**, then
  **Hand back**.

It takes effect on their very next request, not at their next login. The grant is
checked against the database on every request, so there is no cached verdict to
wait out. If their browser is sitting inside your record when you end it, that
same browser lands back in their own account.

What ending access cannot do is take back what was already read. Somebody who
had access could have remembered it, screenshotted it, or written it down. The
product can close the door and cannot reach through it, and the confirmation
dialog says so.

Ending access never deletes the record of it. The row stays with its dates and
with who ended it. Inviting the same person again creates a new row beside the
old one, and they have to accept again.

## Access that lapses on a date

A grant can carry a date it lapses on, checked against the clock on every
request rather than by a sweep that may not have run. A grant one second past
that date is already inert.

The settings form does not ask for a date, so an invitation you send from
**Shared access** runs until somebody ends it, which is the common household
case. If you want a lapse date, send the invitation through the API instead and
pass `expiresAt` on `POST /api/account/grants`. See
[Shared access endpoints](/reference/api-endpoints/#shared-access).

## Sharing with a clinician is a different thing

**Settings → Shared access** is for another account on this instance.
**Settings → Sharing** mints a public, time-boxed, passphrase-protected link for
somebody with no account at all, which is what you hand a doctor at the desk.
The two do not overlap, and a delegate cannot mint one of those links from
inside your record. See
[Sharing documents with a clinician](/guides/document-vault/#sharing-documents-with-a-clinician).

## For operators

Nothing to configure. Sharing is available on every instance, needs no
environment variable, and does nothing until one account invites another.

Two things are worth knowing before somebody asks.

- An invitation to a username nobody uses is refused by name, which tells the
  caller that the name is free. The caller is already authenticated and is
  limited to ten invitations an hour, which bounds how fast the surface could be
  walked.
- Deleting an account removes the grants it is party to. Audit rows naming that
  account as the one who opened somebody's record stay and render as "a deleted
  account", because nulling them would turn "your daughter opened your record"
  into "you opened your record".

Backups do not carry grants. Restoring onto the same instance leaves shared
access exactly as it was; restoring onto a fresh instance means both people have
to invite and accept again. That is covered in
[Backups](/guides/backups/#what-a-backup-does-not-carry).
