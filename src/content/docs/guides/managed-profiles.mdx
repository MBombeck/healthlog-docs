A grant between two accounts covers half of looking after somebody's health. The
other half is the person who has no account to begin with, and never will: a
child, or somebody you care for. A **managed profile** is a health record for
exactly that person.

It has no password, no passkey and no second factor, and it never gains one. The
people who look after it are its **guardians**, and a guardian can switch into
the record the way any shared record is opened.

If the person you want to look after does sign in themselves, you want an
ordinary grant instead. See
[Give another account access to your record](/guides/shared-access/).

## Create the profile

Creating a managed profile asks for your second factor, so it happens in a
browser rather than in the phone app. An account with no second factor enrolled
is refused rather than waved through; set one up under **Settings → Security**
first.

1. Open **Settings → Shared access**.
2. Under **Records you look after**, choose **Create profile**.
3. Give it a **name**, up to 80 characters. This is what you will recognise the
   record by, and it appears wherever the record does.
4. Optionally give a **date of birth**. Leave it empty if you would rather not
   record one. Nothing is guessed from a year you did not give.
5. Pick the **language of this record** and its **timezone**.
6. Confirm your second factor.

The language and the timezone belong to the record rather than to you. They
decide how text written into it is worded, when its days start and end, and how
its reminders read. Your own screens stay in your own language while you work in
it.

You become the profile's first guardian in the same transaction that creates it,
so there is no moment where the record exists and nobody is looking after it.
Afterwards it appears in the account menu under **Open a record**, and under
**Records you look after** in the sharing panel.

The endpoint allows ten profiles an hour per account, the same ceiling the
guardian invitation carries. If you are setting up a household at once and hit
that, wait and carry on.

## Keep its settings

A guardian can keep the profile's own settings: its modules, its units, its
language and timezone, its thresholds and its notification preferences. Each of
those is read and written as that record's setting rather than as yours, so
turning a module off for the profile leaves your own record alone.

Connected services are the exception. Inside a managed profile they show a
status and nothing else. Connecting, syncing and disconnecting a wearable on
somebody else's behalf is not part of this release.

## Where its reminders go

A record with no login has no phone and no chat of its own. So the reminders it
produces reach the people looking after it instead: medication reminders,
measurement reminders, safety-floor alerts and low-stock alerts all go to the
profile's guardians.

Each guardian receives them in their own language and over the channels they had
already chosen, and the message names whose record it is about. It carries no
buttons. Opening the app takes you through the ordinary switch into that record
rather than letting you act on it from a lock screen, because a tap on a lock
screen is a poor place to decide something about another person's medication.

One guardian turning their own reminders off on their own phone does not silence
the others.

## Add another guardian

A second pair of hands is the point of the feature, and it is also the way out
of being the only one.

1. Open the profile's panel under **Records you look after**.
2. Under **Who looks after this record**, enter the username or e-mail address
   of the account you want to add.
3. Optionally give a date the access ends on.
4. Choose **Send invitation**.

They accept it the way any other invitation is accepted, under **Settings →
Shared access → Records I can open** on their own account. Until they accept,
the invitation confers nothing, and the roster shows them as invited rather than
as looking after the record.

A guardian holds manage-level access to that record: they can add, change and
remove entries, and keep its settings. What they cannot do is change who else
looks after it beyond the controls described here, and a managed profile can
never be invited as a guardian of another record, because a record with no self
cannot look after anybody.

## The last guardian

A managed profile can never be left with nobody. The last guardian cannot hand
the record back and cannot be removed from it, and the screen says so at the
place the refusal happens rather than letting it arrive as a surprise.

Two details decide whether you are the last one.

**Only accepted access counts.** An invited guardian who has not accepted yet
does not satisfy the floor. Until they accept, you are still the only one, and
handing your own access back is still refused.

**You cannot remove your own row.** The roster is for ending somebody else's
access. Your own way out is **Hand back** under **Records I can open**, which is
where every delegate ends their own access, and it is refused while you are the
only guardian.

So the way out of a profile you are alone with is one of two things: add
somebody else and wait for them to accept, or delete the profile.

## Delete the profile

Deleting a managed profile takes everything recorded for it: readings,
medications, documents and notes. It cannot be undone, and the confirmation
states how many people look after the record today and will lose it.

This is also the legitimate ending when you are the last guardian. You may end
the record. You may not leave it standing with nobody looking after it.

## What a managed profile is, underneath

A guardian relationship is a manage-level grant with the profile as the account
that granted it. That is worth knowing for two reasons.

Everything that holds for an ordinary manage grant holds here: the access is
checked against the database on every request, ending it takes effect on the
next request rather than at the next login, and every act is recorded under the
guardian's own name with a verb that says what it was.

And the profiles you look after are not a separate list somewhere. They are the
records in your account's own list of what it can open, marked as managed rather
than as shared, which is why they appear in the same switcher as a record a
neighbour shared with you.

## For operators

Nothing to configure. Managed profiles are available on every instance and do
nothing until somebody creates one.

Three consequences worth knowing.

- **Creating one requires a second factor on the creating account.** This is
  structural rather than a policy toggle: the step-up resolves through the
  browser session, so the phone app cannot create a profile at all and an account
  with no second factor is refused rather than exempted. If your users cannot
  find the button, check whether they have enrolled one.
- **A managed profile is a full record on your instance.** It counts against
  storage, it produces rollups and reminders like any other record, and it is
  covered by the same backups and the same encryption key. It has no login, so
  it never appears in a sign-in log.
- **The reminders it produces fan out to its guardians.** A profile with three
  guardians can turn one dose-due event into three notifications, each on that
  guardian's own channels. That is the intended behaviour, and it is the one
  place where a single record's schedule multiplies across accounts.

Deleting a guardian's account ends the grants it held, including guardian
grants, and the floor holds there too: an account deletion that would leave a
managed profile with nobody is refused rather than silently orphaning the record.
See [Data deletion](/account/data-deletion/).
