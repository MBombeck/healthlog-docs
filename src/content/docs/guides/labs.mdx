You have a lab report and want the values in HealthLog, compared against a
reference window and charted over time. The Labs module does that, and this
page walks through adding results, what the range bar shows, how a scanned
report becomes rows, and how the biomarker catalog works.

The module is on by default and can be switched off under Settings, Modules.
The switch hides the surface, including the "Since your last panel" card on
Insights and lab pairs in correlations, and keeps lab values out of every AI
feature: the Coach, the daily briefing, the biomarker note, the reaction to a
new panel and the MCP connector's lab tools and prompts. The doctor report and
the preventive-care reminder read lab rows either way. Nothing here interprets a value. A number
outside its window is information for you and your clinician, and the app
shows it without alarm colours or verdicts.

## Add a result by hand

Open `/labs` and choose **Add** (or **Add manually** when the scan option is
also offered). A result has:

- **Analyte**, free text or picked from the catalog. Picking a catalog entry
  pre-fills unit and range.
- **Value** as a number, or a **text result** such as "negative" for
  qualitative tests. Exactly one of the two.
- **Unit.** A reading in another unit than its marker's is converted where
  the conversion is well defined and refused otherwise; see
  [One unit per marker](#one-unit-per-marker).
- **Sample date and time.**
- Optionally a **panel** label, the **reference range printed on the report**,
  and a **note**. The note is encrypted at rest.

"Save and add another" keeps the form open for the next line of the report. It
keeps the biomarker, the sample date and the visit, clears only the value, its
note and the printed range, and puts the cursor in the value field, so a run of
readings for one marker is a run of values.
Every result is linked to a biomarker in your catalog: if the analyte name
does not exist yet (matched case-insensitively), a catalog entry is created
from what you typed. Two spellings of the same name resolve to one entry.

Results are grouped per biomarker on `/labs`, newest first, with the latest
value, the range badge, the range bar and a small trend line. Open a group for
the history chart (30, 90, 365 days or all) and the full list of readings,
where each one can be edited or deleted. A deletion is soft and offers undo.

A marker with at least three readings over a year also shows its **trend**:
the change per year with its interval, and, where the straight line would
leave the reference range within three years, roughly when. That is a
projection, not a forecast. A series whose newest reading is more than a year
old is shown without a projection.

A spreadsheet of past results can come in through the
[CSV import](/guides/import-csv/#lab-results-and-medications).

## Scan a report

When your configured AI provider can read images, **Add** offers **Scan a
report**. Pick a photo or PDF (PDF only where the provider supports it). The
image is sent to the provider for extraction, never stored, and never logged.
A PDF is read page by page, up to 20 pages and 400 readings. If the provider
stops partway through a page, the readings it returned are kept and the review
names the pages to check against the report. A scan that reaches your daily
AI budget stops there and says so. The scan runs as a background job on the
server, so a slow model is not cut off by a proxy timeout; keep the page open
until it finishes, because nothing is kept until you review it (see
[Long reads run in the background](/guides/document-vault/#long-reads-run-in-the-background)).
Each proposed row comes back with analyte, value, unit, the printed reference
text verbatim, a date, and a confidence per field.

Nothing is written at this point. A review screen lets you confirm, edit or
discard every row, and flags duplicates, low-confidence fields and analytes
that would create a new catalog entry. Only the rows you confirm are saved,
marked with the source "OCR"; a row that duplicates an existing reading is
skipped. If the document vault is on, the file is also filed there and each
saved row keeps a link back to it. That link shows on both
ends, in the **Related** section of the result and of the document; see
[Link related records](/guides/related-records/).

The review also says which of your markers a row will join. A
printed name is matched by your marker's own name first, then by a name you
gave that marker before, then by a common abbreviation of the same test when
the unit fits; the row says **by a name you chose before** or **recognised as
a common abbreviation**. When a row would create a new marker, **Belongs to**
lets you pick one you already track instead, and the printed name is
remembered for the next report. Two spellings of one marker in the same
report are read as one marker.

If your provider is text-only, turn on **Read scans on this device** under
**Settings → Modules → Labs**. The image is then read in your browser (German
and English models), and only the recognised text goes to the provider. It is
less accurate than a vision model, which is why the review step exists; it
cannot read PDFs. The OCR engine and its language data are served by your own
HealthLog server.

A lab report is a document, so scanning follows the rules for
[reading documents](/guides/document-vault/#reading-documents-with-ai).
The first scan that would go to an AI service outside your
server, your own key included, asks for your consent to reading documents; a
local model on your server needs none. The scan also needs the Labs module on.
When the operator has switched off **Reading documents** or the master switch
under Admin, Coach, a scan is refused and nothing is sent. Scans share
an hourly limit per person, set with `LABS_OCR_LIMIT_PER_HOUR` (default 6).

Reports that arrive in the [document vault](/guides/document-vault/) take the
other direction: a stored document that looks like a lab report is staged as
pending facts for the same kind of review, never committed on its own. On a
stored document, **Extract lab values** reads it the way a scan does: a PDF
page by page and text in parts, up to 400 readings. If the provider stops
early, the readings already found are kept and the review warns that some
values may be missing, every time it is opened.

A report usually prints the collection date once, in its header, and not next
to each value. A value from a document that carries no date of its own is
therefore filed under the document's report date, or under the day the
document was filed when it names no report date. An impossible date, such as
30 February, counts as no date. A value that states its own date keeps it.

## One unit per marker

A marker is tracked in one unit, and every reading is stored in that unit.
Spelling is forgiven (`mmol/l` for `mmol/L`, `ug/L` for `µg/L`). A reading
printed in another unit is never relabelled: it is converted where the
conversion is well defined and refused where it is not. That holds on every
path that writes a lab value: the form, a scan, a document from the vault,
the CSV import and the API.

### Unit conversion

Two kinds of pairs are converted:

- **Pairs that differ only by a power of ten**, for any marker: mass
  concentrations (`mg/L` and `g/dL`, `µg/L` and `ng/mL`), amount
  concentrations (`mmol/L` and `µmol/L`), international units (`mIU/L` and
  `µIU/mL`) and blood counts (`10⁹/L`, `G/L` and `10³/µL`).
- **Mass to amount**, for the markers HealthLog can identify, with factors
  derived from the molar mass: glucose (mg/dL and mmol/L), total, LDL and
  HDL cholesterol (mg/dL and mmol/L), creatinine (mg/dL and µmol/L), urea
  and blood urea nitrogen (mg/dL and mmol/L, kept apart because they differ
  by about 2.14), uric acid (mg/dL and µmol/L), total calcium (mg/dL and
  mmol/L), total bilirubin (mg/dL and µmol/L) and 25-OH vitamin D (ng/mL and
  nmol/L). HbA1c converts between % and mmol/mol with the published IFCC to
  NGSP equation.

Some pairs are left out on purpose and stay refused: triglycerides between
mmol/L and mg/dL (the factor rests on an assumed average molecular weight),
haemoglobin in mmol/L, ferritin and vitamin B12 in pmol/L, enzymes in
µkat/L and ionised calcium. A wrong factor would be worse than a refusal.

### What a converted reading keeps

The stored value is the converted one, in the marker's unit, at full
precision; rounding happens only on display. The value and unit the report
printed are kept with the reading, and a quiet line beside it says
"converted from 5.6 mmol/L". The scan review shows the same before you save
("Saved as 100.9 mg/dL, converted from 5.6 mmol/L"). Editing the reading
keeps that record unless you change the value.

A range printed on the report is converted with the value, so the verdict
compares numbers in one unit. The range note then states the window in the
marker's unit, followed by the text as it was printed and labelled with
its unit, for example "as printed in mmol/L: 3.9-5.5". The
doctor report, the Coach, MCP and the FHIR export state the converted range
in the marker's unit too.

### Which markers HealthLog identifies

A marker is identified from its name and unit. The name has to match, as a
whole, one of the names of a common marker in any of the app's languages
(the names offered when you pick a common marker) or one of its known
aliases, and the marker's unit has to be one that analyte can be converted
to. A marker HealthLog cannot identify this way is left unconverted: a
reading in its own unit, or in a power-of-ten variant of it, is stored as
before, and a reading that would need its molar mass is refused with a
message.

Renaming a marker, or changing its unit while it has no readings,
identifies it again. A marker you renamed to "Glucose" is converted from
then on; a marker renamed to something HealthLog does not know loses its
conversion.

### When a reading is refused

The scan review marks such a row before you save ("This marker is tracked
in mg/dL. Change the unit if the report was misread, or deselect this
reading."); the other rows are saved. The API answers `422`
`labs.unit.mismatch` with a reason.

A marker's unit cannot be changed while it has readings, because the stored
values would be shown in the new unit without being converted. Values that
a version before 1.40 stored under the wrong unit cannot be found
automatically. If a marker shows a reading far outside its usual range,
check the report it came from, delete the reading and enter it again.

## Reference ranges and the range bar

Each reading is judged against one window, chosen by a single rule that every
surface shares (the list, the chart, the doctor report, Coach, MCP and the
FHIR export):

1. The **range printed on the report** for that reading wins, when it yields
   at least one numeric bound.
2. Otherwise the **catalog bound** of the biomarker applies.
3. With neither, the reading shows no verdict.

The badge says below, within or above the window. Bounds are inclusive, so a
value exactly on the limit counts as within. Qualitative results never get a
verdict. When the printed range is in force and disagrees with your catalog
bound, a small note says so and names both.

The range bar places the value on the window. It is omitted for qualitative
results and for windows with no bounds. A one-sided limit ("up to 116") is
drawn with a synthetic opposite edge purely for layout; the label still shows
the one-sided limit.

A window whose low bound is higher than its high bound (a transcription
error) is never swapped. The bounds are dropped and the printed text is kept,
because the pair says the transcription is wrong, not which number is. Typing
such a range by hand is refused as an error.

Catalog ranges are editable defaults, not sex- or age-specific. The report's
own printed window is where that adjustment reaches the app.

## The biomarker catalog

Your catalog is your own list of biomarkers, each with a canonical name and
unit, optional bounds, a panel, a note on what it means (encrypted), and a
hidden flag that removes it from pickers without deleting readings. A seed
list of 30 common analytes across ten panels (lipids, glucose, thyroid, iron,
vitamins, inflammation, renal, liver, electrolytes, blood count) is offered
when you add an entry; you accept or override its unit and range. The
catalog is managed under **Settings → Modules → Labs**, together with **Read
scans on this device**. View mode and sort order are under **Settings →
Arrangement → Labs**.

## Merge markers and other names

The same test can arrive under two names from two labs, and then sits in the
catalog twice. You can merge them. In the catalog choose
**Merge**, select at least two markers and pick the one to keep. **Show
preview** lists, before anything changes, how many readings move, which of
them are converted to the kept marker's unit and which names will lead to it
from now on. **Merge now** then moves every reading in one step.

- A reading keeps the name and the value and unit that were printed on the
  report; a converted reading keeps what the report said beside the
  converted value.
- A unit that cannot be converted to the kept marker's unit stops the whole
  merge, and the preview says which one. Leave that marker out or keep
  another one.
- The names of the merged markers become other names of the kept one, so the
  next scan files them there.
- At most ten markers merge into one at a time. There is no undo.

Each marker's page lists its other names under **Also written as**. Add the
names other labs print, or remove one. A name can belong to one marker only;
a name another marker already holds is refused.

A merge rewrites and deletes markers, so inside a shared record it needs
manage access, like editing a marker.

## Where results go

Lab rows appear in the doctor report, in the Coach's data snapshot (latest
reading per biomarker over the last 12 months, notes excluded), through the
MCP `get_labs` tool, and as FHIR `Observation` resources with a LOINC code
where one is confidently known. On the [timeline](/guides/timeline/#lab-values)
a marker can be drawn as a line of its readings, with its reference range
behind them. They can be linked to a visit and to a
document, and picked as an efficacy target for a medication; see
[Link related records](/guides/related-records/).
