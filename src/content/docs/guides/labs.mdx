You have a lab report and want the values in HealthLog, compared against a
reference window and charted over time. The Labs module does that, and this
page walks through adding results, what the range bar shows, how a scanned
report becomes rows, and how the biomarker catalog works.

The module is on by default and can be switched off under Settings, Modules.
The switch hides the surface; the doctor report and the preventive-care
reminder read lab rows either way. Nothing here interprets a value. A number
outside its window is information for you and your clinician, and the app
shows it without alarm colours or verdicts.

## Add a result by hand

Open `/labs` and choose **Add** (or **Add manually** when the scan option is
also offered). A result has:

- **Analyte**, free text or picked from the catalog. Picking a catalog entry
  pre-fills unit and range.
- **Value** as a number, or a **text result** such as "negative" for
  qualitative tests. Exactly one of the two.
- **Unit.** Free text; the app never converts units.
- **Sample date and time.**
- Optionally a **panel** label, the **reference range printed on the report**,
  and a **note**. The note is encrypted at rest.

"Save and add another" keeps the form open for the next line of the report.
Every result is linked to a biomarker in your catalog: if the analyte name
does not exist yet (matched case-insensitively), a catalog entry is created
from what you typed. Two spellings of the same name resolve to one entry.

Results are grouped per biomarker on `/labs`, newest first, with the latest
value, the range badge, the range bar and a small trend line. Open a group for
the history chart (30, 90, 365 days or all) and the full list of readings,
where each one can be edited or deleted. A deletion is soft and offers undo.

## Scan a report

When your configured AI provider can read images, **Add** offers **Scan a
report**. Pick a photo or PDF (PDF only where the provider supports it). The
image is sent to the provider for extraction, never stored, and never logged.
Each proposed row comes back with analyte, value, unit, the printed reference
text verbatim, a date, and a confidence per field.

Nothing is written at this point. A review screen lets you confirm, edit or
discard every row, and flags duplicates, low-confidence fields and analytes
that would create a new catalog entry. Only the rows you confirm are saved,
marked with the source "OCR"; a row that duplicates an existing reading is
skipped. If the document vault is on, the file is also filed there and each
saved row keeps a link back to it.

If your provider is text-only, enable **local OCR** in the Labs settings. The
image is then read in your browser (German and English models), and only the
recognised text goes to the provider. It is less accurate than a vision
model, which is why the review step exists; it cannot read PDFs.

Reports that arrive in the [document vault](/guides/document-vault/) take the
other direction: a stored document that looks like a lab report is staged as
pending facts for the same kind of review, never committed on its own.

## Reference ranges and the range bar

Each reading is judged against one window, chosen by a single rule that every
surface shares (the list, the chart, the doctor report, Coach, MCP and the
FHIR export):

1. The **range printed on the report** for that reading wins, when it yields
   at least one numeric bound.
2. Otherwise the **catalog bound** of the biomarker applies.
3. With neither, the reading shows no verdict.

The badge says below, within or above the window. Bounds are inclusive, so a
value exactly on the limit counts as within. Qualitative results never get a
verdict. When the printed range is in force and disagrees with your catalog
bound, a small note says so and names both.

The range bar places the value on the window. It is omitted for qualitative
results and for windows with no bounds. A one-sided limit ("up to 116") is
drawn with a synthetic opposite edge purely for layout; the label still shows
the one-sided limit.

A window whose low bound is higher than its high bound (a transcription
error) is never swapped. The bounds are dropped and the printed text is kept,
because the pair says the transcription is wrong, not which number is. Typing
such a range by hand is refused as an error.

Catalog ranges are editable defaults, not sex- or age-specific. The report's
own printed window is where that adjustment reaches the app.

## The biomarker catalog

Your catalog is your own list of biomarkers, each with a canonical name and
unit, optional bounds, a panel, a note on what it means (encrypted), and a
hidden flag that removes it from pickers without deleting readings. A seed
list of 31 common analytes across ten panels (lipids, glucose, thyroid, iron,
vitamins, inflammation, renal, liver, electrolytes, blood count) is offered
when you add an entry; you accept or override its unit and range. The
catalog is managed under Settings, Appearance, Labs, together with view mode,
sort order and the local-OCR switch.

## Where results go

Lab rows appear in the doctor report, in the Coach's data snapshot (latest
reading per biomarker over the last 12 months, notes excluded), through the
MCP `get_labs` tool, and as FHIR `Observation` resources with a LOINC code
where one is confidently known. They can be linked to a visit (see
[Checkups and practitioners](/guides/checkups-and-practitioners/)) and picked
as an efficacy target for a medication.
