Fluid intake, dietary energy, the macronutrients, alcohol and the vitamins and
minerals your device or food tracker records have a home in the **Nutrients**
module. It is the supported path for hydration and nutrition; there is no
separate "water" or "calories" measurement type.

## What it surfaces

- **A water tile** on the dashboard shows the day's total fluid intake with
  quick-add amounts.
- **A Nutrients page** under Insights shows hydration over the last month, then
  an **energy and macronutrients** card (energy in kcal; protein,
  carbohydrates, fat, sugar and fiber in g) with a 30-day chart per nutrient,
  caffeine and alcohol when you record them, and the vitamins and minerals
  (sodium and potassium among them) you actually have data for, each against
  its reference daily intake where that applies, shown as context, never as a
  verdict.

Manual water entries and device-synced totals **coexist** instead of overwriting
each other. Sparse logging is never read as a deficiency.

## Enabling it

The module is **off by default for every account**, fresh install included.
Enable it under **Settings > Modules** ("Nutrients"), or with the one-tap
prompt on the Nutrients page. The external-AI options and the daily note stay
off until you turn them on.

While the module is off it also refuses to ingest — synced dietary data is
dropped, not quietly stored, so turning the module on is a real opt-in and not
just a surface toggle.

## Where the data comes from

Dietary intake flows in from your client's health store, from a food tracker
you connect with a token, and from the supplements you take (see
[below](#supplements-as-a-source)). Apple Health records water, caffeine,
energy, macros, and a full set of vitamins and minerals under its dietary
identifiers; those flow into the module **once your client sends them**. The
iPhone app sends energy and the macros from the release that asks for them.

A food tracker or a script can push its own daily totals, including alcohol in
grams of ethanol, with a measurement token; see
[Push daily nutrient totals](/guides/ingest-token/#push-daily-nutrient-totals).
Manual water entry works with no device at all.

:::note[The iOS client must send the dietary data]
Micronutrients and fluid intake are only as complete as what your device holds
and forwards. If the vitamins you expect aren't showing, check that the client is
granted and syncing the dietary data types — the module surfaces what arrives, it
does not invent the rest.
:::

## Supplements as a source

A supplement with a stored composition adds every taken dose to
the day's micronutrients. Give the medication its composition per tablet or
capsule, typed in or read from a label photo; see
[What a supplement contains](/guides/track-medications/#what-a-supplement-contains).

The Nutrients page then shows where each amount comes from: **Synced** (from
Apple Health), **Health Connect**, **External** (a food tracker or script with
a token), **Entered** (by hand) and **Supplements**. The parts shown add up to
the day's total.

## How a day's total is counted

Apple Health, Health Connect and a food tracker each report the whole day, and
usually the same meals: a tracker that writes into Apple Health and also posts
to HealthLog would otherwise count every meal twice. So for each nutrient and
day only the **largest** of those totals counts; the others are stored but
left out. Water you enter by hand and supplement doses are things no tracker
saw, so they **add on top**. The source split on the Nutrients page lists only
the parts that counted.

## Your own targets

On the energy and macronutrients card, **Set target** stores a daily amount
for the nutrient you are looking at: either an amount to reach (protein, for
instance) or a limit to stay under (sugar). The chart then draws your number
instead of the reference value. Clear it with **Remove target**. Targets are
part of the full backup, and clients can read and set them through
`GET` and `PATCH /api/nutrients/targets`.

## Reference values

Each reference names who set it. Where EFSA sets a value per day, HealthLog
uses EFSA's; for the codes added with the energy card:

| Nutrient | Reference a day | Kind | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Protein | 56 g men, 46 g women | recommended intake | IOM 2005 (EFSA gives 0.83 g per kg body weight only) |
| Carbohydrates | 130 g | recommended intake | IOM 2005 (EFSA gives a share of energy only) |
| Fiber | 25 g | adequate intake | EFSA 2010 |
| Sodium | 2000 mg | safe and adequate intake, shown as a ceiling | EFSA 2019 |
| Potassium | 3500 mg | adequate intake | EFSA 2016 |

Energy, fat, sugar and alcohol have no reference: energy needs depend on age,
sex and activity, fat is set as a share of energy, and there is no amount of
sugar or alcohol to aim for. A reference that differs by sex is shown only
when your profile has one.

## Upper limits

For twelve nutrients the European Food Safety Authority has set a tolerable
upper intake level for adults: the highest daily intake not expected to cause
harm. When a day is above it, the Nutrients page says so in one sentence and
names the source. Most limits apply to everything taken in a day. Two apply
to the supplement share only, because the limit is set for what supplements
add:

| Nutrient | Upper limit a day | Applies to | Form the limit covers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Vitamin A | 3000 µg | total | preformed vitamin A (retinol); beta-carotene does not count |
| Vitamin B6 | 12 mg | total | |
| Folate | 1000 µg | supplements | folic acid from supplements and fortified foods |
| Vitamin D | 100 µg | total | |
| Vitamin E | 300 mg | total | alpha-tocopherol |
| Calcium | 2500 mg | total | |
| Magnesium | 250 mg | supplements | readily dissociable magnesium salts |
| Zinc | 25 mg | total | |
| Copper | 5 mg | total | |
| Selenium | 255 µg | total | |
| Molybdenum | 600 µg | total | |
| Iodine | 600 µg | total | |

The source of each value is EFSA's overview of the tolerable upper intake
levels (2024 edition), citing the opinion it rests on. Every other nutrient
has no limit in this sense: EFSA set none, set a safe level of intake instead
(iron, manganese), or set one that depends on a chemical form a composition
cannot state (niacin). EPA and DHA have none either; EFSA found supplemental
intakes up to 5 g a day of both together raise no concern, which is a
statement about the pair. A day without a limit to be above is never shown as
"below the limit".

## Energy balance

Under **Insights**, **Nutrients**, the **Energy balance** card sets what you
ate against what your body used, day by day:

- **Eaten** is the day's energy total from the nutrient data: whatever your
  phone or a bridge sends (Apple Health, Health Connect, or a script on
  `POST /api/nutrients/batch`). HealthLog does not log meals itself.
- **Used** is resting energy plus active energy, the day sums your watch or
  tracker sends (`BASAL_ENERGY_BURNED` and `ACTIVE_ENERGY_BURNED`).
- **Balance** is eaten minus used. A negative number is a deficit, a positive
  one a surplus. The card shows the number and leaves the judgement to you.

The headline is the average balance of the last seven days, once four of them
have one. Below it are the window's averages and the last seven days that have
a balance. Every figure is computed when you open the card, so a corrected or
late import changes it with nothing to recalculate.

When a number is missing:

- A day with nothing eaten logged has no balance. It is not counted as zero,
  which would read as a fast.
- A day without active energy has no balance either: leaving active energy
  out would make a deficit look larger than it was.
- A day without a resting reading takes an estimate from the Mifflin-St Jeor
  equation, using your latest weight, your height, your age that day and your
  sex. The card says on how many days it did. Without one of them in your
  profile there is no estimate, and the card says what is missing.
- Today is still running and stays out of the averages.

The daily balance is also a channel in the
[correlation scan](/insights/how-it-works/), named **daily energy balance**. It
pairs a day's balance with the next day's readings under the same evidence bar
as every other channel: at least 20 days that hold a balance, and only those
days. It is never paired with energy intake, which it is computed from.

The Coach sees one compact line: the averages of intake, expenditure and
balance over the last fourteen finished days, how many of them held a balance,
and on how many resting energy was estimated. It is told to treat the line as a
description of your record and never to turn it into a calorie target, a diet
or a weight forecast. The line is left out when active energy is not among the
Coach's sources, and under a Coach lookback limit of seven days. AI analysis
has to be on for the Coach at all; the card and the correlations do not depend
on it.

For scripts and other clients: `GET /api/nutrients/energy-balance?days=30`
(7 to 90 days), behind the nutrients module, returns the same figures, with
`null` where a day has no data. Why the figures are softer than they look, and
how to read them, is the subject of the Learn article
[Energy balance: what your daily burn can tell you](https://healthlog.dev/learn/energy-balance-and-your-daily-burn).

## How it's read

Each vitamin or mineral is shown against its reference daily intake where one
applies, as **context**. HealthLog never flags a value as low or as a
deficiency, and never turns a day of sparse logging into a verdict. The one
statement about a high value is the upper limit above, worded as a fact with
its source rather than a warning. The
[non-diagnostic standard](/explanation/non-diagnostic-standard/) governs this
surface like every other.
