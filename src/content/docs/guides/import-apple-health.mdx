HealthLog accepts a full Apple Health `export.zip` and folds the contents into
the same timeline as every other source. The importer is streaming end to end —
multi-gigabyte exports never land in V8 heap — and idempotent on the upload's
SHA-256, so re-uploading the same archive merges instead of duplicating.

## What you need

- An iPhone with the Health app and at least a few months of history.
- A HealthLog account you can reach from the device or a workstation. The upload
  goes over your authenticated browser session — no API token to provision.
- Up to 1.5 GB of upload bandwidth. The parser caps inbound bodies at 1.5 GB.

## Do the import

<Steps>

1. **Generate `export.zip` on the iPhone.** Open **Health > profile picture >
   Export All Health Data**. iOS packages the archive in a few minutes; share it
   somewhere you can reach from your instance (AirDrop, iCloud Drive, email).
   The archive contains `apple_health_export/export.xml` (the canonical record
   stream) plus auxiliary folders: electrocardiograms, workout routes and,
   when the Health app holds records from a provider, `clinical-records`.

2. **Upload to HealthLog.** Open **Settings > Export & import** and drop the
   `export.zip` onto the Apple Health control. The browser streams the body
   directly to the server; nothing is buffered into memory on either side. A
   progress indicator polls the job until it reports imported / skipped counts.

3. **Watch the status.** The job moves through
   `queued → unpacking → parsing → upserting → done`, with `failed` as a
   terminal branch. The iOS client polls every two seconds while active; the web
   UI uses the same cadence. Only the job owner (or the admin who triggered it)
   can read the status.

</Steps>

## What gets imported

The parser folds these XML element types into the schema:

| XML element | Maps to | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `<Record>` | `Measurement` rows | One row per spot sample. Keyed by `HKMetadataKeyExternalUUID` when present, otherwise a deterministic `sample:<sha256>` fallback so re-imports stay idempotent. The record's own `unit` attribute is converted before storage. |
| `<Workout>` | `Workout` rows | Activity type + duration + energy + distance. |
| `<Correlation>` | `Measurement` rows | Compound samples like blood pressure explode into per-metric children; the child Records import individually. |
| `<ClinicalRecord>` (lab results) | Lab readings | Lab results from Health Records. The FHIR file each one names under `clinical-records` becomes a reading in [Labs](/guides/labs/#from-apple-health-health-records). |

Other clinical records (conditions, medications, immunisations, vital signs
from a provider) and ECG entries in `export.xml` are counted in the skip tally
but not stored. ECG recordings come in through the export's
`electrocardiograms` folder instead: each CSV file there is stored as a
recording when you upload the ZIP. Workout routes are attached when you keep
them.

### Lab results from Health Records

If you connected a hospital or lab to the Health app (Health Records), the
export carries its lab results, and the import puts them in Labs. Each result
is matched to the marker you already have for it, by name, by an alias, or by
its LOINC code, so "Cholesterol in LDL by calculation" lands on your "LDL"
marker. A result in another unit is converted into the marker's unit, with the
printed value kept beside it. Panels such as a full blood count keep their
name. Preliminary and withdrawn results are left out. The import result shows
a line with how many lab results were found, imported, already in your record,
skipped and failed. A failed lab result never discards anything else from the
archive.

### Values HealthLog wrote itself are left out

The iPhone app writes values into Apple Health: readings you enter in the app,
and readings from other sources it mirrors there. An Apple Health export
carries those back. The import recognises them, by the marker the
app stamps on every sample it writes or, for samples from older app builds, by
their HealthLog id, and leaves them out **when this account already holds
them**. Cycle samples count too: they are left out when the account has a
cycle entry on that day. The result says how many: "3 records that HealthLog
wrote into Apple Health itself were left out, because they are already in your
data."

A marked sample whose reading this account does not hold is imported as usual.
So when you move to a new server without a backup, importing an Apple Health
export still brings those values back. The import takes about as long as
before.

### Cumulative types collapse to one row per day

Cumulative quantity types — steps, active energy, distance, flights — collapse
into **one row per user-local day** to match the iOS daily-aggregation
convention. The external ID format is `stats:<HKType>:<YYYY-MM-DD>` (e.g.
`stats:HKQuantityTypeIdentifierStepCount:2026-05-15`). The day boundary respects
your [timezone preference](/explanation/timezone-model/), defaulting to
`Europe/Berlin` when none is set.

### Sport types and workout effort

These come in as types of their own, through the export and through the
iPhone app:

| Apple Health type | HealthLog type | Unit | Day value |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `DistanceCycling` | `CYCLING_DISTANCE` | m | sum |
| `DistanceRowing` | `ROWING_DISTANCE` | m | sum |
| `RowingSpeed` | `ROWING_SPEED` | m/s | mean |
| `RunningSpeed` | `RUNNING_SPEED` | m/s | mean |
| `RunningPower` | `RUNNING_POWER` | W | mean |
| `RunningGroundContactTime` | `RUNNING_GROUND_CONTACT_TIME` | ms | mean |
| `RunningVerticalOscillation` | `RUNNING_VERTICAL_OSCILLATION` | cm | mean |
| `RunningStrideLength` | `RUNNING_STRIDE_LENGTH` | m | mean |
| `WorkoutEffortScore` | `WORKOUT_EFFORT_SCORE` | 0 to 10 | mean |
| `EstimatedWorkoutEffortScore` | `ESTIMATED_WORKOUT_EFFORT_SCORE` | 0 to 10 | mean |
| `PhysicalEffort` | `PHYSICAL_EFFORT` | kcal per hour per kg | mean |

The two distances are summed per day like walking and running distance, so an
export with hundreds of thousands of cycling records lands as day totals. The
others are readings taken during a workout, so their day value is the mean. A
record in another unit, such as kilometres in an export from a metric account,
is converted on the way in, and a value that only makes sense in the wrong unit
(millimetres sent as centimetres, a running speed above 15 m/s) is refused.

The effort score you logged and the watch's estimate of it are stored apart,
so a day's mean never blends the two, and none of the three effort types is
added to active or resting energy. The eight cycling, rowing and running types
show on charts and to the MCP connector; they have no detail page of their own
and do not feed the Coach, the correlations or personal records. Cycling speed,
power and cadence, swimming, paddling, skiing and skating are not imported yet.

### Units come from the archive, not from the type

Apple stamps every quantity `<Record>` with the account's own display unit, so
the same archive reports walking distance in `km` for a metric account and `mi`
for an imperial one, body mass in `kg`, `lb` or `st`, and energy in `kcal` or
`kJ`. The importer converts each value out of the unit the record carries before
storing it, through the same table the workout import uses. A unit string it
cannot place is stored unconverted rather than rescaled on a guess.

Archives imported before v1.38.14 folded walking distance as if the number were
already metres, so a metric account reads a kilometre day as a couple of metres.
Re-uploading the same `export.zip` on a current release rewrites every
affected day; the fold UPSERTs the same rows, so nothing duplicates. For an
account that no longer has its archive, the
[walking-distance repair runbook](https://github.com/MBombeck/HealthLog/blob/main/docs/ops/apple-health-distance-repair.md)
covers `scripts/repair-apple-health-distance.ts`, which reports without writing
by default and must be run at most once per account: an account that was
re-imported must not then be repaired.

### Re-import idempotency

Two axes keep re-imports clean:

1. **File-level** — re-uploading the same `export.zip` (same byte SHA-256)
   short-circuits to the previous job before the parser starts.
2. **Record-level** — a re-exported archive keeps every record's external ID
   stable; the dedup key is the `(user, type, source, externalId)` tuple, so a
   matching record updates instead of inserting, and the same external ID from
   a different source or metric type stays a distinct row.

The job result reports `recordsInserted` and `recordsUpdated` separately, so a
monthly re-import shows fresh data merged without duplicates.

Lab results have their own rule. Each one keeps the identity its provider gave
it, so importing it again writes nothing, and a lab result you delete in
HealthLog stays deleted when the next archive brings it again. A reading you
already typed, scanned or imported from a CSV on the same day with the same
value is not written a second time. A result the provider corrected later
keeps the value it first arrived with; edit the reading if it changed.

The file-level check only returns an earlier job that was read the way the
current release reads an archive. An archive imported before HealthLog read
lab results is therefore read again when you upload it once more: the
measurements merge as usual and the lab results are added.

A day on which your phone or watch installed an update counts in full. An
archive imported before 1.39.3 may have counted only part of such a day's
steps. Uploading the same `export.zip` again does not correct it, because the
file-level check returns the earlier job; export again from the iPhone and
import the new archive.

### Why the reading count drops after an import

Right after a large import the account can show far more readings than it
will a few days later. That is the nightly clean-up (03:45 Europe/Berlin,
with a catch-up pass after the app starts), which folds high-frequency Apple
Health samples into fewer rows:

| Types | What is kept |
| --- | --- |
| Heart rate, heart-rate variability, blood oxygen | Every sample from the last 90 days. Older samples become one mean per hour of your local day. |
| Respiratory rate, walking speed, step length, walking asymmetry, double support, walking steadiness, walking heart-rate average, stair speeds, environmental and headphone audio exposure | One mean per day, once a day is more than 36 hours old. |
| Steps, active energy, distance, flights climbed, time in daylight, falls | One total per day, once a day is more than 36 hours old. |

An import of several years therefore loses most of its row count on the first
night: every heart-rate, HRV and blood-oxygen sample older than 90 days
collapses into at most 24 rows a day. What goes is the sample-level detail of
older days: long-range charts read daily values, and the
[intraday pulse view](/insights/intraday-pulse/) shows older days at hourly
resolution. The clean-up only touches readings that came from Apple Health
(import or the iOS app); manual entries and other sources are left as they are.

The replaced heart-rate, HRV, blood-oxygen and daily-mean samples stay in the
database marked as deleted for 75 days, so paired devices learn about the
change, and are removed after that. The database volume therefore shrinks
later than the reading count does. The weekly and off-host backups include
those marked rows until they are removed, so backups shrink on the same
schedule.

## Source-priority interaction

Apple Health outranks Withings for **cumulative** metrics (steps, energy,
distance, sleep, HRV, resting HR) because HealthKit folds watch and phone
sensors into one canonical stream. Withings outranks Apple Health for **point**
measurements where the Withings device is the primary sensor (weight, BP, SpO₂,
body fat). The `IMPORT` source tag — the lowest rank — is reserved for legacy
CSV/JSON imports. See [Source priority](/guides/source-priority/).

## Admin variant

`POST /api/admin/import-apple-health-export` is the cookie-only admin variant
(Bearer tokens never elevate). The multipart body adds a `userId` field naming
the target user. The `ImportJob` row carries `triggeredByAdminId`, so the status
endpoint admits both the target user and the triggering admin. Typical use:
migrating a household member's history when they cannot run the import
themselves.

## Failure modes

| Failure | What you see | Recovery |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Missing `export.xml` | `Archive is missing the apple_health_export/export.xml member` | Re-export from the iPhone; a partial AirDrop can lose the entry. |
| Encrypted archive | `Encrypted ZIP entries are not supported` | The file was repacked by a third-party tool — re-export from iOS directly. |
| Unsupported compression | `Unsupported ZIP compression method <n> for export.xml` | Same recovery — re-export from iOS, which always emits method 0 (stored) or 8 (deflate). |
| Worker down | `503 Background worker is not running` | Check the `app-worker` (or `all`-mode `app`) container and restart. |
| Rate-limited | `429 Too many import uploads` | The limit is three uploads per minute per user. The response says how long to wait in `Retry-After`, in whole seconds. |

Large exports, which the Health app writes in the ZIP64 format, are read in
full, ECGs, workout routes and lab results included. Earlier releases
skipped the ECGs and workout routes of such an archive and counted them as
failed; uploading the archive again on a current release brings them in.

## Garmin owners

On iOS, Garmin Connect writes to Apple Health automatically, so the import on
this page brings Garmin data in. The direct
[Garmin](/guides/connect-garmin/) integration reads more (Body Battery,
stress, Garmin's sleep score) with a key from your own Garmin account.

A workout that arrived without its heart-rate curve gets one from the
activity's FIT file; see [Import activity files (FIT)](/guides/import-fit/).
