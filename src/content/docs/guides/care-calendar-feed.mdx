HealthLog can publish your booked visits, the check-ups coming due and your
vaccination boosters as a calendar subscription, so they show up in the
calendar app you already use, next to everything else. The feed is built fresh
each time your calendar app asks for it; HealthLog stays the only place the
data is kept.

Your calendar provider stores what it fetches on its own servers, and turning
the subscription off later does not delete copies it already has. That is the
trade, and the reason the subscription is off until you turn it on.

## Turn it on

1. Open **Settings**, then **Health record**, and find **Care calendar
   subscription** under **Calendar**.
2. Read the note on the card, then turn the subscription on.
3. Confirm it is you with your password or a passkey.
4. Copy the link, or select **Open in calendar app**.

The link is shown only this once. If you lose it, turn the subscription off
and on again for a new one.

Only you can turn it on, and only for your own record. Someone you have shared
your record with, or the guardian of a managed profile, gets a feed of their
own dates, never yours. The subscription is managed on the web.

Adding the link to Google Calendar, Apple Calendar or Outlook works the same
way as for the cycle feed; see
[Add it to your calendar](/guides/cycle-calendar-feed/#add-it-to-your-calendar).

## What the feed shows

- **Booked visits**: every visit you booked for the future, at its time, as a
  one-hour event. A visit stores a start and no end, so the hour is a
  placeholder.
- **Check-ups coming due**: every check-up you set up under
  [Check-ups](/guides/checkups-and-practitioners/), as an all-day event on the
  day it comes due. This is the same due day the Check-ups page shows, so a
  skip, a snooze or a "done" moves it in your calendar too.
- **Vaccination boosters coming due**, the same way. They follow the
  [vaccinations module](/guides/vaccinations/): with it switched off, they are
  left out.

The feed reaches 30 days back, so a date does not vanish the moment it passes,
and about 13 months ahead. Each of the three kinds can be switched off on the
card.

Titles are discreet by default: every event is a dot (●), and the calendar is
called "HealthLog". Descriptive titles name the practitioner of a visit
("Appointment: Practice Lindenhof"), the label of a check-up ("Due: Skin
check") and the booster ("Booster due: Tetanus"). Events never carry a visit's
reason, outcome or body site, a check-up's location, a note, or anything else
from your record.

## Turn it off

**Turn off** ends the link. Also remove the subscribed calendar in your
calendar app: what it already fetched stays there until you do.

## For self-hosters

The link is the credential, so it sits in the URL path
(`/api/care/feed/hlf_…`). HealthLog keeps it out of its own logs, but your
reverse proxy's access log records every path by default. Skip or redact those
requests as described in
[Credentials in the URL path](/guides/reverse-proxy/#credentials-in-the-url-path).
If a forward-auth gateway guards the instance, let `GET /api/care/feed/*`
through, because calendar apps cannot sign in.

The feed is for calendar apps. A Bearer token, such as the iPhone app's, is
refused with `403` and `care.feed.cookie_only` when it tries to manage the
subscription.
