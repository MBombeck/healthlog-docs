HealthLog can publish your cycle forecast as a calendar subscription, so your
next periods show up in the calendar app you already use. HealthLog builds the
feed each time your calendar app asks for it and stays the only place your
cycle log is kept. Setting up cycle tracking itself is covered in
[Track your cycle](/guides/cycle-tracking/).

Your calendar provider stores what it fetches on its own servers, and turning
the subscription off later does not delete copies it already has. That is the
trade, and the reason the subscription is off until you turn it on.

## Turn it on

1. Open **Cycle**, then **Settings**, and find **Calendar subscription**.
2. Read the note on the card, then select **Turn on subscription**.
3. Confirm it is you with your password or a passkey.
4. Copy the link, or select **Open in calendar app**.

The link is shown only this once. If you lose it, rotate it to get a new one.
The card shows when a calendar app last fetched the feed, and which one.

Only you can turn it on for your own record. A guardian can turn it on for a
managed profile, such as a child's record. Someone you share your record with
cannot, at any access level, because the feed puts your dates in their
calendar provider's cloud. The subscription is managed on the web, and the
iOS app shows where to find it.

## Add it to your calendar

- **Google Calendar:** Other calendars, From URL, then paste the link. Google
  refreshes subscriptions on its own schedule, roughly every 12 to 24 hours.
- **Apple Calendar:** on a Mac, File, New Calendar Subscription. On iPhone,
  Settings, Calendar, Accounts, Add Account, Other, Add Subscribed Calendar.
  Apple lets you choose how often it refreshes.
- **Outlook:** Add calendar, Subscribe from web, then paste the link. Outlook
  refreshes every few hours.

## What the feed shows

- **The next period.** Once three cycles are logged, also the ones after it:
  three by default, up to six under **Cycles ahead**. Each event spans the
  whole window the period may start in, and the window gets wider the further
  ahead it is. The feed stops on its own where a date would be more guess than
  estimate, beyond about ten days either way or half a cycle.
- **Past cycles**, if you want them, up to twelve. None by default.
- **The fertile window** of the next cycle, offered only when your goal is
  trying to conceive. It is an estimate, not a contraceptive method.

Titles are discreet by default. Each event is a dot (●) and the calendar is
called "HealthLog". Under **Event titles** you can switch to descriptive
titles, which say "Period (estimate)" or "Period (logged)". Events never carry
symptoms, notes, flow, temperatures, test results or anything else from your
log.

When a period is later than its window, the feed shows no forecast until you
log it, instead of guessing a new date. With predictions switched off, the
feed only shows past cycles, if you chose to include them.

## Rotate or turn off

**Rotate link** gives you a new link, and calendars using the old one stop
updating. **Turn off** ends the link. Either way, also remove the old
subscribed calendar in your calendar app. What it has already fetched stays
there until you do.

Changing your password, or deleting your account, turns the subscription off
as well.

Switching cycle tracking off pauses the link instead: while it is off, the
feed answers like an unknown link, and your calendar keeps what it already
has. When you switch cycle tracking back on, the same link works again. If you
do not want that, turn the subscription off before you switch tracking off.

## For self-hosters

The link is the credential, so it sits in the URL path
(`/api/cycle/feed/hlf_…`). HealthLog keeps it out of its own logs, but your
reverse proxy's access log records every path by default. Skip or redact
those requests as described in
[Credentials in the URL path](/guides/reverse-proxy/#credentials-in-the-url-path).
If a forward-auth gateway guards the instance, let `GET /api/cycle/feed/*`
through, because calendar apps cannot sign in.

An unknown, rotated or revoked link gets a plain 404, the same answer for
every case. The feed sends an `ETag`, so a calendar app that asks again
without changes gets a short 304.
