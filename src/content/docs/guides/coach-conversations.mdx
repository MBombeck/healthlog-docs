Since v1.39.4 a Coach answer shows more than its text. You see which parts of
your record the Coach read while it works, the values it read as a table or a
chart, how a figure was worked out, and up to three suggestions for what to ask
next. When a question could mean several things, the Coach asks back with a
few choices instead of guessing.

Since v1.41.0 the Coach can think before it answers, with a model that
supports it. A single line above the answer says what it is doing while it
works, comparisons of two periods or two metrics share one chart, a question
that could mean two things gets suggested replies, and the Coach remembers
what you tell it, with an undo right where it says so. See
[Thinking depth](#thinking-depth), [The status line](#the-status-line) and
[What the Coach remembers](#what-the-coach-remembers).

All of this is built by the server from what the Coach actually read. The AI
model writes the answer text; it never writes a step label, a table, a chart,
the method line or the wording of a suggestion.

:::note[Which setups show it]
The sources list, tables, charts, method line and suggestions come from the
Coach reading your record piece by piece, which needs a provider that can call
tools: OpenAI, Anthropic, ChatGPT (Codex) and the OpenAI-compatible gateway
entry can. The [**Local** provider](/guides/ai-providers/#local-endpoints)
cannot, and neither can a provider chain that includes it. With such a chain
the Coach reads a summary of your whole record at once, as before: the sources list shows one line, **Reading
your record summary**, and there are no tables, charts or suggestions.

Since v1.41.0 the Coach still thinks on such a chain when the local model can
reason, and it still knows what it remembered about you. Saving something new
to memory during an answer, suggesting a plan and comparison charts need a
provider that can call tools.

An iPhone app from before v1.39.4 shows the answer as before, without these
parts.
:::

## The Coach page

Since v1.40.2 the Coach page has the chat in the middle and your conversations
in a panel on the right.

- **The conversations panel.** Conversations are grouped as Today, Yesterday,
  This week and Earlier, newest first. The toggle at the
  right of the top bar (**Show conversations** / **Hide conversations**) folds
  the panel away, and the page remembers your choice on that device. On a
  narrower window or a phone the panel opens as a sheet from the right and
  closes again when you pick a conversation.
- **Rename and delete.** Each row has a menu with **Rename** and **Delete**. A
  deleted conversation can be brought back for a few seconds with **Undo** on
  the **Conversation deleted** notice. Since v1.42.0 the delete is sent at the
  latest when you leave the page, so a reload or a closed tab inside that
  window no longer brings the conversation back.
- **New chat.** The round button at the bottom of the panel starts a new
  conversation.
- **Quick settings.** The gear in the panel header opens the Coach's settings
  in place: a popover on a wide screen, a sheet on a phone. See
  [Quick settings](#quick-settings).

### Under each answer

One row sits under every answer: copy, **Read aloud**, **Try again** and the
time on the left, the tokens and the model the answer used on the right. On a
computer the row appears when you point at the message; on a touch screen it is
always there. The copy button needs the browser's clipboard, so a server
reached over plain HTTP shows none.

Under your own message the row holds copy, the time and **Remember**, a
bookmark icon. It saves what you said to your self-context, so the Coach knows
it in later conversations; the icon confirms in place.

The thumbs up and down and the **What I'm looking at** box are gone from the
web. What the Coach read is in the list above the answer (see
[What the Coach looked at](#what-the-coach-looked-at)), which now also holds the
tables the answer did not point at and the line on how a figure was worked out.
Follow-up suggestions sit right under the answer, in the size of its text. When
you scroll up in a long conversation, a round button (**Jump to the latest
message**) brings you back down.

### Quick settings

The gear opens these:

- **Model.** **Answers with** picks which of your providers answers. Only
  providers that can actually answer are offered, ones with a working key or
  sign-in. When the server's operator provides the connection, the panel says
  so; once one of your own providers is set up, it answers first. The model
  appears where it is yours to set; otherwise the panel says the model is set
  in AI settings.
- **Thinking depth.** How much the Coach thinks before it answers. A change is
  saved at once. See [Thinking depth](#thinking-depth).
- **What I can see.** The data areas the Coach may read, each a switch, and how
  far back it may look (see below). A change is saved at once.
- **Memory.** A link, **Memory** with the number of things the Coach keeps,
  opens the list under Settings, Coach. See
  [What the Coach remembers](#what-the-coach-remembers).
- **All AI settings.** Keys, sign-ins and the fallback order stay in
  **Settings → AI**.

**Settings → Coach** sums this up in one line, for example "The Coach sees 8 of
10 data areas and looks back 90 days", with a link, **Open Coach settings**,
that opens the Coach page with these settings open (`/coach?settings=data`).

### How far back the Coach looks

Under **How far back the Coach can look** you choose **7 days**, **30 days**,
**90 days**, **12 months** or **All**. **All** is the default.

Since v1.40.2 this is a real limit. Before, it only set where the Coach started
reading, and a tool could still reach further. Now it holds for everything the
Coach reads in any answer: the summary of your record it starts from, every
reading tool, and the overviews such as correlations, lab results and glucose.
An overview that needs more history than the limit allows is shortened or left
out rather than computed over a shorter window that would disagree with its
page.

The line under the choice says what that means:

- With a limit: "The Coach reads nothing older than 90 days, in any answer."
- With **All**: everything you recorded, the last 12 months in daily detail,
  older readings as monthly values up to 10 years back, and lab results from
  the last 12 months.

Some things are not history and stay visible under any limit: your profile
(age, sex, height), your current medications, an illness that is still open,
upcoming visits, and what you told the Coach (remembered facts, plans,
reminders). Answers the Coach gave earlier, in older conversations, may still
quote older values.

When you ask about something older than the limit, the Coach does not say the
data is missing. It says the readings lie beyond the lookback and that you can
widen it in the Coach settings, and it names no figure from them: no count, no
date, no value.

Connected tools using [MCP](/guides/mcp-server/) are not affected. They read
with their own time windows; the lookback is a Coach setting.

## Thinking depth

With a model that can reason, the Coach thinks before it answers. **Thinking
depth**, in the quick settings and under Settings, Coach, sets how much:
**Off**, **Low**, **Medium** or **High**. **Medium** is the default. Deeper
thinking gives more careful answers, takes a little longer and uses more
tokens.

Some models cannot switch thinking off. With them the lowest option reads
**Minimal**, and the model thinks as little as it can. That is the case for
OpenAI's GPT-5 models before GPT-5.1 and its o-series, and for Claude models
from generation 5 on. A model that cannot reason at all, such as `gpt-4o`,
simply answers, and the setting changes nothing.

The server's operator can switch thinking off for everyone or set the highest
depth anyone may pick. A level above that limit stays in the list but cannot be
chosen and says **(limited by the admin)**; a choice you saved earlier then runs
at the limit. With thinking switched off, the field is locked and says so. When
the operator pays for the model (the server's shared key or shared ChatGPT
connection), the Coach thinks at **Medium** at most.

The **Reasoning** choice of a Local or OpenAI-compatible provider in AI
settings still applies to everything else that provider does. In the Coach,
thinking depth applies. Which providers reason and how is on
[AI providers](/guides/ai-providers/#reasoning).

### How long the Coach keeps looking

Until v1.41.0 the Coach read your record in at most three rounds per answer.
Now a question gets a budget of time and tokens instead, so a "why" question
can look at several series, compare them and come back with an answer:

| Who pays for the model | Tokens per answer | Time | Rounds |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| You (your own key, your ChatGPT sign-in, your local model) | 120,000 | 150 s | 12 |
| The server's operator | 40,000 | 90 s | 6 |

Thinking counts toward the tokens. Input a provider serves from its cache
counts at a tenth of its size, since later rounds resend almost the whole
conversation. The Coach also stops when another lookup brings nothing new.
Room for the answer is held back from the start, so a question never ends
without one. Every round is reserved against your daily AI budget before it
runs and settled afterwards, and at most two answers run at the same time per
person.

### Shorter answers

The first sentence of an answer answers the question. Most answers stay
between 40 and 120 words and end with at most one next step.

## The status line

While the Coach works, one row at the top of the answer shows a brain icon
and what it is doing right now, with a soft moving highlight and animated
dots: **Thinking…**, **Fetching blood pressure, last 90 days…**,
**Summarising 214 readings…**, **Writing the answer…**. Since v1.41.1 there
is no running seconds count. With a provider that sends summaries of its
reasoning, a short title from that reasoning takes the place of
**Thinking…**. When the answer is ready, the row reads **Thought process**
with the number of steps, and it closes again when a turn finishes.

When the Coach had to stop early, the line says why: **Budget reached,
answering with what it has**, **Time limit reached, answering with what it
has**, **Many steps, wrapping up** or **No new data, wrapping up**.

Nothing opens by itself. Tap the row to see the steps on a thin line, each
with its own icon and the reasoning summary under its title. Below the steps come the line on
[how a figure was worked out](#how-a-figure-was-worked-out) and the tables the
answer read without pointing at them. Once open, the trail stays open until
you close it and keeps growing while the Coach works.

Reasoning text is model text, so it is checked like an answer before you see
it. A title or summary that names a dose change, a diagnosis or a figure the
Coach did not read is dropped, and the step keeps its plain label. Titles are
cut at 80 characters and summaries at 400. The text is stored encrypted with
the answer and only loaded when you open the trail. The labels themselves
("Fetching", "Summarising" and so on) are written by the server, never by the
model. A screen reader hears the line through a polite status region, at most
once every one and a half seconds, and the summary once at the end.

Answers saved before v1.41.0 keep the sources list described below.

### Tables while the Coach works

Since v1.41.1 tables appear only with the answer: the tables the answer
points at become its charts, and the rest sit under **Data used** in the
trail. Before that, a short line per table showed under the status line while
the Coach worked.

## What the Coach looked at

Before v1.41.0, while the Coach worked, a line above its answer names the source it is reading,
for example **Checking: Blood pressure, last 90 days…**. Once the answer is
complete, the line reads **Looked at 3 sources**. Tap it to open the list: one
row per source, with the time window and how many readings it found, or why it
found none, such as **no readings**, **no readings in this window**,
**switched off** for a module that is off, or **couldn't be read**. Several
reads of the same source, such as this month and the month before, count as
one source.

The list shows only what the Coach read, never a value. A row names a part of
your record, a window and a count. The name of a lab test, which comes from
your own records, never appears there.

## Tables of your values

When the Coach reads a stretch of days, weeks or months, or a set of workouts,
sleep nights, lab results or medication adherence, it keeps what it read as a
table. The tables the answer relies on appear under the answer. Tables it read
without pointing at them sit under **Data used** in the opened sources list
above the answer.

- **The latest rows in view.** A table over time shows its latest 12 periods,
  still in date order, and any other table its first 12 rows. **Show all**
  opens every row in a scrolling area whose header stays in place.
- **Absence stays visible.** A day, week or month without a reading shows as
  `—`, never as zero, and a screen reader hears "no reading".
- **Weeks and months mean one thing per metric.** A total such as steps,
  active energy, distance or daylight is the sum of its days. A level such as
  blood pressure, weight or pulse is the average of its days. The numbers are
  the ones the app's own charts show for that period.
- **The default grain follows the window.** Up to 90 days are listed by day,
  the last 12 months by week, and all time by month, unless you ask for another
  grain. No table reaches past [the lookback you set](#how-far-back-the-coach-looks).
- **From an earlier answer.** When the Coach shows a table again that an
  earlier answer in the same conversation read, the table says **From an
  earlier answer** with that answer's date. It is the stored table with the
  values it had then, not a new read. Ask for fresh figures to have the Coach
  read again.

A table keeps the values the answer was given with. A reading you edit or sync
later does not change a table an earlier answer showed.

### Copying a table

The copy button beside a table's title offers two forms:

- **Copy for spreadsheet** puts the table on the clipboard as tab-separated
  values and as an HTML table, so a spreadsheet gets a grid and a document gets
  a table.
- **Copy as text** gives aligned plain text, for a message or a note.

Both copy every row, not only the ones on screen, with the column headings and
their units. A cell that a spreadsheet would read as a formula (one starting
with `=`, `+`, `-`, `@` or `|`) gets a leading apostrophe so it stays text; a
plain negative number is left alone. The button needs the browser's clipboard,
which browsers offer only over HTTPS or on `localhost`, so a server reached
over plain HTTP shows no copy button.

## Charts

The server picks a chart for each table from its shape and the metric. The
model has no say in it.

| Table | Chart |
| --- | --- |
| A level metric over time (blood pressure, weight, pulse, sleep, mood, …) | A line. Blood pressure draws systolic and diastolic together |
| A total over time (steps, active energy, distance, flights, daylight, …) | Vertical bars, one per day, week or month |
| Counts per category (workouts by sport) | Bars, horizontal once there are more than six categories. The eight largest are drawn and the rest are grouped as **Other** |
| How often each range of values came up | A histogram |
| A comparison of two periods of one metric | Both periods overlaid in one chart, labelled for example "Sep vs Aug" |
| A comparison of two metrics over the same window | Both series in one chart, on two axes when their units differ |
| Lab results, or a table with a single row | No chart, the table only |

A chart never mixes units on one axis: a table with values in different units
draws the first value column, plus a second only when it shares the unit. A
comparison of two metrics is the exception and gets a second axis.

### Comparisons

Ask how this month compares with the last one, or whether your sleep moves
with your resting heart rate, and since v1.41.0 the Coach reads both series
in one step and draws them in one chart. Two periods of one metric are the
current window against the one before, or against the same window a year
earlier; the server works out the change between them, and the model gets
both summaries and that change. Two metrics share the same window. Both sides
are read like any other table, so a switched-off module, an excluded metric
or [the lookback you set](#how-far-back-the-coach-looks) holds for each of
them.

A table with a chart has a **Chart** / **Table** toggle. A table under the
answer opens as the chart unless you asked for the table, a table under
**Data used** opens as the table, and the toggle does not change what is
stored. The chart has a text name for screen readers that points to the table
view for every value.

**Histograms.** When a conversation already holds a table of days, ask how
often your values fell into each range ("how often was my blood pressure in
each range?") and the Coach shows that table as a histogram: how many days
fell into each range. The ranges are 5 mmHg wide for blood pressure, 5 bpm for
pulse, 0.5 kg for weight, 30 minutes for sleep, 10 mg/dL for glucose and 1,000
for steps; other metrics get a width worked out from the spread of the values.
There are at most 20 ranges. A histogram
needs at least 10 values. With fewer, the table keeps its usual chart rather
than drawing a histogram of noise.

## How a figure was worked out

At the end of the opened sources list, the line **How this was worked out** lists each
source the answer rests on with its window, the number of readings and how
they were combined, for example "Blood pressure, last 90 days: 142 readings,
weekly averages · Sleep, last 30 days: no readings in this window". A source
the Coach found nothing for says so, and a table taken from an earlier answer
is marked **from an earlier answer**. The line is written by the server from
what was read. It carries counts, never a health value.

**Asking "Is that right?"** When you question a figure in a conversation that
holds a table ("is that right?", "that seems high"), the Coach is instructed
to name the source and window it used, read them again, and compare. If the
second read differs, it corrects the answer and says what changed; if it
matches, it says so. It is told never to adjust a figure to fit what you
expected.

## Follow-up suggestions

Under the latest answer, up to three buttons suggest what to ask next. Each
appears only when what it asks for is grounded in what the Coach just read:

| Suggestion | Offered when |
| --- | --- |
| **Show as a chart** / **Show as a table** | A table of this answer has a chart and is shown in the other view |
| **Compare with the period before** | A table over time covers the current period |
| **Compare with a year earlier** | The same, over at most 90 days, and your readings of that metric go back more than a year |
| **Instead:** *choice* | The answer assumed something instead of asking (see [When the Coach asks back](#when-the-coach-asks-back)) |
| **Look further back** | Your record holds readings older than the table's window |
| **See also:** *metric* | The Coach found a link between a metric it read and another metric you record |
| **Keep looking** | The Coach had to stop before it finished (see below) |

The comparison and wider-window suggestions are offered for metrics read by
day, week or month; glucose, medication adherence and workouts have their own
reads and get none of them. The model may say which of the allowed suggestions
fit its answer best, which only changes their order. It cannot add one, and it
never words one.

**Show as a chart** and **Show as a table** are answered from the stored table
without asking the model at all. The reply says "Here are the same figures from
my last answer, unchanged." The others start a normal Coach turn.

Suggestions sit under the latest answer only, disappear while the Coach is
answering, and do not appear beside a clarifying question. To turn them off,
switch off **Suggest follow-up questions** under **Settings → Coach** in
**Coach settings**, then **Save**. That also turns off **Keep looking**.

## Keep looking

When the Coach had to stop for its budget, its time or its number of rounds
(see [How long the Coach keeps looking](#how-long-the-coach-keeps-looking)),
it answers with what it has and offers **Keep looking**. Stopping because
nothing new turned up offers no such button. Tapping it continues the same question: the Coach is told what
it already read, so it can show those tables again instead of reading them a
second time, and goes on from there. An answer can be continued once; the
continued answer offers no further **Keep looking**.

## When the Coach asks back

If the answer really depends on something the conversation does not settle,
such as "how is my pulse?" when you record pulse, resting heart rate and
walking heart rate, the Coach asks one short question instead of answering.
Since v1.41.0 the choices appear as suggested replies right under the
question, in the conversation, rather than in a card above the message box.
Tap one, or type your own answer.

- The question can be about which metric, which time window, what to compare
  with (the period before, a year earlier, or your usual 90 days), which of
  your goals or active plans, from which starting point (a medication start,
  an illness, a cycle phase), or something only you can tell, which has no
  choices and is answered by typing.
- The choices come from your record, written by the server: a metric is only
  offered when you record it, a goal only when you have it. There are at most
  four. If fewer than two remain, the question is dropped and the Coach
  answers.
- The question names what the Coach will assume if you do not answer, and
  that assumption is the first choice.
- The Coach asks at most once in six turns of a conversation and three times a
  day. It is instructed never to ask about doses, medication changes or
  diagnoses, and a question that trips the reply screening loses its choices.

When the Coach may not ask, because of those limits or because the question
did not hold up, it goes ahead on the likelier reading and says which one in a
line under the answer, **Assumed:** *value*, with **Change**, which offers the
other readings. Each also appears among the follow-up suggestions as
**Instead:** *the other choice*, and tapping it asks the same question again on
that reading. An answer carries at most two such
assumptions.

## What the Coach remembers

Since v1.41.0 the Coach remembers what you tell it about your preferences,
your goals and your circumstances, and uses it in later conversations.

When it keeps something, a note under the answer says so, **Remembered:**
*what it kept*, with **Undo** right beside it. Anything about your health (a
condition, a limitation, a medication) it only keeps after you agree: the note
then asks, with **Yes, remember it** and **No**. What it keeps has to come
from your own message in that turn, words and figures alike, so a document you
discussed, a quote or the Coach's own idea cannot end up in memory. There is at
most one note per answer, and nothing is kept when the answer itself is
blocked or fails.

The **Remember** button under one of your own messages saves that message as
it is. The tap is your agreement, so it works for a health message too. If the
Coach already knows it, it says so.

Every question starts with what the Coach knows about you: up to 8 remembered
things and up to 6 active plans with how they are going, plus reminders that
are due soon, in about 1,500 characters at most. This is only sent when the
question itself may go to your provider, and things about a medication stay
out while the Medications module is off. Once a conversation has been quiet
for 30 minutes, a background pass may also note what it learned from it. A
health fact that pass finds is offered to you once, in a later answer, and kept
only if you say yes; unanswered, it lapses after 14 days.

### The memory list

**What the Coach remembers** under Settings, Coach lists everything, by
category, each with where it came from: **from you** for what you asked it to
keep or confirmed, **from the Coach** for what it kept or worked out itself.
**Edit** changes the wording (3 to 160 characters); an edited health entry
counts as confirmed by you. **Forget** removes one entry, **Forget everything**
all of them. The list stays readable and editable while the Coach is off. The
**Memory** link in the Coach's quick settings opens it.

### Plans

When you name a goal or agree to a suggestion, the Coach can propose a small
plan: one if-then intention tied to one metric, with an optional target and a
look-back after 7 to 56 days. It appears under the answer with **Take on this
plan** and **Not now**. Only your tap starts it. The plan's text goes through
the same check as an answer, so a plan that would change a dose is never
proposed. There is at most one proposal per answer and at most three open at a
time, and one you leave unanswered lapses after 14 days. The daily briefing
mentions how up to two active plans are going, with figures the server worked
out from your readings. See [Coach plans](/guides/coach-plans/).

## What the model sees and what you see

A table's values are health data, so they are handled as such:

- **Stored encrypted.** The tables are kept with the answer, encrypted with
  AES-256-GCM like the conversation itself. Only a description (which source,
  window and grain, and how many rows) is stored readable, never a value.
  Deleting the conversation deletes its tables. See
  [Encryption at rest](/explanation/encryption-at-rest/).
- **The model reads a summary, you see the table.** For a table of days, weeks
  or months, the model gets the number of readings, the average, lowest and
  highest value, the first and last period and at most 60 values, capped at
  about 1,500 tokens. You see every row. The figures in the answer are checked
  against what the model was given.
- **Earlier tables stay on the server.** In a later turn the model sees only a
  short description of the tables earlier answers read (source, window, row
  count), never their values. To use one again, it asks the server to show the
  stored table.
- **Excluded metrics never reach the model.** A metric you excluded under
  **Exclude metrics** in Coach settings, or whose module is switched off, is
  not read on any turn. An earlier table of that metric is no longer named to
  the model and cannot be shown again.
- **Switched-off modules hide their tables.** If you later switch off the
  module a stored table belongs to, the table is not shown ("This table is
  hidden because its module is switched off") and is not even decrypted.
  Switch the module back on and it returns.

- **Reasoning state stays with the provider.** Some providers hand back a
  signed or encrypted form of their reasoning that must go with the next round
  of the same answer. HealthLog passes it on within that answer and never
  stores it. What you can read in the trail is the screened summary, stored
  encrypted.

Your stored tables stay readable while the Coach is unavailable, like the
conversations they belong to.

## Cost

A table of days, weeks or months adds its summary, at most about 1,500
tokens, to what the model reads.
**Show as a chart** and **Show as a table** cost nothing: no model call, no
share of your AI budget. The other suggestions and **Keep looking** are normal
turns and cost what any question costs. A clarifying question ends the turn
early, so it costs less than an answer. Thinking adds tokens on every round;
**Low** adds the least and **High** the most, which is the reason for the
[budget per answer](#how-long-the-coach-keeps-looking). Every message, a tapped suggestion
included, counts toward the limit of 20 Coach messages a minute.

## Limits

| What | Limit |
| --- | --- |
| Tables per answer | 8 |
| Rows per table | 400, the latest kept; the table then says "Showing the latest 400 of *n* rows" |
| Stored tables per answer | 128 KB before encryption; tables past it are dropped from the end, before the answer is shown |
| Rows shown before **Show all** | 12 |
| Lookups per answer | 48 |
| Steps in the status line's trail | 99 |
| Rounds per answer | 12 on your own provider, 6 on the operator's, then **Keep looking** |
| Clarifying questions | 1 in 6 turns of a conversation, 3 a day |
| Remembered things a question starts with | 8, plus 6 active plans |
| Open plan proposals | 3; an unanswered one lapses after 14 days |
| Follow-up suggestions | 3, under the latest answer only |
| Choices on a clarifying question | 4 |
| Earlier tables the model is told about | 3 from each of the last 5 answers that have tables |
| Histogram | At least 10 values, at most 20 ranges |

## For API clients

The stream, its frames (`step`, `result`, `followUps`, `clarification`, and
since v1.41.0 `activity`, `memoryNote`, `planProposal`, interim `result` frames
and `done.stop`) and the request fields for tapping a suggestion (`followUp`),
answering a question (`clarification`) or answering a memory note or plan
(`memoryDecision`, `planDecision`) are described in the
[OpenAPI document](https://github.com/MBombeck/HealthLog/blob/main/docs/api/openapi.yaml).
A stored answer carries only the tables' descriptions; the values come from
`GET /api/insights/chat/{id}/messages/{messageId}/results`, and the text of
the trail from `GET /api/insights/chat/{id}/messages/{messageId}/trail`. See
[API endpoints](/reference/api-endpoints/#insights--coach).
