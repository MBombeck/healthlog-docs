HealthLog's medication tracking is built on a single recurrence engine and a
**dose ledger**: every expected dose becomes a slot with an explicit status —
taken on time, taken late, skipped, or missed. The
[compliance rate](/explanation/medication-compliance/) reads from the same
ledger, so the percentage can never disagree with the timeline beneath it.

## Create a medication

Open `/medications` and start the wizard. You set the name, an optional dosage
and unit, a route of administration (oral / injection / other — injection adds a
site picker), an optional supply count for the runway estimate, and a schedule.

The detail page that follows is a tabbed layout: **Übersicht** (overview),
**Wirkung** (effect on a metric), **Zeitplan** (schedule, which also carries
the reminder card), **Bestand** (supply), **Verlauf** (history), **API** (the
per-medication ingest token), and **Erweitert** (advanced). Injectables get an
extra **Injektion** tab. Reminders live with the schedule they gate; charts
load on demand.

## Pick a cadence

The schedule discriminator (`MedicationScheduleType`) has three top-level
shapes, and within `SCHEDULED` the recurrence engine supports several cadences:

| Cadence | How to express it | Compliance denominator |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Daily** | `FREQ=DAILY`, one or more dose times | Every day counts |
| **Weekday-only** | `FREQ=WEEKLY;BYDAY=MO,WE,FR` | Only the named weekdays |
| **Every N weeks** | `FREQ=WEEKLY;INTERVAL=2;BYDAY=WE` | The recurring week only |
| **Rolling interval** | An `intervalWeeks` value anchored to the start date | The recurring slots only |
| **One-time** | A single dose on a date | That one slot |
| **As-needed (PRN)** | `scheduleType = PRN` | **Excluded** — never projected, never reminded, never counted |
| **Cyclic** | `scheduleType = CYCLIC`, N weeks on / M weeks off | Only the on-weeks; the inner cadence applies within them |

The engine accepts RFC-5545 `RRULE` strings directly. A once-weekly GLP-1
injection, a weekday-only oral dose, and a cyclic on/off plan therefore each
score against their **real** cadence rather than a daily denominator — see
[why compliance is cadence-aware](/explanation/medication-compliance/).

:::note[GLP-1 example]
Once-weekly GLP-1 receptor agonists are a common reason to need weekly cadence.
The drug class works by mimicking the incretin hormone GLP-1 — glucose-dependent
insulin secretion, suppressed glucagon, slowed gastric emptying
([overview on Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GLP-1_receptor_agonist)).
HealthLog only tracks the dose schedule and intake; it gives no dosing advice.
:::

## Set a dose window

Each dose time can carry either a point time or an explicit range — for example
`07:00–09:00`. The default is ±1 hour. The window defines the **on-time band**:
an intake that lands inside it counts as on time rather than late. Edit the
window on the **Zeitplan** tab or in the wizard.

This matters because the ledger grades each slot against its window. A take a
few minutes outside the band reads as late, not missed; a weekly dose taken
inside its multi-day catch-up window reads as late, not missed.

## Log an intake

Mark a dose taken, skipped, or snoozed from the card, the detail page, or a
reminder action button. The write path attributes the intake to the slot whose
window it falls in. A take outside every window records as **ad-hoc** rather than
snapping to a far-off slot. Marking taken or skipped recomputes the history and
rate immediately.

Editing a dose's time re-attributes it and is guarded: the picker caps at now,
rejects timestamps before the medication existed, and warns when the new time
sits more than 48 hours from the scheduled slot — so a day/month typo cannot
silently corrupt the today view.

:::note[Somebody marking a dose in your record]
If you have given another account
[write or manage access](/guides/shared-access/) to your record, they can mark a
dose taken or skipped, one at a time or by clearing everything due at once, and
you are notified when they do. They cannot snooze it. Pushing back a reminder
you set for yourself is a change to your schedule rather than a note about your
day, so it stays with you. So does overturning a dose you have already decided:
re-sending the same decision works, because a second tap and a retry after no
signal both look like that, but turning your "taken" into a "skipped" is refused
at every level.
:::

## Attribute an off-schedule dose to its slot

When an intake lands off schedule, the **Verlauf** (history) row shows when the
dose had been due and offers to **attribute** the intake to that slot. The
attributed dose is visibly badged, always counted as **taken late** (never
washed to on-time), and reversible at any time.

This is the manual counterpart to the automatic window matching: use it when you
logged a dose generically and want it bound to a specific expected slot after the
fact.

## Reminders

Set reminder times and channels on the reminder card inside the **Zeitplan**
tab, under the times editor. The dispatcher fans
each reminder out to whichever channels the user enabled, with late/missed
escalation. See [Reminder channels](/guides/reminder-channels/) for the channel
cascade and the per-channel setup.

## Supply and side effects

The **Bestand** tab tracks a supply count and surfaces a runway ("X days left").
For injections, a site-rotation picker and structured side-effect tracking live
under the advanced sections.

A container in that tab can also carry the **manufacturer** and the **strength
printed on the carton** (v1.30.34). Both are optional and both are blank on
containers created before that release — a blister pack or a bottle has no use
for either. They matter for pens: the companion app's pen list uses the two as
its headline and subhead, so a container entered on the web could not be shown
there until the server held them.

## See a medication's effect on a metric

The **efficacy chart** plots how a metric moved since a medication started — for
example how blood pressure tracks against when you began taking a BP medication.
It draws:

- **A data-range axis.** The axis scales to the data, so a real trend no longer
  looks flat.
- **A "% since start" view.** Read the change as a percentage relative to where
  the metric sat when the medication began.
- **The full intake history.** The history tab reaches your complete intake
  record, not just the last 90 days.

The chart is descriptive: it shows what your own data did, and never claims the
medication caused the change. Interpreting an effect is a conversation for your
clinician.
