Apple delivers a push to the HealthLog app from the App Store only when it is
signed with the key of the Apple developer account that publishes the app. A
self-hosted server does not have that key. On its own it cannot reach the App
Store app: no quiet confirmation that a dose was recorded on another device, no
silent sync that tells the phone to refresh, and no ending a medication Live
Activity from the server.

The push relay closes that gap. It is a small service run by the HealthLog
project at `push.healthlog.dev`. Your server seals each notification for the
one device it is meant for, hands the sealed envelope to the relay, and the
relay passes it to Apple inside a fixed template. The relay holds the Apple
push key; it never holds a key that opens an envelope.

The relay is off until an admin of your server turns it on, and after that
each person decides for themselves in the iPhone app. Its source, protocol and
test vectors are public in the
[healthlog-push repository](https://github.com/MBombeck/healthlog-push)
(Apache-2.0; the specification and test vectors CC0).

:::note[Household use, not a medical service]
The hosted relay is for a server that a person runs privately for themselves
or their household. It is not a medical service: a notification can be late,
arrive twice or not at all, and the reminders the app schedules on the iPhone
stay the ones to rely on. Organisations (a practice, a care service, an
association) use their own Apple push key, run their own relay, or write to
`relay@healthlog.dev`.
:::

## What the relay sees, and what it does not

The relay sees delivery metadata (the device's push token while it forwards,
kept at rest only as a keyed hash; your server's instance id; the class,
lifetime, size and time of each push; the version of the person's consent),
never what a notification says. The full list, and how long each item is kept,
is the relay's own [privacy notice](https://push.healthlog.dev/privacy), which
is the authoritative text. With the separate Live Activity consent, the content
state of a Live Activity start or update passes the relay and Apple in the
clear; see [Live Activity over the relay](#live-activity-over-the-relay).

Every alert and every silent push carries an envelope sealed with
HPKE (RFC 9180, auth mode, X25519, HKDF-SHA256, ChaCha20-Poly1305) for that one
device. The envelope has a fixed size per class and its header names no key,
so envelopes do not tell devices or servers apart either.

Within a class the relay cannot tell one kind of push from another. Over the
relay every alert lives 15 minutes and every silent push an hour, whatever it
is, and the retry schedule is one per class too. What stays readable beside the
envelope, because the relay needs it to hand the push to Apple:

- the class (alert, silent, Live Activity), which also sets Apple's priority;
- the remaining lifetime, rounded down to the minute, which falls with each
  retry and says nothing finer than the class;
- the size, fixed per class, and the time;
- an opaque collapse id per device, the same for pushes that replace each other
  (a reminder and its follow-ups for one dose, a device's syncs), so the relay
  sees that two pushes belong together, never what they are about;
- for a Live Activity push, whether it starts, updates or ends it.

What timing can reveal is said plainly. If you record a dose on one device and
another device of yours gets the confirmation, the relay and Apple can see that
something happened at that moment, and a medication reminder that goes over the
relay shows them when a dose is due. Apple can link the push token to an Apple
account. That is true of every iOS push service. It is why a device that plans
its own reminders keeps them off the relay, and why turning the relay on is each
person's own choice.

Whoever holds the relay's Apple push key, the relay operator or anybody who
steals that key, can make a notification appear in the app's name on a device
whose token they have seen. They cannot read your data, and the app acts only
on a notification whose envelope it could open with a sender key it pinned
from your server.

## What goes over the relay

Only what your own server sends to your own devices. The operator of the hosted relay never sends anything through it: no announcements, no update notices, no messages of any kind.

- the medication reminder, to each device that gets its reminders from your
  server (see below);
- the quiet confirmation that a dose was recorded on another device, so the
  iPhone withdraws a reminder it already scheduled (see
  [Doses recorded somewhere else](/guides/track-medications/#doses-recorded-somewhere-else));
- the silent sync that tells another device to refresh;
- the end of a medication Live Activity, sent with a statement the device
  signed when the Activity started, so the relay never learns when an Activity
  starts;
- other server notifications, such as low stock or a seizure cluster;
- security alerts: a sign-in from a new device, a passkey added or removed, an
  access token revoked, a device signed out because its sign-in token was used
  twice. They break through a Focus;
- a silent wake when a server-side sync stored new readings (Withings, Garmin,
  Oura, WHOOP, Polar, Strava, Google Health, Nightscout, or a food tracker
  posting with a token), so the app, its widgets and the Watch are current
  without opening the app. It names only the kind of data that changed
  (measurements, sleep, workouts, nutrition), never a value;
- a silent wake asking the app to run its Apple Health upload when the server
  has heard nothing from it for six hours while the app is still in use.

The wakes are rationed, because Apple stops waking an app that gets too many of
them. The data wake goes out at most once every five minutes and three times an
hour per account, with changes in between merged into the next one. The Apple
Health wake goes to a device at most three times a day, at least four hours
apart, only while the app was opened in the last three days, and stops after two
weeks without any upload. No device gets more than four wakes an hour. Both
wakes go only to app versions that registered a push key.

Starting and updating a Live Activity is a separate choice, described
[below](#live-activity-over-the-relay).

Mood, measurement and cycle reminders do not go over the relay: the app plans
them itself. A medication reminder goes over the relay to exactly the devices
your server would remind over its own Apple push key: a device set to server
delivery, a device without its own setting on an account set to server
delivery, and a device that reminds itself but whose local plan has run out
(past the runway it reported, or three days without a request). A device that
reminds itself keeps the reminder, and the follow-ups for a dose still open, on
the iPhone.

Nothing about another person's record (guardian features) goes over the relay:
no reminder or other alert about a managed profile, and not the silent sync a
guardian's iPhone gets when a managed profile's dose changes. Over a server's
own Apple push key that sync still reaches the guardian; over the relay the app
refreshes a managed profile when it opens.

## Turn it on (admin)

1. After the update every admin sees a one-time notice on the admin start page
   that the relay exists, what it does and what it sees. **Got it** dismisses
   it for good.
2. In **Admin > Push and channels**, switch on **Offer the HealthLog push
   relay to this server's users**. The dialog has three boxes to tick: that you
   accept the relay terms, that you have read its privacy notice, and that you
   run this server privately for yourself or your household.
   If the relay asks for an invite code, enter it in the same dialog, under
   **Invite code (optional)**; see [Invite codes](#invite-codes).
3. Your server creates its instance key, stores it encrypted, and registers
   with the relay. The card then shows the relay's state (Connected,
   Disrupted, Not reachable or Off) and how many devices are bound.

When the relay's text changes in an update, as it did in 1.49.0 when
medication reminders began to go over the relay, the card asks every admin to
read the new text and confirm it once (**Read and confirm**). The relay keeps
running until then, and confirming changes nothing at the relay. The app gets
the new version of its own consent text at the same time, so it can show its
card again; your server never unbinds a device over an older version.

Turning the switch off deletes your instance at the relay, which removes every
device binding with it, and deletes the instance key on your server. The apps
notice on their next sync and go back to local reminders by themselves.

### Invite codes

A relay can accept new servers by invitation only, and the hosted relay may run
that way while it is new. Then a server that registers for the first time needs
a one-time invite code:

- **Where to get one.** Write to `relay@healthlog.dev` and ask for a code for
  your server. If you run your own relay, `healthlog-push admin invite` prints
  one.
- **What it looks like.** 24 characters (letters, digits, `-` and `_`), valid for
  7 days, for exactly one server.
- **Where to enter it.** In the dialog that turns the relay on, in **Invite code
  (optional)**. If you already turned the switch on without a code, the card
  shows **Enter invite code**; use it and the server registers again.

The code is sent with that one registration and never stored or logged on your
server. The relay spends it only when the registration succeeds, so a refused
attempt keeps it. If the relay refuses, the card says why:

- **This relay accepts new servers by invitation only. Enter an invite code.**
  No code was entered.
- **The relay did not accept this invite code.** The code is unknown, expired or
  already used. Ask for a new one.
- **The relay is not taking new servers right now.** Registration is paused at
  the relay; your server asks again on its own later.

After an invite refusal your server does not keep asking the relay; it waits
until you enter a code. A server that is already registered never needs a code
again, also not after an update or a relay restore.

### Check that it works

**Send test push** on the relay card sends one harmless sealed notification to
each of your own devices that use the relay. The card shows what the relay
answered, checked against the relay's pinned signing key, and whether the push
was accepted for delivery. Use it after turning the relay on, after an update,
or when someone says notifications stopped. If none of your own devices is
bound yet, the card says so: turn the relay on in the app on your iPhone first.

### The instance key

Your server signs every relay request with its own instance key. It replaces
that key every 30 days, and **Rotate relay key** does it at once (the relay
allows one rotation a day). The new key is kept before the request leaves, so a
lost answer cannot lock the server out, and the old one is kept for 7 days.

### Deleting an account

When an account is erased, by the person, by an admin, or by the purge after
the grace window, the server tells the relay to drop the binding of each of its
devices. The unbind is queued with the erasure, so a relay that is down delays
it without blocking the deletion; it is retried for 7 days. The app's own
"turn off" also revokes its binding directly at the relay.

Only the relay's signed answer confirms an unbind. While your server cannot
reach the relay it registered with (`PUSH_RELAY_MODE=off`, or a
`PUSH_RELAY_URL` changed after the relay was turned on), the unbinds wait and
the relay card says how many, and that the server is still registered there.
That relay keeps your server's device bindings until the server leaves it: set
the variable back and switch the relay off.

## Opt in on the iPhone

Each person sees a card in the HealthLog app once, after the admin turned the
relay on, and decides for themselves. Only **Turn on** creates the keys on the
iPhone and binds the device. The choice stays in the app's notification
settings and can be withdrawn there at any time, even while your server is
unreachable. "My data at the relay" in the same place shows what the relay
holds about the device, and turning the relay off erases it there. Up to five
devices per account can use the relay.

This needs the iPhone app 1.7 or later. An older build keeps working as before,
with its local reminders.

## Live Activity over the relay

Without it, the server can only end a medication Live Activity over the relay.
Starting it from the server, and updating it as the dose turns due, overdue or is
snoozed, is a separate choice, off by default, and needs two things:

1. The operator sets `PUSH_RELAY_LIVE_ACTIVITY=on` in the server's environment
   (on the compose whitelist; restart after the change). Leave it off until the
   1.7 app has been tested on your devices.
2. Each person turns on the second switch for Live Activities over the relay in
   the app, beside the relay switch. It is off by default, the device signs that
   consent with its binding key, and turning the relay off turns it off too. It
   can be switched off again at any time; the app then tells the relay directly.

The content a Live Activity shows cannot be sealed, because Apple's Live
Activities cannot decrypt anything. With the switch on, the relay and Apple can
read the content state of each start and update: the dose state (pending, due,
overdue, snoozed, taken or skipped), whether it is confirmed, the countdown and
the time format.
It never carries a medication name, a dose or any free text; the relay refuses
anything else. It follows the medication's own dose windows so the lock screen
says due when the app does, and the relay limits starts and updates per device
and day.

There is one Activity per dose. The server starts one at most once for a dose,
and not for a dose the app already shows in an Activity of its own. When the
app starts its own Activity for a dose the server already started one for, the
server ends its own, so the lock screen never shows the same dose twice. The
server also runs at most one Activity of its own per device: before it starts
one for the next dose, it ends the one still running. It sends updates only to
the Activities it started; an Activity the app started gets only the end. A server with its
own Apple push key is not affected by the variable.

## When the relay is down

A device that plans its own reminders keeps them. A device that gets its
reminders from your server gets none whose deadline passes during the outage.
Your server keeps each push with a deadline (an alert 15 minutes, a silent push
1 hour, a Live Activity end 4 hours, a Live Activity start or update 15 minutes)
and retries with backoff. A push past its deadline is dropped, never delivered
late. After three failed connections in a row the server stops trying item by
item and checks the relay's status every minute, later every five.

A relay outage never counts against a device and never switches a
notification channel off. Only Apple's "unregistered" answer for a token
deletes that device, and if more than a third of the relay devices get that
answer within an hour, the server holds further deletions for a day, because
that pattern points at a fault rather than a wave of uninstalls.

While the relay is down, or when pushes it accepted never reached a device, the
app shows that syncing with your other devices is disrupted, so nobody relies
on a confirmation that did not come.

## Privacy notice in short

The full texts are the relay's own
[privacy notice](https://push.healthlog.dev/privacy) and
[terms](https://push.healthlog.dev/terms); what the relay keeps, and for how
long, is stated there and nowhere else. In short:

- The relay is run by the maintainer of the HealthLog project, as an
  independent controller for the relay's metadata. Contact:
  `privacy@healthlog.dev` for data protection, `abuse@healthlog.dev` for abuse
  and appeals, `relay@healthlog.dev` for operation.
- Delivery rests on each person's consent, given on the app's card and
  withdrawable there at any time. Without it nothing goes over the relay and
  everything else works as before. "My data at the relay" in the app shows
  what the relay holds about the device.
- The relay is free, run on a best-effort basis with a target of 99.5 percent
  availability over 30 days and no service-level agreement, and a shutdown
  would be announced at least 90 days in advance.

## Security and hosting

The relay runs on its own virtual server, with nothing else on it and no health
records on it: it only ever sees sealed envelopes and the delivery metadata
above.

- **Two processes.** The relay and the component that holds the Apple signing
  key run in separate containers as unprivileged users, with a read-only root
  filesystem and all Linux capabilities dropped (the relay keeps only the right
  to bind its port). The signing component has no network access at all; the
  relay only ever gets a short-lived provider token from it.
- **Signed both ways.** Every request from a server is signed with that
  server's key, and every answer from the relay is signed with the relay's
  identity key, which your server pins. An answer without a valid signature is
  treated as if it never arrived, never as a refusal, a revocation or a
  deletion. A DNS or TLS takeover therefore turns into an outage, not a
  takeover.
- **Binding needs the device.** A device binding needs a statement signed by a
  key only the app holds, so a server that merely knows a device token cannot
  bind it.
- **Host.** Ubuntu LTS, hardened against the CIS benchmark (Level 1 Server,
  with selected Level 2 settings). Only port 443 is public, with TLS 1.3; there
  is no public SSH and no password or root login. Outbound traffic is limited to
  Apple's push service and the certificate authority, plus write-only endpoints
  for the host's own logs and metrics. The host holds no credentials for any
  other system. Security updates install daily. Only a daily, encrypted export
  of the registered servers and the block lists is backed up, to separate
  storage in Germany; device bindings, counters and keys are never backed up.
- **What is not guaranteed.** Anyone with full control of the host could read
  the relay's keys and the metadata passing through it at that moment, though
  not the content of notifications. Nobody can prove which code runs on a server
  they do not control; the relay reports its version and image digest at
  `https://push.healthlog.dev/version`, and the release images are signed.

## Configuration

All four variables are optional and on the compose whitelist.

| Variable | Default | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `PUSH_RELAY_MODE` | `allowed` | `off` hides the relay entirely, the admin notice included |
| `PUSH_RELAY_URL` | `https://push.healthlog.dev` | The relay origin; `https` only |
| `PUSH_RELAY_KEYS` | the hosted relay's keys | The relay's identity public keys, Ed25519, unpadded base64url, comma separated |
| `PUSH_RELAY_LIVE_ACTIVITY` | off | `on` lets Live Activity start and update go over the relay to devices whose owner gave the separate consent; see [Live Activity over the relay](#live-activity-over-the-relay) |

A relay without pinned keys cannot be used at all: the card says why and the
switch stays off.

A server with its own Apple push key (`APNS_*`, see
[Reminder channels](/guides/reminder-channels/#apple-push-apns)) does not need
the relay. It seals the same envelopes and sends them to Apple itself, so the
app sees one format on every path.

## Run your own relay

A relay you run yourself cannot serve the App Store app: Apple accepts pushes to
that app only with the HealthLog project's key. Your own relay works with an app
you build from source and sign under your own Apple developer account.

- One server and your own app build: use your own Apple push key (`APNS_*`).
  No relay needed.
- Several servers, or an organisation, with your own app build: run your own
  relay from the [healthlog-push repository](https://github.com/MBombeck/healthlog-push),
  set `PUSH_RELAY_URL` and `PUSH_RELAY_KEYS` on each server, and point your app
  build at it. The repository's self-hosting guide covers keys, the container
  layout and the firewall rules.
