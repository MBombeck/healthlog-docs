Most achievements wear their requirements on their sleeve. The dashboard tells you "log 50 weigh-ins" and you know exactly what to do. A small set of **hidden achievements** work the other way around — you don't see what triggers them until you trigger them. They're a tiny celebration buried in the app, nothing more.

## What you'll see before unlocking one

On the [Achievements](/features/achievements-hidden/) page, every hidden achievement renders as an opaque card with a single label:

> **Hidden achievement**
>
> Keep using HealthLog — this one reveals itself when you earn it.

The real title, the real description, the icon, the trigger metric, the target — none of it reaches the rendered HTML, the API response, or anywhere a curious user could `Cmd-F` to spoil the surprise. You see a placeholder; the placeholder reveals itself once you've actually earned the badge.

## How they reveal

The moment a hidden achievement unlocks, two things happen:

1. The placeholder card on the Achievements page swaps out for the real card — title, description, icon, unlock timestamp.
2. A toast notification fires with a longer Sparkles celebration and a localized headline along the lines of "you unlocked a hidden achievement!".

After the unlock, the hidden achievement behaves exactly like a regular one — it sits in your list with its real strings, it counts toward your total, and it shows up in the dashboard's Recent achievements card if you have that widget enabled.

## Categories that exist in HealthLog

The Achievements page groups badges into the following categories. Hidden ones share the same gameplay loop as the public ones; they're just initially redacted.

| Category | What's there |
|---|---|
| **Streaks** | Consecutive days of the same kind of activity — login streaks, compliance streaks, mood streaks, all-data entry streaks. |
| **Milestones** | Cumulative counters — total weigh-ins, total BP readings, total intakes. |
| **Consistency** | Reaching a coverage threshold inside a window — e.g. at least 25 distinct entry-days in a calendar month. |
| **Improvement** | Personal-baseline-vs-recent comparisons — your 7-day mean is better than the prior week's, judged against your own data only, not anyone else's. |
| **Discovery** | Engaging with parts of the app you might not otherwise notice. |
| **Hidden** | Easter-egg achievements that paint as placeholders until earned. |

:::note[Not the same as Today's milestones]
This gamification page — streaks, counters, hidden easter eggs — is distinct from
the **milestones** that surface on the [Today view](/insights/today/). Those are
durable *states reached* (a personal best, a return to your usual range, a
sustained stretch in range), framed deliberately as calm arrivals with nothing to
keep and nothing to break — not the streak-and-counter loop described here. The
"Milestones" category in the table above is a gamification counter and is a
different thing from the Today milestone card.
:::

## Why they're hidden

Two reasons.

The first is that they're little jokes. Bury a few delights in the app, let them surface when the moment is right. If the trigger is something you'd never optimise for — a particular calendar quirk, a particular time of day — telling you up front would just make you feel obliged to chase it. Better to let it land on its own.

The second is that the discoverability of all the other achievements depends on them not crowding the page. A **discovery filter** hides locked public badges until you have data for the metric they care about (so a streak badge for blood pressure stays out of the list until you log your first BP reading). Hidden achievements ignore that filter — they always render — but they render as opaque placeholders, so they don't reveal what they're for.

## Where the strings live

For self-hosters and the merely curious: the redaction is enforced server-side. The `/api/gamification/achievements` route strips the title, description, icon name, and trigger metric out of the response body for any achievement flagged `isHidden` and not yet unlocked. The string-keyed lookup that would resolve the localized title is short-circuited at the page boundary — a `hiddenLocked` entry makes the page render the placeholder card and never look up the real strings. The DOM never sees them.

The bundled English and German translations still ship with the strings (so unlocked clients can render them without a round-trip), which means a determined user can `Cmd-F` through `_next/static/chunks/*.js` and find them. That's a small known leak. For now the page-level redaction is enough — you'd have to actively dig for it.

## How many there are

Six at present. The number isn't fixed; new ones can land in any future release without breaking your existing unlocked set, and they'll all behave the same way: placeholder card → unlock event → real card.

If you've earned an unusually high number of them already, it might be worth keeping that to yourself. They are, after all, hidden.
