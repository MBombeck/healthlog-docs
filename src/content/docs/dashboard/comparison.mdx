A **comparison baseline** turns charts and tiles into a "this month vs. last month" view. You pick it per chart in the overlay cog, it is stored once as a dashboard preference, and every dashboard chart, dashboard tile, and insights metric page reads the same value. This page covers where the control is, what it shows, and what the numbers mean.

## Where the control lives

Each chart card carries an **overlay cog** — the same menu that holds the trend, trend-arrow, and target-range overlays (see [Dashboard Customization](/features/dashboard-customization/)). Under the *Comparison* label sit three buttons: *Off*, *vs. last month*, and *vs. last year*.

The choice is not per chart, though: it writes into the persisted dashboard-widgets preference, so setting it on one chart sets it everywhere. The dashboard tiles, every dashboard chart, and the insights metric pages all resolve the same stored baseline, and it survives reload and follows you across devices. Default is off; nothing changes until you pick a baseline.

## What the overlay actually shows

With a baseline picked, three things change.

### 1. The chart gets a dimmed prior-period line

The current period renders in full colour as the primary line. The prior period — last month or last year, depending on the baseline — renders as a **dimmed line beneath it**, lower opacity.

The two periods share the **same visible x-axis**. The prior-period series is **forward-shifted** internally: a reading from a year ago renders at the position of "today − 365d + offset", so each prior-period point sits directly under the current-period point it should be compared against. Same time of month, same day of week. The vertical distance between the two lines is the comparison — no eye-balling alignment.

### 2. The dashboard tiles pick up a delta callout

Tiles whose metric carries the comparison fields show a **Δ ±N** chip. The number is the difference between the current 30-day mean and the prior-period 30-day mean, in the metric's own units. Colour follows the same metric-aware sentiment as the 7-day trend chips (green for "improvement", orange for "concerning", muted for "neutral").

The two fields on the data summary (`avg30LastMonth`, `avg30LastYear`) feed the delta computation, so the chart and the tile read from the same source of truth.

### 3. The AI insights surface narrates the comparison

With a baseline on, the AI summary card narrates the comparison in plain language: "your average BP improved by 4 mmHg vs. last month". The prior-period mean is added to the insight prompt, so the model sees both periods and produces a sentence comparing them. The narration is generated **once per snapshot** with the rest of the insight, so it doesn't double the AI cost when you switch the baseline on and off.

## What "vs. last month" / "vs. last year" mean

- **vs. last month** — the comparison mean is the average over the **30 days before** your current 30-day window. So if you're looking at the last 30 days of weight readings, the prior-period mean is the 30 days before that. Not "the same calendar month last year"; "the equivalent-length window immediately before".
- **vs. last year** — the comparison mean is the average over the **30 days starting 365 days ago**. So the same time of year, the same 30-day window length, just one calendar year earlier.

Both modes use the same 30-day window length as the current period. That means the comparison stays apples-to-apples even if you don't have a full year of history yet — the older window simply has fewer readings, and the delta is computed from whatever's there.

## When the overlay can't render

- **No prior-period data on a chart.** When the chart discovers the selected prior period carries nothing to overlay, its caption states that the comparison is unavailable, and the cog's comparison buttons render visibly disabled — reduced opacity, with a tooltip saying why. *Off* stays enabled as the escape hatch, and the moment a prior-period reading lands (say, you backfill a paper diary), the overlay starts showing.
- **A missing mean on a tile.** The tile delta needs both a current and a prior 30-day mean; when either side is missing, the chip is suppressed rather than shown as a dash.

The current period gets its own empty-state handling — a 1- or 2-point chart still renders an explicit empty state — independent of the overlay.

## Performance

Enabling a comparison **widens the chart's data window** by the shift length — 30 days for last-month, 365 for last-year — so the chart runs one refetch for the wider window and then renders both periods from that single response. The tile deltas are cheaper: the prior-period means ride the summary fields already in the dashboard fetch, so the tiles need no extra request at all.

The AI insights narration adds the prior-period means to the prompt — that's a small token-budget hit per generation, not a separate request. The cached insight payload includes the narration, so switching the comparison off and back on doesn't trigger a regeneration.

## Reduced motion

The overlay line fade-in respects `prefers-reduced-motion` — for users who've turned reduced motion on, the prior-period line snaps to final state instantly instead of fading in.

## What's not in the comparison overlay

- The comparison is a two-mode split — **last month** and **last year**. There are no quarterly or arbitrary-window modes; the month/year split covers the common "am I trending up or down?" question without a date-range picker.
- The medication-compliance heatmap doesn't get a prior-period overlay; the heatmap shape doesn't translate well to overlay layering.
